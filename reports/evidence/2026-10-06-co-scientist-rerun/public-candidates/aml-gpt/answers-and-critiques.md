At verified clinically achievable unbound exposure, teriflunomide will inhibit DHODH and induce pyrimidine-depletion-mediated maturation with durable loss of leukemia propagation preferentially in salvage-low, oxidative, cytarabine-persistent AML, compared with paired untreated AML and salvage-high survivors, while relatively sparing normal hematopoietic progenitors. These selectivity and exposure claims are assumptions to test.

Supported premises and novelty boundary. Cytarabine survivors exhibited oxidative-metabolism features without consistent leukemia-stem-cell enrichment [S2]; they cannot be equated with the ROS-low, BCL-2-associated population in [S1]. Experimental DHODH inhibition promoted AML differentiation [S4]. Neither finding establishes teriflunomide efficacy or an oxidative-state–DHODH dependency. Venetoclax–azacitidine is an established metabolic comparator [S3]. Prior teriflunomide/leflunomide AML studies require verification before asserting originality. The distinct prediction is a residual-state/salvage-dependent response, not discovery of DHODH-mediated differentiation.

Drug and exposure gap. Unverified prior knowledge: teriflunomide is approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis, inhibits mitochondrial DHODH, and extracellular uridine salvage can bypass de novo pyrimidine synthesis. The packet provides no sourced human unbound-exposure or drug-specific potency estimates; numerical feasibility cannot honestly be asserted. Before efficacy testing, verify approval, indication, safety, protein binding, human steady-state unbound trough exposure, and published DHODH potency. Measure the unbound-medium EC80 for cellular DHODH inhibition under physiological uridine/protein conditions. Require an unbound trough/EC80 ratio ≥1 for the proposed sustained-inhibition schedule; otherwise this exposure claim fails. Test verified concentration–time profiles and measure free drug rather than substituting total concentration.

Quantitative context. Use independent primary donors, paired untreated cultures and cytarabine-pulse survivors after documented washout, followed by cytarabine-treated xenografts. Lock salvage-low/high at ≤25th/≥75th percentiles of viable-cell-normalized labeled-uridine→UTP flux, and oxidative-high at ≥75th percentile ATP-linked oxygen consumption, using an independent calibration cohort. These are prospective design cutoffs, not validated biomarkers. Measure de novo flux, proliferation and nucleotide-pool turnover. Use plasma-containing medium with measured physiological uridine; retain intermediate, salvage-high and less-oxidative controls.

Causal discrimination. Demonstrate DHODH-pathway inhibition and pyrimidine depletion before maturation. Include vehicle, cytarabine-only washout controls, DHODH knockdown, an unrelated DHODH inhibitor, viability-matched mitochondrial stress, and venetoclax–azacitidine [S3]. Preemptive uridine add-back must demonstrably restore intracellular pyrimidines before maturation begins. Rescue alone cannot establish low-salvage causality: inducibly raise or lower a validated uridine-salvage bottleneck after washout, at fixed extracellular uridine, with inactive-construct controls. Accept perturbations only when flux changes without major baseline viability/proliferation shifts. Increased salvage should suppress sensitivity; reduced salvage should confer it. Prespecify donor-blocked drug×persistence×salvage interaction analyses, accounting for respiration and cycling, with a minimum 10-percentage-point differentiation interaction.

Differentiation versus selection. Genotype retention alone cannot exclude selective survival of mature leukemic cells. Index-sort initially immature cells, follow individual lineages by live imaging, and confirm leukemia genotype at endpoints. Require a ≥20-percentage-point increase over vehicle in observed maturation among all starting tracked lineages, supported by morphology and a donor-validated myeloid-marker panel. Report apoptosis, absolute viable counts and cell cycle separately. After verified drug washout, require ≥50% reduction in serial colonies and ≥threefold reduction in leukemia-initiating frequency through equal-viable-leukemic-cell limiting-dilution transplantation. These are decision thresholds, not validated clinical surrogates; power validation at the donor level.

Selectivity and translation. Require ≤20% loss of normal-donor hematopoietic progenitor clonogenicity under matched exposure and culture conditions. In xenografts, measure plasma and marrow free exposure, differentiation and secondary engraftment, alongside normal-progenitor function. Unequal cycling or culture conditions must not masquerade as selectivity.

Alternatives, falsification and limitations. Off-target cytostasis, nonspecific respiratory injury, generalized nucleotide starvation and cytarabine carryover remain alternatives. With verified target inhibition and pyrimidine depletion, confidence bounds excluding the prespecified maturation or propagation effects would falsify the proposed benefit in the selected subgroup. Bounds excluding the interaction would reject residual-context specificity. Differentiation despite preemptive pyrimidine restoration, or failure of flux-confirmed salvage manipulation to alter response, would reject the proposed mediation. Activity confined to supraclinical exposure or excessive normal-progenitor injury defeats translation. Marrow-dependent salvage, donor heterogeneity, long-term hematopoietic toxicity and imperfect modeling of clinical residual disease remain unresolved. Bulk killing, tracked maturation and functional propagation must therefore remain separate outcomes.
