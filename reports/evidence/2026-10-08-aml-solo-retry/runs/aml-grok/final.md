Hypothesis title: Teriflunomide, an approved multiple-sclerosis drug, relieves differentiation blockade in human AML through on-target DHODH inhibition at exposures separable from venetoclax–azacitidine oxidative-phosphorylation suppression.

Biological context and hypothesis. The claim covers primary human AML only when viable blasts have a fraction positive for any of CD11b, CD14, or CD15 below 20% and morphologic myelocytes, metamyelocytes, and monocytes together below 10%. In that gated population, teriflunomide will induce cell-intrinsic myeloid maturation by inhibiting dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) at free concentrations at or below the unbound plasma maximum of its approved multiple-sclerosis dose, with normal CD34-positive colony formation retained at or above 70% of vehicle. Samples missing the immaturity gate, and samples lacking same-sample DHODH-pathway suppression, lie outside the claim and are reported separately. Bulk differentiation, leukemia-initiating activity, ROS-low quiescent leukemia stem cells, and cytarabine-residual oxidative cells stay separate endpoints.

Supported premises. [S4] reports that DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation in experimental AML systems and affected disease-related endpoints in those models, tying that result to myeloid maturation. [S4] leaves open whether an approved drug reproduces that result at suitable exposure and selectivity. [S1] reports that leukemia stem cells in the studied specimens often had low reactive-oxygen-species levels and elevated BCL-2, and that BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells. [S3] connects venetoclax–azacitidine, an established regimen and mechanistic comparator, to TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation changes in leukemia stem cells. Repeating that combination is not the proposed discovery. [S2] reports that cytarabine-residual cells were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells and showed oxidative-metabolism and fatty-acid-oxidation-related features, so residual disease and the ROS-low population are distinct contexts.

Speculative bridges, labeled as such. Unverified prior knowledge requiring source checking: teriflunomide is approved for relapsing multiple sclerosis and is described as a DHODH inhibitor; brequinar is the experimental DHODH inhibitor associated with the differentiation result summarized by [S4]; suppression is read as dihydroorotate accumulation and uridine-triphosphate depletion; and exogenous uridine bypasses a DHODH block. Approved dose, protein binding, free plasma Cmax, and marrow free level are unverified and must be locked from primary sources before testing. [S1]–[S4] do not establish those facts. Bridge one: multiple-sclerosis-compatible free exposure inhibits DHODH in gated blasts enough to recapitulate [S4]. Bridge two, which [S4] alone does not entail: when dihydroorotate and uridine-triphosphate changes match brequinar, maturation scales with the depth of pyrimidine-pool depletion. Bridge three: maturation is dissociable from loss of ROS-low, BCL-2-high leukemia stem cells [S1] and from the cytarabine-residual oxidative state [S2]. Bridge four: uridine rescue assigns the phenotype to DHODH rather than to off-target oxidative-phosphorylation suppression of the venetoclax–azacitidine type [S3].

Alternative explanations. These include selective death of immature blasts, proliferation arrest without lineage maturation, off-target oxidative-phosphorylation stress [S1], [S3] or use of the oxidative state in [S2], and host immunomodulation without blast-intrinsic DHODH inhibition.

Discriminating preclinical test. The numeric cutoffs below are prespecified decision thresholds, not results from [S1]–[S4]. In paired gated AML blasts and normal CD34-positive cells, and in separate ROS-low [S1] and cytarabine-residual [S2] fractions, compare teriflunomide, vehicle, brequinar, and venetoclax–azacitidine [S3], with teriflunomide capped at the pre-locked unbound plasma Cmax. Same-sample readouts are dihydroorotate and uridine triphosphate. Differentiation versus vehicle requires at least a 15-point rise in morphologic myelocytes, metamyelocytes, or monocytes and at least a twofold rise in viable cells acquiring CD11b, CD14, or CD15, at viability at least 70% of vehicle, plus a viability-matched cell-cycle comparison so G0/G1 arrest alone fails. The shift must reach at least half the brequinar morphologic and marker change at matched pathway suppression. Uridine must restore uridine triphosphate and reverse the AML differentiation and growth effects. Normal colonies must remain at or above 70% of vehicle at that AML concentration. An oxygen-consumption drop of 30% or more with viability loss is oxidative-phosphorylation suppression, scored apart from differentiation. The added positive pattern is a graded pyrimidine-pool versus maturation relationship across the series.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis fails if pathway-suppressed gated blasts miss the morphology, CD11b, CD14, CD15, and viability-matched cell-cycle criteria; if the uridine-reversible differentiation EC50 exceeds verified free plasma or marrow concentration; if uridine restores uridine triphosphate and differentiation persists; or if normal CD34-positive colonies fall below 70% of vehicle at the AML differentiation concentration. Failure of the locked free maximum to suppress dihydroorotate and uridine triphosphate falsifies the exposure bridge. Samples still unsuppressed above that ceiling lie outside the on-target claim. Uridine-resistant cytotoxicity plus ROS-low cell loss and reduced oxygen consumption reassigns the result to the comparator in [S1] and [S3] (PMIDs 23333149 and 30420752) even when a maturation marker rises. A finding limited to cytarabine-residual cells is interpreted with [S2] (PMID 28416471) and does not confirm the [S4] differentiation mechanism (PMID 27641501).

Limitations. [S4] is confined to the models studied. Genotype range inside the gate is unverified. Differentiation can transiently enlarge recognizable myeloid bulk. Cytarabine interaction is untested.
