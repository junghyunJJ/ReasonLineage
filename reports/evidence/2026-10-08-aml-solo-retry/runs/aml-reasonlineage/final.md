Title: Atovaquone differentiates salvage-limited, differentiation-blocked cytarabine-residual AML by collapsing ubiquinone-dependent DHODH activity before ATP loss.

Hypothesis and context. In post-cytarabine residual AML that still shows a differentiation block, relies on de novo pyrimidine synthesis, and retains oxidative metabolism, atovaquone at a verified unbound exposure will inhibit respiratory complex III, shrink the oxidized ubiquinone pool, and thereby restrict DHODH long enough to induce genotype-confirmed myeloid maturation and lower leukemia propagation. The intended mediator is pyrimidine depletion with ATP still intact. The original indication is treatment and prophylaxis of Pneumocystis pneumonia, and atovaquone plus proguanil for malaria; those label facts, reported protein binding above 99.9%, and any mammalian complex III potency are unverified prior knowledge and must be confirmed before exposure claims are made.

Supported premises. Cytarabine-resistant cells in the studied models were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells; they showed mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation-linked oxidative metabolism, and changing that state changed response [S2]. Separately, DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation and affected disease endpoints in experimental AML [S4]. ROS-low, BCL-2-high quiescent leukemia stem cells are a distinct population and a selectivity benchmark [S1]. Venetoclax–azacitidine is only the established metabolic comparator [S3]. Nothing in [S2] or [S4] shows that residual cells are DHODH-dependent or salvage-limited; that gap is a go/no-go, not an assumption.

Speculative bridges. Bridge 1: ubiquinone redox restriction will suppress DHODH before nonspecific energetic failure at unbound levels that do not exceed label-verified unbound Cmax. Bridge 2: residual cells that pass the gates below will mature, whereas normal progenitors in a matched cycling state will keep colony-forming capacity. Bridge 3: a functional respiratory chain can support DHODH, so an oxidative phenotype is a complex III entry point, while pyrimidine dependence is set by de novo-versus-salvage flux. Human DHODH use of oxidized ubiquinone, uridine bypass of de novo synthesis, and the properties of Saccharomyces cerevisiae URA1 (cytosolic, fumarate-linked, respiratory-chain-independent type 1 DHODH) are unverified prior knowledge. Teriflunomide, the active metabolite of leflunomide (rheumatoid arthritis; teriflunomide itself approved for relapsing multiple sclerosis; unverified prior knowledge), is the reference DHODH inhibitor, not the repurposing claim.

Preclinical strategy. Lock gates on the same primary specimens before any atovaquone attribution. Eligibility requires a cytarabine pulse and washout, residual oxygen consumption with an etomoxir-sensitive fraction at least 1.5-fold matched pretreatment blasts, proliferation recorded as a covariate, baseline CD11b and CD14 each below 20% of genotype-confirmed cells with blast morphology, and salvage limitation defined by tracer partitioning (at least half of UTP from the de novo route) or by uridine reversing at least 70% of the reference inhibitor’s nucleotide and maturation effects. Labeled-uridine incorporation is only a cycling covariate. ROS-low leukemia stem cells [S1] and pretreatment bulk blasts are separate arms. The go/no-go requires teriflunomide, in protein-containing medium, to raise dihydroorotate:orotate at least twofold and CD11b by at least 15 percentage points while ATP stays within 20% of vehicle, with uridine reversing both. Failure aborts the atovaquone test in that context. Isolated-enzyme assays must show atovaquone inhibits complex III and spares DHODH, and teriflunomide the reverse. Measure free atovaquone; the translational claim fails if the free EC50 for a twofold dihydroorotate:orotate shift exceeds label-verified unbound Cmax. A time gate must show ubiquinone reduction and pyrimidine-flux change while ATP loss is still under 20% and before maturation. Coincident ATP collapse makes the run uninterpretable and stops it. In an engineered line, induce URA1 only after the residual state is established and prove that pyrimidine restoration leaves complex III and ubiquinone inhibition intact. Uridine and URA1 must first reverse teriflunomide; if either rescue fails against teriflunomide, stop. Later endpoints are morphology plus the marker panel, absolute viable counts, apoptosis, and post-washout clonogenicity. Selectivity fails if normal CD34-positive colony inhibition at the free EC50 is within threefold of AML. Only after these gates, cytarabine-primed xenografts test marrow free drug, differentiation, limiting-dilution initiating-cell frequency (prespecified threefold reduction), and normal hematopoietic recovery. A documented search must separate this residual-state, salvage-gated, ubiquinone-timed claim from any published atovaquone or leflunomide AML work; this packet does not establish novelty.

Alternatives and falsification. Apparent maturation can reflect preferential loss of immature cells, delayed cytarabine injury, direct DHODH binding, other atovaquone targets, or ATP collapse. The relationship is falsified, even if atovaquone remains cytotoxic, if validated uridine and URA1 rescues restore pyrimidine pools and leave complex III and ubiquinone inhibition intact yet differentiation and at least a threefold clonogenic loss persist, while the same rescues reverse teriflunomide.

Limitations. Protein binding may leave no unbound window; proliferating normal progenitors may be equally sensitive; differentiation need not durably clear leukemia; [S2] models may differ from clinical marrow; [S4] does not transfer across genotypes; murine pyrimidine metabolism may diverge. Exposure, construct biochemistry, and prior AML studies require external verification.
