# AML evidence packet — draft v1

This curated input is not a comprehensive review. Every method receives the same UTF-8 file. Summaries are original paraphrases. Bibliographic metadata and abstracts were checked through the public NCBI ID converter and PubMed EFetch on 2026-10-05. Selected studies predate 2025. Co-Scientist's validated candidates and generated proposals are not input or the only correct answers.

## Research question

Propose one mechanistically specific AML drug-repurposing hypothesis using a drug approved for a different indication. Specify the biological context, supported premises and speculative bridges, selectivity and translational limitations, and a discriminating preclinical validation strategy with a falsifying outcome. State the original indication and distinguish verified facts from prior knowledge that needs checking. Do not present an established AML regimen as a new repurposing discovery. This adapts the Co-Scientist objective rather than reproducing a private original prompt.

## S1 — A leukemia-stem-cell metabolic dependency

Lagadinou et al. (2013), *BCL-2 inhibition targets oxidative phosphorylation and selectively eradicates quiescent human leukemia stem cells*. Cell Stem Cell. DOI: `10.1016/j.stem.2012.12.013`; PMID: `23333149`; PMCID: `PMC3595363`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23333149/).

Functionally defined leukemia stem cells in the studied specimens frequently had low reactive-oxygen-species levels and elevated BCL-2. BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells. This motivates investigating specific dependencies and selectivity. It does not establish that an arbitrary mitochondrial inhibitor has an acceptable therapeutic window or works across every AML context.

## S2 — Resistant cells are not necessarily the same stem-cell population

Farge et al. (2017), *Chemotherapy-Resistant Human Acute Myeloid Leukemia Cells Are Not Enriched for Leukemic Stem Cells but Require Oxidative Metabolism*. Cancer Discovery. DOI: `10.1158/2159-8290.CD-16-0441`; PMID: `28416471`; PMCID: `PMC5501738`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28416471/).

Residual cells in cytarabine-treated models were not consistently enriched for immature, quiescent leukemia stem cells. They displayed oxidative-metabolism-associated properties, including mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation-related features. Altering that state changed treatment response in the studied settings. This cautions against equating all residual disease with the ROS-low population in S1. These model findings do not establish clinical efficacy of a new compound.

## S3 — An established mechanistic comparator

Pollyea et al. (2018), *Venetoclax with azacitidine disrupts energy metabolism and targets leukemia stem cells in patients with acute myeloid leukemia*. Nature Medicine. DOI: `10.1038/s41591-018-0233-1`; PMID: `30420752`; PMCID: `PMC7001730`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30420752/).

The report connected venetoclax–azacitidine treatment to changes in leukemia-stem-cell energy metabolism, including TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation effects, using patient-associated observations and experimental follow-up. It provides an established comparator. Repeating this combination is not a new repurposing proposal. The findings cannot be transferred directly to another drug or treated as universal randomized comparative efficacy evidence.

## S4 — Differentiation as a distinct outcome

Sykes et al. (2016), *Inhibition of Dihydroorotate Dehydrogenase Overcomes Differentiation Blockade in Acute Myeloid Leukemia*. Cell. DOI: `10.1016/j.cell.2016.08.057`; PMID: `27641501`; PMCID: `PMC7360335`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27641501/).

DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation in experimental AML systems and affected disease-related endpoints in the investigated models. This connects metabolism with differentiation and supplies an alternative to nonspecific cell killing. It does not establish that every approved drug affecting this pathway reproduces the result or achieves suitable exposure and selectivity.

## Evidence boundaries

- Specify context and endpoint; distinguish bulk-cell effects, stem-cell effects, and differentiation.
- Approval, original indication, exposure and selectivity facts outside these summaries need explicit verification. Label recollections as unverified prior knowledge.
- Include normal-cell and mechanistic comparators; a pathway link alone is insufficient evidence of efficacy.
- State what would disconfirm the proposed drug–mechanism–context relationship.
- Absence from Co-Scientist's reported positive candidates is not an incorrect-answer label.
