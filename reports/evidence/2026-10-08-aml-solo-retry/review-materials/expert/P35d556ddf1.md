HYPOTHESIS TITLE: Repurposing teriflunomide, an approved multiple sclerosis drug, as an on-target DHODH-directed differentiation therapy for differentiation-blocked AML

CORE HYPOTHESIS. Teriflunomide (the active molecule corresponding to the leflunomide class; original indication: relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis, with leflunomide historically approved for rheumatoid/psoriatic arthritis - approval facts are unverified prior knowledge to be confirmed against the regulatory label) reaches clinically achievable free concentrations that inhibit dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) in AML blasts. Blocking DHODH-mediated conversion of dihydroorotate to orotate (textbook biochemistry, outside-packet general knowledge) depletes de novo pyrimidine synthesis, releases the myeloid differentiation block, and drives terminal maturation with cell-cycle exit. The hypothesis is only claimed for differentiation-blocked AML contexts where the dominant residual pathology is an immature, lineage-blocked blast population; it is not a stem-cell-eradication claim and teriflunomide is not an established AML regimen.

SUPPORTED PREMISES (from the packet).
- P1 (S4, core): DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation and altered disease-related endpoints in experimental AML, linking pyrimidine metabolism to the differentiative state. S4 explicitly does not establish that every approved drug acting on this pathway reproduces the result or achieves suitable exposure and selectivity; closing exactly that gap is the purpose of this hypothesis.
- P2 (S2, context): chemotherapy-persistent AML cells were not consistently LSC-enriched but depended on oxidative metabolism, showing that residual disease contains metabolically definable, targetable states distinct from bulk untreated biology.
- P3 (S1, framing): quiescent ROS-low leukemia stem cells were BCL-2-dependent and OXPHOS-linked, demonstrating that AML populations can carry selective metabolic liabilities (e.g., venetoclax-class sensitivity). This motivates selectivity reasoning only; it does not predict DHODH effects on LSCs.
- P4 (S3, comparator): venetoclax-azacitidine showed patient-associated TCA-cycle/OXPHOS changes in leukemia stem-enriched cells, providing an established metabolic-mechanism comparator and a biomarker template. No packet source supports teriflunomide efficacy in AML; that gap is what the hypothesis proposes to test.

EXPLICITLY SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (assumptions, not evidence).
- B1: clinically achievable free teriflunomide exceeds the DHODH inhibitory threshold in blasts. Teriflunomide is reportedly highly protein-bound with a long half-life (unverified prior knowledge); B1 is the central quantitative assumption and may fail.
- B2: blasts depend on de novo pyrimidine synthesis despite plasma uridine salvage, and normal CD34+ cells depend less, yielding a therapeutic window. Indirectly consistent with S4, but unproven for this drug and compartment.
- B3: because DHODH donates electrons into the CoQ/respiratory chain, inhibition might additionally perturb respiration in the OXPHOS-dependent residual cells of S2. Conjecture, treated only as a secondary endpoint.
- B4: differentiation induction may impair leukemia stem-cell self-renewal without direct stem-cell killing; if it does not, teriflunomide would be an add-on rather than a root-of-disease agent.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. Reported extra-DHODH activities of the leflunomide metabolite on tyrosine kinases such as JAK3/EGFR at higher concentrations (unverified prior knowledge); nonspecific nucleotide depletion causing cytostasis, apoptosis, or marker drift rather than true maturation; mitochondrial electron-flux effects independent of pyrimidine starvation; differentiation-marker changes as artifacts of culture conditions or serum pyrimidine content.

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL STRATEGY.
1) Engagement: LC-MS quantification of the dihydroorotate:orotate ratio and UTP/CTP pools in primary blast cultures from a genomically diverse panel (NPM1-mutant, FLT3-ITD, TP53-mutant, MLL-rearranged) at free-fraction-matched teriflunomide concentrations derived from label pharmacokinetics.
2) Specificity matrix: pyrimidine (uridine) rescue must abolish both metabolic and differentiative effects; DHODH overexpression or an inhibitor-resistant variant should confer resistance; a structurally unrelated DHODH inhibitor should phenocopy. Absence of rescue or genetic interaction indicates off-target action.
3) Endpoint separation: quantify CD11b/CD14/CD16 induction and NBT reduction separately from S-phase exit and apoptosis, benchmarked against a purely cytotoxic control.
4) Selectivity: normal cytokine-supported CD34+ HSPC colony-forming assays at identical free concentrations.
5) In vivo: NSG xenografts/PDX dosed to human-equivalent exposure, measuring marrow blast differentiation, DHODH-substrate accumulation, and survival; S3-inspired biomarker logic as the analytic template.
6) Only after single-agent validation, exploratory testing of teriflunomide added to an HMA or venetoclax in residual-disease protocols, using S3 as the established comparator rather than as the proposal itself.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION. At free concentrations matching clinically achievable exposure: (a) no DHODH engagement in blasts; or (b) engagement without differentiation (only cytostasis/apoptosis); or (c) differentiation effects that are neither rescued by pyrimidines nor modified by DHODH genetic state; or (d) comparable killing of normal CD34+ colonies. Any of (a)-(d) across the panel and in vivo disproves the proposed drug-mechanism-context relationship.

LIMITATIONS. Multi-week half-life implies slow pharmacodynamics and delayed response assessment (unverified label detail); known class risks include immunosuppression, myelosuppression, hepatotoxicity, and teratogenicity, all requiring label confirmation; murine xenografts poorly model human pyrimidine salvage; I cannot verify from the packet whether teriflunomide or leflunomide has been tested in AML, so if comparable data exist the novelty claim must be re-scoped; investigational DHODH inhibitors in AML development are not approved drugs and do not satisfy, nor pre-empt, the repurposing question posed here.
