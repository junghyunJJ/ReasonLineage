# P1e6aa4ab32

Title: Stress-activated phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 initiates selective nuclear import failure upstream of TDP-43 mislocalization in TDP-43-proteinopathy motor neurons.

Hypothesis statement. In vulnerable motor neurons of ALS characterized by TDP-43 proteinopathy (scoping to sporadic disease; C9orf72-initiated disease is intentionally excluded as a separate context), sustained cellular stress activates a nuclear kinase that phosphorylates the FG-repeat nucleoporin Nup98 at interphase sites. This phosphorylation weakens the NPC permeability barrier and Ran-dependent selective import - the initiating lesion. TDP-43 egress to the cytoplasm and transport-factor sequestration are downstream amplifiers, not the first cause.

Supported premises (from the packet only).
- S1: in ALS/FTD tissue and experimental systems, NPC components associate with insoluble TDP-43, and disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport. This establishes that pore/transport dysfunction accompanies TDP-43 pathology, but not its direction.
- S3: persistent, stress-granule-independent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies impair nuclear import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, recruit or mislocalize transport proteins, and are associated with cell death. This supports the downstream amplification arm.
- S4: stress granules sequester transport factors and impair transport; altering SG assembly modifies transport phenotypes. This defines the sequestration-based alternative my hypothesis must be discriminated from.
- S2: C9orf72 repeat RNA mislocalizes RanGAP and impairs import in patient-derived neurons. This supports transport dysfunction in that genetic context only; the hypothesis does not claim to cover it, though convergence is allowed.

Assumptions and speculative bridges (unverified by any supplied source).
- A1: an interphase kinase phosphorylates Nup98 in adult motor neurons. Candidate assumption: stress-activated p38/JNK; alternative assumption: ectopic mitotic-like kinase activity. No packet source supports any kinase.
- A2: the relevant phospho-sites cluster in or near Nup98 FG repeats and phosphorylation there raises passive permeability and lowers selective import. This is motivated by unverified prior knowledge that Nup98 is multiply phosphorylated during mitotic NPC disassembly - analogy only, not packet evidence.
- A3 (directional inversion): pore phosphorylation precedes TDP-43 egress. S1 documents co-occurrence, not direction.
- A4: the mechanism is specific to TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS rather than universal.

Causal chain. Initiating: stress kinase activation -> Nup98 phosphorylation -> weakening of the FG network -> passive leak plus selective import decline plus Ran-gradient dissipation -> failure to retain/import nuclear TDP-43. Downstream: cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing (S3-type, SG-independent), SG-mediated transport-factor sequestration (S4-type), further transport collapse, cell death.

Alternative explanations to discriminate.
1. Sequestration-first: SGs or TDP-43 assemblies deplete soluble import machinery; phosphorylation is absent or irrelevant.
2. Physical displacement: insoluble TDP-43 directly anchors to or distorts pores (consistent with S1), with phosphorylation as a bystander.
3. Genetic route: C9orf72 repeat RNA/DPR mechanisms (S2) produce the same transport phenotype independently.
4. Reverse causality: phosphorylation is a late consequence of stress or of pore disassembly, not an initiator.

Discriminating preclinical strategy (temporal plus perturbational).
- Models: isogenic human iPSC-derived motor neurons with TDP-43-proteinopathy phenotypes (sporadic patient lines; optionally inducible nuclear TDP-43 depletion) and healthy isogenic controls.
- Temporal test: time-resolved phosphoproteomics of purified nuclear-envelope fractions plus high-content imaging of phospho-Nup98 signal, NLS-reporter import, Ran-gradient integrity, and TDP-43 localization across stress onset. Hypothesis predicts the Nup98 phospho-signal rises before import declines and before TDP-43 egress; sequestration-first predicts soluble transport-factor depletion precedes any detectable Nup phosphorylation.
- Perturbational test: CRISPR knock-in of phospho-null (Ser/Thr to Ala) and phosphomimetic (Ser/Thr to Asp/Glu) Nup98 alleles. Support: phospho-null preserves import and nuclear TDP-43 even with stress granules assembling normally, and phosphomimetic Nup98 acutely impairs import in unstressed neurons without inducing TDP-43 aggregates. Contra-support: phospho-null fails to rescue while SG dissolution rescues transport with no Nup phospho-dependence.
- Kinase test: small-molecule blockade plus a conditional kinase-null allele; require concordant rescue to control off-targets; monitor SG formation in parallel so kinase effects on granules are not confounded with pore phosphorylation.
- Mechanistic separation: co-measure passive NPC permeability (small-tracer flux), facilitated import rates, and Ran gradient, distinguishing pore modification from transport-factor depletion rather than relying on timing alone.
- In vivo and tissue: neuron-targeted Nup98 phospho-site editing or kinase inhibition in a conditional TDP-43 mislocalization mouse model, with motor-neuron import markers, TDP-43 localization, and motor behavior/ survival as endpoints; post-mortem ALS motor cortex/spinal cord immunostained for phospho-Nup98 in neurons lacking cytoplasmic TDP-43 inclusions (early) versus affected neurons (late).

Falsifying observation. In phospho-null Nup98 knock-in stressed motor neurons, nuclear import still fails and TDP-43 still mislocalizes, while soluble transport factors are depleted from the nucleoplasm temporally before any detectable Nup98 phosphorylation; additionally, phosphomimetic Nup98 leaves import kinetics unchanged in healthy neurons. Both outcomes together would falsify the initiating-phosphorylation hypothesis.

Limitations. The hypothesis is scoped to TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS and may not generalize to C9orf72-initiated disease (S2 context). Packet evidence for pore involvement is associative; kinase identity, sites, and direction are assumptions. Nucleoporin redundancy may buffer single-site mutations; iPSC and mouse models may not reproduce decades-long onset; post-mortem tissue is late-stage; phospho-signals may be transient.
