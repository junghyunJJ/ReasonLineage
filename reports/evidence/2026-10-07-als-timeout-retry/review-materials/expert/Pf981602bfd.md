# Pf981602bfd

FG-region NUP98 phosphorylation by reactivated CDK1 initiates Ran-gradient failure after nuclear TDP-43 dysfunction in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In motor neurons carrying ALS-associated TARDBP M337V, nuclear TDP-43 dysfunction promotes CDK1 phosphorylation of NUP98 inside its FG/GLFG cohesive region. That modification initiates the transport lesion: it weakens FG cohesion, raises size-selective passive permeability, and collapses the Ran/NTF2 gradient before persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies appear. Import failure then deepens nuclear TDP-43 loss, permits cytoplasmic demixing, and drives neuronal dysfunction. “Initiating” means the start of the transport defect in this TARDBP context. The M337V association, CDK1 identity, and FG-domain biology are unverified prior knowledge. C9orf72 repeat RNA is a separate route to RanGAP mislocalization and import failure [S2] and is a boundary contrast, not support for this initiator.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form without conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and accompany cell death; phosphorylated TDP-43 accumulates in those assemblies [S3]. Cellular stress can also sequester transport factors in stress granules and impair transport [S4]. No supplied source identifies a nucleoporin phosphorylation event or its order relative to TDP-43 dysfunction.

Speculative bridges. Unverified prior knowledge holds that NUP98’s N-terminal FG/GLFG repeats cohere to set passive permeability, that its C-terminal domain anchors it at the pore, and that mitotic CDK1 phosphorylation of NUP98 promotes NPC disassembly. The hypothesis assumes that partial FG-restricted phosphorylation in postmitotic neurons can increase permeability and disturb the Ran gradient without full NPC disassembly. It assumes nuclear mutant TDP-43 alters splicing of a CDK1 regulator or an opposing phosphatase so CDK1 occupancy on NUP98 rises. It assumes the import defect is sufficient for later TDP-43 redistribution. Any claim that phosphorylated TDP-43 causes demixing is untested; [S3] only places phosphorylated TDP-43 inside assemblies. No kinase, residue, or phosphorylation-dependent contact is given by [S1]–[S4].

Alternatives. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 demixing [S3] or stress-granule sequestration of transport factors [S4] may impair transport first, with NUP98 phosphorylation appearing afterward. Generic proteostatic or metabolic stress may shift phosphorylation and transport together. Phosphorylation confined to the NUP98 anchor domain, or of another nucleoporin, may account for the pore phenotype. Repeat-RNA mechanisms [S2] stay outside this TARDBP test. GSK3β or JNK marking is a competing stress-kinase explanation.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Study independently derived TARDBP M337V iPSC motor neurons and isogenic corrected controls, without an exogenous TDP-43 inducer. Live-cell imaging and bulk biochemistry share a pre-registered maturation clock. Define t0 from live imaging as the first window in which mutant import of a releasable NLS reporter diverges from corrected cells by a pre-set effect size. At matched earlier and later windows, measure a nuclear TDP-43 splicing target that encodes a CDK1 regulator or opposing phosphatase; CDK1–NUP98 occupancy versus GSK3β and JNK occupancy; NPC immunoprecipitate phosphoproteomics; pore-associated NUP98; size-resolved passive permeability; the Ran gradient; transport-factor localization; conventional stress granules; cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies; firing and survival; and energy status plus general-stress markers.

Site acceptance is pre-registered and precedes allele engineering. A residue must be detected on endogenous NUP98, enriched in mutant neurons at or before t0, map in the FG/GLFG region rather than solely in the anchoring domain, and match a CDK consensus. If no residue qualifies, engineering stops and the FG–CDK1 hypothesis fails in this model. If one or more qualify, introduce endogenous phospho-resistant substitutions and a separate CDK1 loss-of-function allele, each with sequence-reverted and kinase-intact controls. Add acute NUP98-directed phosphatase recruitment with catalytically inactive and untargeted controls. In corrected neurons these reagents must preserve NUP98 abundance, baseline permeability, and import. Expression of nuclear-retained wild-type TDP-43 in mutant neurons, versus a cytoplasmic-restricted control, tests whether phosphorylation tracks nuclear TDP-43 function. Separate suppression of cytoplasmic TDP-43 demixing and of conventional stress-granule assembly asks whether the early permeability and Ran defects remain when those downstream events are blocked. Sufficiency uses site-specifically phosphorylated versus unmodified NUP98 in a reconstituted FG-cohesion and permeability assay; only a biochemically validated phosphomimetic is then introduced into unstressed corrected neurons, with confirmation of normal pore incorporation.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis is falsified if no qualifying FG-region phosphorylation precedes t0, or if two orthogonal interventions that prevent that phosphorylation and preserve baseline pore function leave the timing and magnitude of import failure, Ran-gradient loss, and neuronal dysfunction unchanged. A splicing defect that appears only after phosphorylation, or phosphorylation that falls when general-stress markers fall while the nuclear TDP-43 splicing defect persists, also contradicts the proposed order. A phosphomimetic that fails the reconstituted permeability test is inconclusive.

Limitations. Undetected small assemblies, redundant kinases, and additional phosphosites can hide single-site effects. Phosphatase recruitment and amino-acid substitution can act off-target. NUP98 also functions in mRNA export and chromatin contacts. Reconstituted assays omit neuronal context, and cultured motor neurons omit aging and non-cell-autonomous influences. A positive result would support a bounded TARDBP mechanism, not a validated treatment or a cause shared by every ALS subtype.
