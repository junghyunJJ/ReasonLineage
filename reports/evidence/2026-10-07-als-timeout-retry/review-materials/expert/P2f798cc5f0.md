# P2f798cc5f0

Nup98 phosphorylation by a stress-responsive proline-directed kinase initiates nuclear-pore barrier failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy models of ALS

Hypothesis. In neuronal models of TDP-43 proteinopathy, a setting relevant to ALS and ALS/FTD cases that accumulate cytoplasmic TDP-43, phosphorylation of Nup98, an FG-nucleoporin by unverified prior knowledge, is proposed as the initiating, pore-intrinsic cause of selective nucleocytoplasmic-transport failure. Impaired nuclear import, loss of nuclear TDP-43, growth of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and later neuronal dysfunction are the proposed downstream responses. Nucleoporin phosphorylation remains an untested candidate cause.

Supported premises. Nuclear-pore and transport components associate with insoluble TDP-43, and disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in cell systems, with related pore pathology in ALS tissue [S1]. Those findings support a TDP-43-pore connection while leaving the initiating biochemical event and cross-subtype generality unresolved [S1]. In a separate genetic context, expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA is linked to altered RanGAP localization and impaired nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons, and transport or repeat-RNA manipulations modify model phenotypes [S2]. This is a C9orf72-specific route and leaves both sporadic ALS and any nucleoporin phosphorylation step unresolved [S2]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, and accompany import failure, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death; phosphorylated TDP-43 accumulates in the assemblies [S3]. That phosphorylation mark is on TDP-43, and the experiments leave open other physiological trajectories [S3]. Cellular stress recruits transport factors into stress granules and impairs transport, and changing stress-granule assembly modifies transport defects and disease-model phenotypes [S4].

Speculative bridges. The kinase, the residues, and the causal order are assumptions. Unverified prior knowledge, held outside this packet and given no citation identifier, is that Nup98 phosphorylation during mitotic or stress signaling can relax nuclear-pore selectivity. The working assumption is that a proline-directed stress-responsive kinase, provisionally CDK5 or ERK1/2, phosphorylates Nup98 FG-repeat or C-terminal residues in post-mitotic neurons expressing pathogenic TDP-43. The proposed sequence is kinase activation, Nup98 phosphorylation, altered NPC barrier function, and only then sequestration of transport factors plus loss of nuclear TDP-43. Nup98 phosphorylation is the initiating pore event. Import failure, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death are downstream responses of the kind reported with cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies [S3]. Scope is limited to TDP-43 proteinopathy models. C9orf72-ALS retains RanGAP mislocalization as a separate entry point [S2].

Alternative explanations. Sequestration of transport receptors and Ran regulators into stress granules [S4], or into stress-granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies [S3], can impair import while nucleoporins themselves remain unmodified. Nucleoporin mislocalization can follow binding to insoluble TDP-43 [S1]. Broad cellular stress can degrade transport without a Nup98 phospho-event. Repeat RNA and RanGAP pathology can account for transport failure in C9orf72 models [S2]. Shared late phenotypes, reduced import and nuclear TDP-43 loss, therefore fail to identify which event came first.

Discriminating preclinical test. Use a neuronal TDP-43 proteinopathy model that develops transport defects, and run a C9orf72 repeat model only as a context comparator. On a time course, measure Nup98 phosphorylation in nuclear-pore fractions by mass spectrometry and phospho-specific blot, Nup98 localization at NPCs, nuclear import of a reporter and of endogenous TDP-43, stress-granule markers, and stress-granule-independent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. The prediction is that Nup98 phosphorylation rises before import falls and before transport factors accumulate in either compartment. A non-phosphorylatable Nup98 that incorporates into NPCs should preserve import when stress granules are induced [S4] and when early cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing is present [S3]. A phosphomimetic Nup98 in otherwise unstressed neurons should impair import without stress granules and before large TDP-43 aggregates. Kinase inhibition should copy the non-phosphorylatable Nup98 result only while those Nup98 sites are intact. If phosphomimetic Nup98 is already installed, blocking stress-granule assembly or TDP-43 de-mixing should leave a residual import defect. The sequestration alternatives predict the complementary pattern: phosphomimetic Nup98 is inert when transport factors remain free, and blocking granules or TDP-43 assemblies restores import regardless of the Nup98 phospho-state.

Falsifying observation. Reject the hypothesis in this model if import failure and nuclear TDP-43 loss occur with a flat Nup98 phosphorylation time course. Reject it if NPC-incorporated non-phosphorylatable Nup98 leaves the onset and size of the transport defect unchanged while stress-granule or TDP-43-assembly interventions fully account for that defect. Reject it if phosphomimetic Nup98 impairs import only when transport factors are sequestered. A phospho-Nup98 rise confined to the C9orf72 comparator, with no rise in the TDP-43 model, rejects the proposed disease context.

Limitations. Sites and kinase identity are assumed, and phosphorylation may be a later mark of pore injury rather than its start. Neuronal cultures omit glia, aging, and circuit context, and partial Nup98 replacement can mask an effect. Phospho-specific reagents can cross-react with phosphorylated TDP-43 or other FG-nucleoporins. Preservation of import by non-phosphorylatable Nup98 would still leave other nucleoporins and the RanGAP axis [S2] open. The experiment tests a mechanism and does not validate a treatment.
