# Pfb5cbca8fc

Title: Stress-activated phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 initiates selective nuclear import failure upstream of TDP-43 mislocalization in TDP-43-proteinopathy motor neurons.

Hypothesis. In sporadic ALS motor neurons with TDP-43 proteinopathy, sustained stress activates a kinase that phosphorylates Nup98 at FG/GLFG-region sites, weakening the NPC permeability barrier and the rate of facilitated NLS-cargo import. This initiating pore modification reduces nuclear retention and import of TDP-43; cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing, stress-granule-driven sequestration of transport factors, Ran-gradient collapse, and cell death are downstream amplifiers, not the first cause. C9orf72-initiated disease (S2 context) is excluded from scope, though convergence is permitted.

Supported premises (packet only). S1: NPC/transport disruption accompanies TDP-43 pathology in ALS tissue and experimental systems; co-occurrence, not direction. S3: stress-granule-independent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and recruit/mislocalize transport proteins — the downstream arm. S4: SG assembly sequesters transport factors and impairs transport — both the sequestration alternative and the reason a phospho-null cannot be predicted to fully preserve import, since SGs independently impair transport. S2: transport dysfunction in the C9 genetic context only.

Assumptions (no packet support). A1: a stress-activated kinase (p38/JNK as candidates) phosphorylates Nup98 in adult motor neurons. A2: candidate residues are 2–3 clustered Ser/Thr sites in/near GLFG repeats and adjacent linkers, nominated a priori from mitotic Nup98 phospho-mapping; this sourcing is unverified prior knowledge and analogy, to be re-confirmed by MS before use. A3: the directional claim that phosphorylation precedes TDP-43 egress, inverted from S1's co-occurrence. A4: specificity to TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS. Asp/Glu phosphomimetic substitutions are acknowledged as chemically poor phosphate mimics and will anchor no conclusion.

Causal chain (revised). Initiating: kinase activation → Nup98 FG phosphorylation → increased passive permeability and reduced facilitated NLS-import at the pore. Secondary: reduced nuclear TDP-43 retention → cytoplasmic de-mixing (S3) → SG formation and transport-factor sequestration (S4) → Ran-gradient dissipation → cell death. Ran gradient is deliberately decoupled from the initiating lesion: RanGAP/RCC1 localization primarily maintain it, so gradient changes are used as downstream readouts, not bundled into the initiation claim.

Alternative explanations. (1) Sequestration-first: SGs or TDP-43 assemblies deplete soluble import machinery; phosphorylation is absent or irrelevant. (2) Physical displacement: insoluble TDP-43 directly distorts pores (consistent with S1), phosphorylation a bystander. (3) Genetic route: C9orf72 repeat RNA/DPR mechanisms (S2). (4) Reverse causality: late phosphorylation secondary to stress or disassembly.

Discriminating strategy.
• Modification validation first: knock in endogenous tagged Nup98; map phosphorylation sites by MS of purified nuclear-envelope fractions across a stress time course; validate phosphopeptide-specific antibodies against mapped sites, or use chemically inducible kinase recruitment to tagged Nup98 for acute, reversible gain-of-function — this replaces reliance on Asp/Glu phosphomimetics, which remain secondary only.
• Direct attribution: digitonin-permeabilized neurons reconstituted with recombinant phosphorylated versus phospho-null Nup98 (FG-region constructs MS-verified) to measure passive tracer flux and NLS-import kinetics directly attributable to the modification, separating pore effects from import-factor or Ran effects.
• Temporal tests: (i) stress-onset course of Nup98 phospho-occupancy, NLS-reporter import, and TDP-43 localization; (ii) stress pulse–washout: if phosphorylation initiates, the phospho-signal should dissipate before import recovers; import failure persisting after phospho-loss supports a sequestration-first alternative and counters late-bystander phosphorylation.
• Perturbational test with graded predictions: a CRISPR multi-site phospho-null panel (clustered Ala substitutions across the nominated GLFG cluster), interpreted only after MS verification that candidate-site phospho-occupancy is suppressed. Outcome metric: NLS-import decline normalized to SG burden and soluble importin availability, quantified across mild-to-strong stress. Graded prediction: phospho-null neurons show a significantly attenuated early-phase import decline versus wild type at matched SG burden — not full preservation, because S4 establishes SG-independent-of-phosphorylation impairment. Late-phase failure in phospho-null neurons is expected and non-discriminating; only the early phosphorylation-attributable component counts. Interpretation rule: verified occupancy suppression plus an indistinguishable early normalized decline falsifies the hypothesis; failure to suppress occupancy (compensatory kinases/buffering) makes the test uninformative, not falsifying.
• Staged in vivo arm: only after in vitro discrimination, neuron-targeted multi-site Nup98 editing or kinase inhibition in a conditional TDP-43-mislocalization mouse, with import markers, TDP-43 localization, motor behavior, and survival as endpoints; post-mortem ALS tissue immunostained with the validated phospho-Nup98 reagent in neurons lacking cytoplasmic TDP-43 inclusions (early) versus affected neurons (late), as corroboration only.

Falsifying observation. With MS-verified suppression of the targeted Nup98 phospho-cluster (and no compensating phosphorylation at other mapped sites), mild stress produces an early-phase normalized import decline equal to wild type at matched SG burden and soluble importin availability; and in pulse–washout, import failure precedes or outlasts detectable Nup98 phosphorylation. Together these falsify initiating phosphorylation. Phosphomimetic results alone would not count toward falsification.

Limitations. Scoped to TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS; the S2 context is excluded. Packet evidence for pore involvement is associative; kinase identity, residues, and direction remain assumptions pending MS confirmation. Asp/Glu mimicry is weak, so gain-of-function relies on inducible kinase recruitment. Buffering is addressed only for the nominated cluster; broader nucleoporin compensation remains possible. iPSC and mouse models may not reproduce decades-long onset; post-mortem tissue is late-stage; phospho-signals are transient.
