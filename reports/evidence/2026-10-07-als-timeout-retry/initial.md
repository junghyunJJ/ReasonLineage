NUP98 Ser606 phosphorylation as an initiating pore lesion in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In motor neurons carrying the ALS-associated TARDBP M337V variant, increased phosphorylation of NUP98 at candidate residue Ser606 reduces NUP98 retention at nuclear pores, initiating impaired nuclear import before detectable cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. Import failure subsequently promotes nuclear TDP-43 depletion, cytoplasmic accumulation, and neuronal dysfunction. Here, “initiating” means initiating the transport defect—not preceding the genetic mutation or explaining every ALS subtype. The variant’s disease association and the selection of NUP98 Ser606 draw on unverified prior knowledge, not the supplied packet.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can impair import and accompany nuclear TDP-43 depletion and cell death without conventional stress granules [S3]. Conversely, stress granules can sequester transport factors and disrupt transport [S4]. These observations justify testing a pore–transport–TDP-43 feedback mechanism, but none establishes NUP98 phosphorylation as its starting point. C9orf72 repeat RNA provides a distinct, genetic-context-specific route to transport dysfunction [S2]; the proposed mechanism should therefore initially be evaluated only in the specified TARDBP context.

Speculative bridges. The hypothesis assumes that mutant TDP-43 changes an unidentified kinase/phosphatase balance, that Ser606 becomes phosphorylated in these neurons, and that this modification weakens pore association rather than merely marking stress. It further assumes that the resulting import defect contributes causally to TDP-43 redistribution and functional decline. These are experimentally separable links within one causal hypothesis, not established findings. No kinase identity or phosphorylation-dependent interaction is supplied by [S1]–[S4].

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Study several independently derived patient motor-neuron lines alongside isogenic TARDBP-corrected controls. First verify the candidate residue against the expressed NUP98 sequence and establish site-localized phosphorylation with targeted mass spectrometry. Across a longitudinal sampling series, quantify Ser606 occupancy relative to total NUP98, pore-associated NUP98, nuclear-import kinetics using a releasable localization reporter, nuclear/cytoplasmic TDP-43, transport-factor sequestration, conventional stress granules, neuronal firing, and survival. Include passive-permeability, energy-status, and general-stress measurements to distinguish import failure from nonspecific cellular deterioration. The predicted sequence is increased phosphorylation, reduced pore-associated NUP98, impaired import, and then TDP-43 redistribution and dysfunction. Temporal precedence alone would not establish causation.

Test necessity by introducing an endogenous phospho-resistant S606A substitution into mutant and corrected lines, with wild-type sequence-reverted controls. Seek orthogonal confirmation through inducible NUP98-directed phosphatase recruitment, using catalytically inactive and untargeted controls and measuring neighboring phosphorylation sites. Both interventions should preserve pore localization and import before substantially changing cytoplasmic assembly burden, followed by improved neuronal function. Verify that neither intervention independently disrupts baseline transport or NUP98 abundance in corrected neurons. For biochemical sufficiency, compare authentic, site-specifically phosphorylated and unphosphorylated NUP98 in a reconstituted pore-association and transport assay without cytoplasmic assemblies. Its feasibility is an assumption; a phosphomimetic substitution alone would provide only provisional evidence.

Alternatives and falsification. Transport-factor sequestration could initiate failure, with NUP98 phosphorylation occurring afterward [S3, S4]; broader stress could independently cause both changes. Compare phosphorylation-directed interventions with independently validated suppression of conventional stress granules and cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, monitoring intervention effects on phosphorylation and general stress. The proposed ordering predicts an early phosphorylation-dependent defect even when assemblies are suppressed. A decisive falsifying outcome would be verified prevention of Ser606 phosphorylation by two orthogonal interventions, with baseline pore function preserved, yet unchanged timing and magnitude of import failure and neuronal dysfunction. Phosphorylation consistently appearing only after transport failure would also contradict the proposed initiating role.

Limitations. Undetected small assemblies could precede apparent phosphorylation, and phosphatase targeting or amino-acid substitution could have unintended effects. Reconstituted pores cannot establish neuronal sufficiency, and cultured motor neurons may omit aging and non-neuronal influences. Positive findings would support a bounded mechanism in this TARDBP context, not a validated treatment or universal ALS cause.
