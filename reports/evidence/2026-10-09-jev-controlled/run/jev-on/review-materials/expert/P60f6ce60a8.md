# P60f6ce60a8

HYPOTHESIS TITLE: Stress-triggered phosphorylation of Nup98 at mitotic-disassembly-linked sites initiates nucleocytoplasmic transport failure upstream of TDP-43 mislocalization in TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS motor neurons.

MECHANISM AND CAUSAL CHAIN. The proposed initiating event is a pore-intrinsic post-translational modification: in post-mitotic motor neurons exposed to ALS-relevant stress (cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing or repeat-RNA stress), an as-yet-unidentified kinase phosphorylates the nucleoporin Nup98 at residues homologous to those that drive partial nuclear-pore disassembly during mitosis (the existence of such mitotic phosphorylation sites is unverified prior knowledge, not from the packet). The speculative causal chain is: phosphorylated Nup98 weakens pore integrity/barrier function without wholesale disassembly; the Ran gradient and classical NLS-import collapse locally; nuclear TDP-43 is lost; cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulates and acquires pathological phosphorylation within de-mixed assemblies [S3]; these assemblies and stress granules then sequester transport factors, amplifying the defect [S3, S4]. Under this hypothesis, nucleoporin phosphorylation is the initiating lesion, while transport-factor sequestration into stress granules or TDP-43 assemblies is a downstream amplifier, and TDP-43 phosphorylation itself is a consequence, not a trigger.

SUPPORTED PREMISES (from the packet). (1) TDP-43 pathology physically associates with nuclear-pore and transport components, and disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and transport in experimental systems and ALS tissue, motivating a pore-adjacent mechanism [S1]. (2) A genetic ALS context (C9orf72 repeat expansion) shows RanGAP mislocalization and impaired import in models and patient-derived neurons [S2]. (3) Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and drive cell death independent of conventional stress granules [S3]. (4) Stress granules independently sequester transport factors and impair transport, and perturbing granule assembly modifies transport phenotypes [S4]. These premises establish that transport failure is central in ALS and come in at least two flavors—assembly/sequestration-based (S3, S4) versus pore-associated (S1)—but none identifies or supports a nucleoporin phosphorylation event as causal.

SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (explicit assumptions). Bridge 1: a site-specific Nup98 phosphorylation event actually occurs in ALS neurons; the packet provides no evidence for this. Bridge 2: a disease-relevant kinase can phosphorylate mitotic-type Nup98 sites in post-mitotic neurons; candidate identities (e.g., ectopic CDK-like or stress-activated kinase activity) are unverified prior knowledge and should be discovered empirically, not assumed. Bridge 3: phosphorylation temporally precedes import collapse rather than following it; this is the key untested claim.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. (A) Sequestration-only model: transport factors trapped in stress granules [S4] or TDP-43 assemblies [S3] cause transport failure, and any nucleoporin phosphorylation is a secondary stress response. (B) Direct-binding model: insoluble TDP-43 aggregates mechanically associate with and disrupt pore components, with phosphorylation incidental [S1]. (C) C9orf72-specific mechanisms (repeat RNA, dipeptides) impair transport independently of pore modification [S2].

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL TEST STRATEGY. In iPSC-derived motor neurons from a C9orf72 carrier and a TDP-43-mislocalization induction system, with a live NLS-import reporter: (1) Time course—quantify site-specific phospho-Nup98 (phospho-antibodies plus mass spectrometry), RanGAP mislocalization, and import rate at high temporal resolution. The hypothesis predicts phospho-Nup98 appears before or at the earliest import decline, not after. (2) Necessity—CRISPR knock-in of phospho-dead Nup98 (Ser-to-Ala at candidate disassembly-linked sites): protection from import failure, RanGAP mislocalization, nuclear TDP-43 loss, and cell death after cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing induction supports upstream causality. (3) Sufficiency and directionality—inducible phospho-mimetic Nup98 should reproduce import failure and trigger TDP-43 mislocalization without cytoplasmic assemblies; early recruitment of an NPC-targeted phosphatase should partially rescue even under stress, whereas late dephosphorylation should not, separating initiation from downstream amplification. (4) Orthogonal discrimination from the sequestration alternative—in a parallel arm, stress-granule assembly is blocked (G3BP1/2 deletion or ISRIB; unverified prior knowledge that these suppress granules). If granule blockade fully prevents defects and abolishes phospho-Nup98, the sequestration model dominates; if transport defects persist after granule blockade but are rescued by phospho-dead Nup98, the pore-intrinsic model is favored. (5) In vivo—AAV9 motor-neuron expression of phospho-dead Nup98 in ALS mouse models, tracking phospho-occupancy before symptom onset.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION. Phospho-dead knock-in neurons show the same timing and magnitude of import failure, RanGAP mislocalization, and nuclear TDP-43 depletion as wild-type after TDP-43 de-mixing or repeat-RNA stress, and phospho-Nup98 signal arises only after import collapse or alongside cell-death markers. This would recast Nup98 phosphorylation as a consequence and affirm the sequestration-based alternatives [S3, S4].

LIMITATIONS. The packet contains no direct evidence that nucleoporin phosphorylation initiates ALS, so Bridges 1–3 are assumptions; selected studies predate 2025 and do not cover all ALS subtypes, so the hypothesis is restricted to TDP-43-proteinopathy contexts (C9orf72 and TDP-43-mislocalizing sporadic disease), not all ALS. Pore redundancy among nucleoporins, difficulty achieving site-level knock-in in neurons, and unresolved kinase identity limit inferential strength; results must be interpreted perturbationally, not correlationally.
