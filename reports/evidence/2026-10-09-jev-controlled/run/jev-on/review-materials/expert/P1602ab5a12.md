# P1602ab5a12

RESEARCH PROPOSAL

Title: Repurposing teriflunomide (approved for relapsing multiple sclerosis) as a DHODH inhibitor that couples pyrimidine starvation to oxidative-metabolism collapse and myeloid differentiation in cytarabine-residual, OXPHOS-high AML.

Hypothesis and biological context. I propose testing teriflunomide — the active metabolite of the rheumatoid-arthritis drug leflunomide and, per unverified recollection requiring confirmation, an approved oral therapy for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis — as a single-agent dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) inhibitor in AML contexts where oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) is prominent: ROS-low leukemia stem cell (LSC) fractions (S1) and cytarabine-surviving OXPHOS/fatty-acid-oxidation-high residual cells (S2). Mechanistic claim: clinically achievable teriflunomide exposures inhibit DHODH, depleting de novo pyrimidines while collapsing the respiratory-chain-coupled metabolic state these cells share, and thereby force myeloid differentiation or loss of LSC function rather than nonspecific bulk killing.

Supported premises (from the packet). S1: ROS-low LSCs are enriched for BCL-2 and OXPHOS dependency, and metabolic targeting can eliminate them in studied specimens. S2: cytarabine-resistant residual cells are OXPHOS/FAO-high, are not necessarily LSC-enriched, and altering their metabolic state changed response in those models. S4: DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation in experimental AML, linking metabolism to a non-cytotoxic outcome — with the caution that not every approved drug touching this pathway reproduces it. S3: venetoclax–azacitidine disrupts TCA-cycle/OXPHOS in LSCs and is the established mechanistic comparator; teriflunomide must be benchmarked against, not conflated with, that regimen.

Mechanistic bridge. DHODH resides in the inner mitochondrial membrane and transfers electrons to coenzyme Q (general biochemistry; verify independently), so pyrimidine synthesis is coupled to respiratory-chain flux. Supported-by-S4 premise: DHODH inhibition can trigger differentiation. Speculative bridges: (1) teriflunomide free plasma exposure reaches DHODH-inhibitory levels in marrow at approved dosing (PK recollection, needs verification); (2) inhibition drains pyrimidine pools with an antiproliferative, uridine-rescuable component while a separable differentiation component persists (recalled from the brequinar literature, unverified); (3) cytarabine survivors (S2) are at least as dependent on de novo pyrimidines as bulk blasts, which salvage pathways may negate.

Alternative explanations. Observed differentiation could reflect cytotoxic selection of already-mature cells; teriflunomide has reported non-DHODH activities at higher concentrations (e.g., tyrosine-kinase effects, unverified); primary ETC disruption could make this venetoclax-like killing rather than DHODH-specific differentiation; genotypes with high nucleoside salvage may be refractory.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. (1) Ex vivo dose-response in genotypically diverse primary AML (FLT3-ITD, NPM1-mutant, TP53-mutant, MLL-rearranged) versus normal CD34+ HSPCs: CD11b/CD14/CD64 staining, viability, and S1-style CD34+CD38− ROS-low LSC gating. (2) On-target engagement: LC-MS pyrimidine pools, dihydroorotate accumulation, Seahorse OCR; uridine/pyrimidine-nucleoside rescue; DHODH knockout and rescue with an inhibitor-resistant DHODH allele. (3) Residual-disease assay: cytarabine pulse to generate S2-like survivors, then teriflunomide; ask whether survivors are killed or differentiated. (4) Comparator arms: brequinar (experimental reference DHODH inhibitor), venetoclax–azacitidine (S3), vehicle, cytarabine alone. (5) In vivo NSG xenografts of primary AML with LSC frequency by limiting dilution, plus toxicity testing in human CD34+–engrafted mice. (6) Exposure reconciliation: compare ex vivo IC50 with achievable free plasma concentrations from verified pharmacokinetic data.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis fails if (a) teriflunomide at clinically achievable concentrations does not induce differentiation or reduce LSC frequency in the majority of primary specimens; (b) all phenotypes are fully uridine-rescuable, indicating pure antiproliferative cytotoxicity; (c) DHODH genetic manipulation neither recapitulates nor protects the effect, indicating an off-target mechanism; (d) normal HSPCs are more sensitive than blasts without a differentiation readout, indicating no therapeutic window; or (e) required exposures exceed tolerable human levels while brequinar shows the expected on-target profile.

Limitations. Teriflunomide approval status, dosing, long half-life, and safety liabilities (teratogenicity, hepatotoxicity, immunosuppression) are outside the packet and must be verified; ex vivo differentiation assays may not predict marrow behavior; de novo pyrimidine dependence is heterogeneous; and positive preclinical results would not establish clinical efficacy or any advantage over the established regimen in S3.

Verified facts are the packet summaries [S1]-[S4]; all approval, pharmacokinetic, and literature-recall statements above are labeled unverified prior knowledge requiring direct checking.
