# P97cd135acf

Teriflunomide-driven differentiation of uridine-salvage-low, differentiation-arrested AML

Hypothesis. Low functional uridine salvage will predict greater teriflunomide-induced, DHODH-dependent differentiation than high salvage in differentiation-arrested AML, at human-attainable unbound exposure that preserves normal-progenitor function. Cytarabine persistence is a separately tested context, not an assumed cause of salvage deficiency. The intended outcomes remain viable leukemic-cell maturation and durable loss of clonogenic recovery—not nonspecific killing or presumed stem-cell eradication.

Evidence and context correction. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation in experimental AML [S4]. However, neither teriflunomide efficacy nor salvage-based selection follows from that finding. Cytarabine-resistant cells exhibited oxidative-metabolism-associated features, without an established salvage defect [S2]. Therefore, include untreated, de novo salvage-low AML alongside persistent disease. The ROS-low/BCL-2-associated population in [S1] must remain distinct; venetoclax–azacitidine is an established metabolic comparator [S3], not the repurposing discovery.

Novelty and pharmacological uncertainty. This proposes an approved-drug implementation with prospective salvage stratification, not discovery of DHODH-driven AML differentiation [S4]. Unverified prior knowledge: teriflunomide is an oral drug approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis, inhibits DHODH, and is highly protein-bound; DHODH participates in de novo pyrimidine synthesis. Recollections about dedicated clinical DHODH-inhibitor programs and comparative immunosuppressive/off-target liabilities also require verification. Teriflunomide is chosen for its recalled non-AML approval, not demonstrated superiority over purpose-built inhibitors. The speculative bridges are that salvage capacity becomes rate-limiting under marrow conditions and creates a leukemia-versus-normal therapeutic window.

Pilot and locked population definition. Before efficacy testing, screen 12 paired pretreatment/post-cytarabine specimens after cytarabine washout. Prespecify a four-hour [U-13C9,15N2]uridine pulse at 5 µM total uridine, 50% labeled, measuring enrichment-corrected fractional UTP labeling: below 10% defines salvage-low, at least 20% defines salvage-high, and intermediate specimens remain unclassified. These are proposed operational cutoffs, not established biomarkers; report absolute incorporation, UTP pools, viability, cycling, and frequency uncertainty. Five micromolar is an assay setting, not a claimed marrow concentration. Require at least three salvage-low residual donors before expanding that arm. Subsequently use an independent pretreatment/persistent × salvage-low/high design, separating salvage effects from treatment history.

Quantitative exposure gate. Feasibility cannot be inferred from total plasma concentration. Before differentiation testing, lock E in nM: a conservative, independently verified human steady-state unbound trough at approved dosing. Measure C80, the free concentration producing 80% cellular DHODH inhibition, and test 0.1, 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0 times E under measured binding conditions. Advance only if C80 ≤ E, UTP falls at least 30%, and normal-progenitor colony output remains at least 80% of vehicle. Absolute exposure values are unavailable in this packet and must be verified rather than guessed. Repeat under measured marrow-like uridine conditions and compare potency/exposure margins with a verified, available clinical DHODH inhibitor.

Locked differentiation and selectivity endpoints. After seven treatment days, require a 20-percentage-point increase in viable leukemia-genotype-positive CD11b-positive cells coexpressing CD14 or CD15, corroborated by blinded mature morphology. Require at least 50% fewer colonies during a 14-day drug-free assay after washout, seeded with equal viable leukemic inputs. Validate the proposed marker panel independently. Longitudinal single-cell tracking must distinguish maturation from selective survival of pre-existing mature cells. Measure early DHODH activity, pyrimidine pools, ATP, respiration, apoptosis, and viability; compare matched-cycling normal CD34-positive progenitors. Any leukemia-initiating-cell claim requires separate functional transplantation.

Causal discrimination and alternatives. Include vehicle, high-salvage AML, orthogonal DHODH inhibition, genetic DHODH suppression, and venetoclax–azacitidine [S3]. Uridine supplementation should restore nucleotide pools and attenuate maturation; this is a pathway control, not the novel contribution or sufficient proof of direct targeting. A catalytically validated teriflunomide-resistant DHODH construct, if obtainable, provides stronger discrimination. Reversible cytostasis, off-target mitochondrial injury, and selection of mature cells remain alternatives, especially if ATP collapse or apoptosis precedes differentiation. Advance only passing specimens into patient-derived models with measured marrow drug and uridine.

Falsification and limitations. Confirmed engagement and depletion at ≤ E without the specified maturation and durable clonogenic response falsify the proposed relationship in the tested salvage-low context. Equivalent responses in independently validated salvage-high cells undermine salvage-based selection. Failure of the pilot gate closes the persistence extension without disproving the broader context. Unattainable exposure or comparable normal injury rejects the translational window. Marrow compensation, heterogeneous salvage, and systemic immune effects may defeat selectivity; immunodeficient models cannot establish immune safety. Differentiation alone establishes neither clinical benefit nor stem-cell eradication.
