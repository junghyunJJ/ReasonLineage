# P01dbdf1ca1

TITLE: Teriflunomide repurposing — an approved multiple-sclerosis drug used as a DHODH inhibitor to force myeloid differentiation of oxidative-metabolism-dependent AML cells and cytarabine-resistant residual disease.

HYPOTHESIS. Teriflunomide (recalled: approved for relapsing multiple sclerosis; its parent leflunomide approved for rheumatoid arthritis; both facts require verification under packet boundaries) achieves systemic exposure of its active metabolite A77 1726 sufficient to partially inhibit DHODH in AML marrow. Because DHODH activity couples de novo pyrimidine synthesis to the mitochondrial coenzyme-Q/electron-transport node, the drug should simultaneously (a) induce myeloid differentiation of AML blasts, as shown for DHODH inhibition generically in S4, and (b) impair oxidative-metabolism-dependent quiescent leukemia stem cells (S1) and cytarabine-resistant residual cells with oxidative and fatty-acid-oxidation features (S2).

SUPPORTED PREMISES (from the packet). (1) S1: ROS-low, BCL-2-high quiescent LSCs depend on oxidative phosphorylation and are vulnerable when this metabolism is targeted. (2) S2: chemotherapy-surviving AML cells are not necessarily the LSC population, yet require oxidative metabolism; altering that state changes treatment response. (3) S4: DHODH inhibition drives myeloid differentiation, establishing differentiation as a drug-reachable metabolic outcome distinct from nonspecific killing. (4) S3: venetoclax-azacitidine establishes that metabolic perturbation can reach LSC energy metabolism in patients; it is used here only as the mechanistic comparator, since that combination is an established AML regimen and is NOT the repurposing proposal.

SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (labeled assumptions). (i) Teriflunomide/A77 1726 is a clinically used DHODH inhibitor and DHODH donates electrons to coenzyme Q in the inner mitochondrial membrane — both are general biochemistry/pharmacology recalled from outside the packet and must be verified. (ii) Achievable clinical exposures suppress marrow DHODH enough for differentiation without prohibitive host toxicity; the free fraction is uncertain because of recalled high protein binding. (iii) The coenzyme-Q linkage means a "differentiation drug" could hit the OXPHOS-high states of S1/S2; the packet itself does not demonstrate this. (iv) Brequinar, an investigational DHODH inhibitor reportedly tested in AML (recalled, unverified), would be a positive mechanistic control but lacks the approved-drug property.

SELECTIVITY AND TRANSLATIONAL LIMITATIONS. Normal hematopoietic stem/progenitor cells also rely on oxidative metabolism, and S1 explicitly warns that not every mitochondrial inhibitor has a therapeutic window. Sustained DHODH inhibition causes immunosuppression and recalled teratogenic risk, limiting exposure. S4's models are experimental; no clinical efficacy is implied. Pyrimidine-starvation cytotoxicity could masquerade as a differentiation benefit unless proliferation and maturation readouts are separated. APL or TP53-mutant contexts may not obey this logic.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. Effects could be off-target (A77 1726 has recalled, unverified off-target kinase effects); apparent differentiation could reflect selective survival of mature cells rather than induced maturation; any benefit in residual disease could come from generic respiratory impairment unrelated to DHODH, which would look identical with an unrelated complex inhibitor.

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL STRATEGY. Stage 1, on-target engagement: treat primary AML blasts and S2-style cytarabine-residual populations; measure dihydroorotate accumulation and de novo pyrimidine flux by isotope tracing; test uridine rescue of proliferation. Stage 2, outcome discrimination: flow and single-cell CD11b/CD14/CD16 induction, morphology, serial replating, and xenograft limiting-dilution LSC frequency; use uridine to separate the pyrimidine-depletion arm from the differentiation arm. Stage 3, population mapping: assess responses separately in ROS-low/BCL-2-high (S1-like) versus OXPHOS-high residual (S2-like) fractions to test whether the coenzyme-Q bridge is real. Stage 4, selectivity and benchmark: compare normal CD34+ cord-blood/mobilized progenitor colony formation with AML sensitivity, and benchmark LSC depletion against venetoclax-azacitidine in matched PDX models (S3 comparator only).

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION. If teriflunomide at free concentrations matching or exceeding clinically achievable exposure shows clear DHODH engagement (dihydroorotate accumulation, reduced pyrimidine flux) yet induces no maturation markers and does not reduce functional LSC frequency in primary AML or PDX models, and if normal CD34+ progenitors are at least as sensitive as AML blasts (no therapeutic window), the drug–mechanism–context relationship is disconfirmed. Dihydroorotate accumulation without any phenotypic outcome would specifically show that DHODH inhibition is insufficient in this context, breaking the bridge from S4 to teriflunomide.

LIMITATIONS. This proposal is packet-constrained, preclinical, and mechanism-first. All approval status, original indication, exposure, and off-target claims about teriflunomide, leflunomide, and brequinar are unverified prior knowledge requiring explicit checking; the packet provides the AML mechanistic premises only.
