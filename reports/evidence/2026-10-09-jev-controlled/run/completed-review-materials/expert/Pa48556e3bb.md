# Pa48556e3bb

Hypothesis title: Teriflunomide, the active metabolite of rheumatoid-arthritis drug leflunomide, induces uridine-rescuable myeloid differentiation of cytarabine-residual human AML at exposures used in its approved non-AML indications, while normal CD34-positive progenitors retain greater clonogenicity than the leukemic residual cells.

Biological context. The setting is primary human AML after cytarabine, focused on residual blasts with oxidative-metabolism features. The primary endpoint is myeloid differentiation together with reduced leukemic engraftment. Bulk killing, acute loss of oxidative phosphorylation, and clearance of ROS-low leukemia stem cells are recorded separately.

Supported premises. In the models covered by [S2], cytarabine-resistant human AML cells were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells. They showed mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation-related oxidative metabolism, and altering that state changed response in those settings. [S2] does not establish clinical efficacy of a new drug. [S4] shows that DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation in experimental AML and affected disease-related endpoints in the systems tested. [S4] does not show that every approved drug acting on this pathway reproduces the result or achieves selective exposure. [S1] shows that functionally defined leukemia stem cells in the studied specimens often had low reactive oxygen species and high BCL-2, and that BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells. This defines a stem-cell state and a selectivity constraint for mitochondrial disruption. [S3] links venetoclax plus azacitidine, an established AML regimen, to TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation effects in leukemia stem cells. That combination is a comparator. The proposal uses a drug from a different indication and a differentiation endpoint.

Unverified prior knowledge, which needs checking before the experiment is treated as informed: leflunomide is approved for rheumatoid arthritis; teriflunomide, its active metabolite, is approved for multiple sclerosis; teriflunomide inhibits DHODH and de novo pyrimidine synthesis; mitochondrial DHODH passes electrons to coenzyme Q, coupling pyrimidine synthesis to respiration; steady-state plasma levels for approved schedules are published in pharmacology sources. The packet never names these drugs, an AML approval, or an inhibitory concentration in AML.

Speculative bridges. Bridge one: teriflunomide at steady-state exposure from approved non-AML dosing will inhibit DHODH in primary AML and reproduce differentiation reported for experimental DHODH inhibition [S4]. Bridge two: cytarabine-residual cells that maintain oxidative metabolism [S2] will support DHODH turnover because respiration regenerates ubiquinone; that electron-acceptor link is unverified prior knowledge and is not a finding of [S2]. Bridge three: differentiation and loss of engraftment will occur in residual blasts outside the ROS-low, BCL-2-high state in [S1] and will separate from the venetoclax–azacitidine metabolic pattern in [S3]. Bridge four: at that same exposure, normal CD34-positive progenitors will preserve more clonogenic output than leukemic residual cells.

Alternative explanations. Off-target kinase or aryl-hydrocarbon-receptor effects, recorded only as unverified prior knowledge, could drive the phenotype. Pyrimidine depletion could kill cycling bulk blasts and leave both residual oxidative cells and ROS-low stem cells in place. In vivo uridine salvage could erase an on-target effect that appears in low-uridine medium. Shared DHODH dependence in normal progenitors would close the window. Respiratory shutdown without a differentiation program would copy the metabolic vulnerability described across [S1] and [S2].

Discriminating preclinical test. Pair primary AML with normal CD34-positive cells and prepare a cytarabine-residual fraction in culture or a patient-derived xenograft, following the residual-disease logic of [S2]. Apply teriflunomide across concentrations that include verified human steady-state levels. Arms are vehicle, teriflunomide, teriflunomide plus uridine, venetoclax as the [S3] comparator, and DHODH disruption where feasible as an on-target benchmark of the [S4] class. Readouts are myeloid morphology and CD11b, CD14, and CD15; engraftment or serial colony formation by the residual fraction; oxygen-consumption rate relative to the BCL-2 result in [S1]; and normal CD34-positive colony formation. Split samples by ROS level. Record genotype as a covariate, not as a claimed biomarker.

Falsifying observation. The claim is falsified if clinical-range exposure yields no rise in myeloid differentiation and no selective drop in engraftment of cytarabine-residual AML. Failure of uridine to rescue the phenotype places the effect outside DHODH. Equal or greater loss of normal CD34-positive clonogenicity removes selectivity. Death driven by an acute fall in oxygen consumption without differentiation matches BCL-2-linked suppression in [S1] and falsifies the differentiation mechanism.

Limitations. [S4] and [S2] support a differentiation mechanism class and a residual oxidative phenotype only inside the studies summarized. [S1] and [S3] supply the stem-cell and venetoclax comparators that keep this proposal from being restated as an established AML regimen. Drug approval, original indications, plasma levels, AML potency, and off-target binding are unverified prior knowledge. Culture nucleosides can misrepresent patient salvage. Short assays can miss relapse from an unsampled stem-cell fraction. Hepatic and teratogenic risks attributed to this class remain unverified prior knowledge and would still constrain translation after a positive laboratory result.
