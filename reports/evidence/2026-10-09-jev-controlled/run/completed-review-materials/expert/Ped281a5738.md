# Ped281a5738

CDK1-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation initiates nuclear-import failure in sporadic ALS motor neurons

Hypothesis. In C9orf72-expansion-negative, sporadic-ALS-derived motor neurons that initially retain nuclear TDP-43, stress-induced CDK1 phosphorylation of NUP98 reduces its residence at nuclear pores. This intrinsic pore alteration initiates nuclear-import failure, causing nuclear TDP-43 depletion and subsequent neuronal dysfunction. “Initiates” means a proximal trigger of transport failure in this defined context, not the first cause of ALS. Unverified prior knowledge motivates the NUP98/CDK1 nomination; the supplied packet provides no evidence for this pair or a particular phosphorylation site.

Supported premises. TDP-43 pathology can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nucleocytoplasmic transport [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can inhibit import and accompany nuclear TDP-43 depletion and cell death without conventional stress granules [S3]. Separately, stress granules can sequester transport factors and impair transport [S4]. C9orf72-repeat models demonstrate another route involving altered RanGAP localization and impaired import [S2]. These findings establish transport vulnerability and competing causal routes, but none establishes nucleoporin phosphorylation as an initiating ALS mechanism.

Speculative bridges. The proposed chain requires CDK1 activity to increase before import failure, directly phosphorylate pore-associated NUP98, and weaken its pore retention sufficiently to impair transport. It further assumes that resulting nuclear TDP-43 depletion contributes to functional decline, rather than merely marking injury. Later cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies could amplify transport failure through the mechanisms described in [S1] and [S3]. This feedback is compatible with phosphorylation being upstream initially but downstream during later degeneration; temporal ordering alone would not establish causality.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Study motor neurons from several independent sporadic-ALS donors and matched unaffected donors, using within-line isogenic perturbations. Follow initially transport-intact neurons during maturation and a calibrated, transient sublethal stress challenge. Serially measure nuclear-import kinetics, endogenous TDP-43 nuclear-to-cytoplasmic distribution, NUP98 pore residence, conventional stress granules, granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies, transport-factor localization, and later evoked firing and survival. Measure export, passive nuclear permeability, cellular energy status, and general stress responses to distinguish import impairment from nonspecific cellular collapse.

First, use endogenous phosphoproteomics in matched cultures to identify NUP98 sites whose phosphorylation rises before pore displacement and import failure. Test whether acute CDK1 suppression prevents these changes and whether purified CDK1 directly phosphorylates the same sites. Freeze the candidate site set before independent validation; absence of an early, directly CDK1-dependent signal would count against the proposed mechanism rather than justify an unrestricted kinase search. Then introduce an inducible, endogenous-level phospho-resistant NUP98 replacement after neuronal differentiation, alongside matched wild-type replacement and wild-type reversion controls. Confirm equivalent baseline abundance, pore incorporation, transport, and neuronal health. Protection must accompany verified suppression of the nominated phosphorylation, not simply reduced stress or altered differentiation.

Test the competing sequestration explanation factorially by blocking NUP98 phosphorylation with or without suppression of conventional stress-granule assembly. Continue measuring granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies because eliminating conventional granules cannot exclude the route in [S3]. Rescue of early import by phospho-resistant NUP98 at comparable initial stress and assembly burden would favor intrinsic pore alteration. Conversely, rescue confined to preventing factor sequestration would favor that alternative. To test the proposed downstream mediator, an independently validated, near-endogenous nuclear-retention TDP-43 intervention should improve neuronal function without correcting NUP98 phosphorylation; otherwise nuclear depletion remains an associated readout rather than a demonstrated mediator.

Falsification and limitations. The central causal claim would be falsified if blocking the nominated NUP98 phosphorylation before onset, while preserving baseline pore incorporation, leaves early import failure and subsequent nuclear TDP-43 depletion unchanged. Phosphorylation consistently appearing only after import failure would additionally contradict its proposed initiating role. Donor heterogeneity, neuronal immaturity, stress-challenge artifacts, and structural effects of phospho-resistant substitutions limit interpretation. Even a positive result would support this mechanism only in the tested sporadic-ALS context, not establish a universal ALS pathway or a validated treatment.
