# P3ad721d457

CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation as an upstream trigger of nuclear-import failure in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In mature human TARDBP-M337V spinal motor neurons, a sublethal arsenite pulse activates CDK5; NUP98 phosphorylation weakens NUP96 binding, reducing pore residence and dominantly initiating early nuclear-import failure. Nuclear TDP-43 depletion, persistent cytoplasmic assemblies, and impaired firing follow. “Initiating” means a proximal transport defect in this model, not the initiating cause of ALS.

Supported premises and alternatives. TDP-43 pathology can disrupt nucleoporin localization and transport, with related pore abnormalities in ALS tissue [S1]. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can impair import without conventional stress granules [S3], while stress-granule sequestration provides another route [S4]. These establish plausible reverse causality and sequestration alternatives, not the proposed phosphorylation mechanism. C9orf72 repeat-RNA/RanGAP-associated defects concern another genetic context [S2]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 is not evidence of phosphorylated NUP98 [S3].

Selection rationale and assumptions. Unverified prior knowledge suggests that calcium/calpain-mediated p35-to-p25 conversion can activate neuronal CDK5, that NUP98 phosphorylation regulates pore association during mitosis, and that NUP98 associates with NUP96. This motivates testing a neuronal stress kinase against a potentially phosphorylation-sensitive anchor—not assuming mitotic behavior transfers to postmitotic neurons. Arsenite engaging this pathway, CDK5 targeting NUP98, the residues involved, and phosphorylation-dependent NUP96 dissociation remain assumptions.

Preclinical strategy. Use motor neurons from multiple patient-derived induced pluripotent stem-cell lines, with mutation-corrected isogenic controls. Prespecify 100 μM sodium arsenite for 30 minutes followed by washout. Proceed only with two-hour viability >95% and ATP ≥90% of unstressed levels. Sample at 0, 5, 15, 30, 60, and 120 minutes, then 4, 24, and 72 hours. Measure calcium, oxidative stress, p35 cleavage, CDK5 activity, phosphosite occupancy, NUP98–NUP96 binding, pore residence, other nucleoporins, nuclear TDP-43, cytoplasmic assemblies, transport-factor sequestration, firing, and survival. Measure import kinetics with cytoplasm-photoactivated NLS–3×GFP; NLS-free cargo and envelope imaging distinguish import defects from altered permeability or rupture. The initiating sequence requires phosphorylation and binding loss before import decline, preceding TDP-43 assemblies and global pore disruption.

In independent discovery neurons, map stress-responsive NUP98 sites and require cellular CDK5 dependence plus direct phosphorylation by purified CDK5. Lock sites and analysis windows before blinded validation. Test phosphorylation-sensitive NUP96 binding, including phosphatase reversal. Failure to identify an early compatible modification counts against the hypothesis, not grounds for switching targets. Validate endogenous phosphoresistant substitutions against wild-type repair controls with normal baseline expression, pores, and import. Combine with acute CDK5 suppression and active-versus-inactive rescue. CDK5 activation in corrected neurons tests substrate-dependent kinase action, not NUP98-phosphorylation-alone sufficiency.

To test mediation independently, engineer an inducible endogenous NUP98–NUP96 tether with inert-tether controls. Require restored pore residence, normal baseline transport and architecture, and unchanged stress-induced phosphosite occupancy relative to untethered neurons. Import rescue despite persistent phosphorylation would support anchoring loss as the mediator; phosphoresistance alone cannot establish this.

Directly challenge the assembly alternative using a proposed cytoplasm-restricted TDP-43 assembly-blocking intrabody. Before use, require ≥80% reduction of granule-free assemblies without altering baseline total/nuclear TDP-43, import, initial stress, or conventional-granule burden. Cross this intervention with NUP98 protection and conventional-granule suppression. Measure reciprocal effects: assembly blockade preventing NUP98 phosphorylation supports assembly-first causation; persistent phosphorylation and early import failure despite blockade favor the proposed sequence. Merely matching measured sequestration cannot exclude hidden assembly effects.

Decision criteria and falsification. The primary endpoint is mean import rate at 30–120 minutes; meaningful rescue restores ≥50% of the mutant stress-induced deficit. Power validation using discovery variance. For protection-versus-repair comparisons, require 90% confidence intervals for initial calcium, oxidative-stress, and ATP ratios within 0.9–1.1, and pre-import transport-factor sequestration within five percentage points of controls. Verified ≥90% phosphorylation prevention throughout the early window, with the upper 95% confidence bound below 20% import rescue, falsifies the dominant-trigger prediction. Phosphorylation consistently following import failure supports a downstream response. Import rescue without improved firing would break the proposed downstream neuronal link.

Limitations. Site mutations, tethers, intrabodies, and kinase perturbations may have independent effects; failed feasibility or specificity checks leave causal tests unresolved. Temporal precedence alone is insufficient. Positive results would establish neither a universal ALS pathway nor a validated treatment.
