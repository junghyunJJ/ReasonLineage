# P458e3c1155

Teriflunomide-driven differentiation of uridine-salvage-poor, cytarabine-persistent AML

Hypothesis. At clinically attainable unbound exposure, teriflunomide will induce DHODH-dependent pyrimidine depletion and durable myeloid differentiation in cytarabine-persistent AML blasts with low functional uridine-salvage capacity, reducing their clonogenic recovery while relatively sparing normal hematopoietic progenitors. The intended endpoint is differentiation of viable leukemic cells, not merely bulk-cell killing or presumed leukemia-stem-cell eradication.

Supported premises. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation and affected disease-related endpoints in experimental AML systems [S4]. Cytarabine-persistent AML cells exhibited oxidative-metabolism-associated features but were not consistently enriched for immature, quiescent leukemia stem cells [S2]. These findings justify studying residual blasts as a distinct population; they do not establish that oxidative metabolism predicts DHODH sensitivity. Likewise, the ROS-low, BCL-2-associated stem-cell dependency described in [S1] cannot be assigned automatically to these residual cells. Venetoclax–azacitidine provides an established energy-metabolism comparator, not the proposed repurposing discovery [S3].

Unverified prior knowledge and speculative bridges. Teriflunomide’s approval for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis, its DHODH-inhibitory activity, its high protein binding, and DHODH’s role in de novo pyrimidine synthesis are recalled facts requiring independent verification; none is established by this packet. The proposed causal chain is teriflunomide exposure → DHODH inhibition → insufficient pyrimidine production → release of differentiation blockade. The central speculative bridge is that weak utilization of extracellular uridine prevents metabolic compensation in a subset of residual AML. A second necessary assumption is that this dependency permits a differentiation-producing exposure below the threshold that damages normal progenitors. Neither selective exposure nor this residual-disease vulnerability follows directly from [S4].

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Obtain paired pretreatment and cytarabine-persistent primary AML specimens where possible, alongside normal CD34-positive progenitors. After cytarabine washout, prospectively stratify specimens by isotope-measured extracellular-uridine incorporation into pyrimidines, using a prespecified salvage classification independent of drug response. Measure oxidative metabolism separately rather than using it as a surrogate for salvage capacity. Culture all groups under matched, measured uridine and protein-binding conditions, including marrow-like conditions. Establish a concentration range bounded by independently verified human unbound exposure, and measure free drug rather than relying on nominal concentrations.

Measure early DHODH engagement, pyrimidine pools, respiration, ATP, and viability before evaluating later differentiation. Require concordant morphology, maturation-associated immunophenotypes, and persistent loss of clonogenic recovery after washout. Confirm that differentiated cells retain the leukemia genotype. Longitudinal single-cell tracking should distinguish actual maturation from preferential death of immature cells and survival of an already differentiated minority. Compare normal-progenitor colony formation under matched proliferative conditions. Any leukemia-initiating-cell endpoint should be assessed separately through functional transplantation, not inferred from blast maturation.

Mechanistic discrimination and alternatives. Include vehicle, an orthogonal DHODH inhibitor, and venetoclax–azacitidine as a distinct established metabolic comparator [S3]. Genetic DHODH suppression should phenocopy the relevant biochemical and differentiation responses. Uridine supplementation should restore pyrimidine pools and attenuate differentiation; a biochemically validated, catalytically active teriflunomide-resistant DHODH rescue construct, if obtainable, would provide stronger target-specific discrimination. Uridine rescue alone would not prove direct drug–target causality. Competing explanations include reversible cytostasis, nonspecific mitochondrial injury, off-target toxicity, and selection of pre-existing mature cells. These alternatives become more likely if ATP collapse or apoptosis precedes maturation, or if target-specific rescue fails. Advance only exposure-feasible, selective responses into patient-derived models with measured marrow drug and uridine levels.

Falsification and limitations. The hypothesis is falsified if salvage-low residual AML shows confirmed DHODH engagement and pyrimidine depletion at attainable free exposure but no genotype-confirmed differentiation or durable reduction in clonogenic recovery. Comparable normal-progenitor injury or activity confined to unattainable exposure would reject its proposed selective translational window. Physiological uridine availability, marrow interactions, heterogeneous salvage capacity, and differences between experimental and human exposure may eliminate efficacy. Even convincing differentiation would not establish clinical benefit or eradication of leukemia stem cells.
