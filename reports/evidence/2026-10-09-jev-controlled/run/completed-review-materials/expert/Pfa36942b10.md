# Pfa36942b10

Hypothesis: Nup153 FG-domain phosphorylation as a required pore-level mediator linking TDP-43 cytoplasmic de-mixing to nucleocytoplasmic transport failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy-associated ALS

MECHANISM AND SUPPORTED PREMISES

Supported premises from the evidence packet:
1. TDP-43 pathology is associated with nucleoporin mislocalization and disrupted nucleocytoplasmic transport in ALS tissue and experimental systems [S1].
2. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies form independently of stress granules and impair nuclear import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and cause cell death [S3].
3. Stress granule assembly sequesters transport factors and impairs transport through a phosphorylation-independent sequestration mechanism [S4], representing the primary alternative.
4. C9orf72 repeat expansion causes transport defects through repeat-RNA-associated mechanisms [S2], establishing that ALS transport failure is context-dependent; my hypothesis does not extrapolate to C9orf72 ALS.

Causal chain proposed:
1. Initiating event (supported by S3): Cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing occurs in susceptible motor neurons, generating persistent cytoplasmic assemblies.
2. Speculative bridge 1: These TDP-43 assemblies or the cellular stress they generate activate or recruit a kinase to the nuclear pore, or suppress a pore-associated phosphatase. Kinase identity is an assumption; candidates such as MAPK-family kinases are unverified prior knowledge outside this packet.
3. Speculative bridge 2 (core hypothesis): The kinase phosphorylates the nuclear-basket nucleoporin Nup153 at one or more serine/threonine residues in its FG-repeat or adjacent linker region. This phosphorylation destabilizes Nup153's docked conformation or weakens its interaction with importin-β/Ran, functionally impairing the pore barrier even for Nup153 copies not physically sequestered by TDP-43 aggregates.
4. Speculative bridge 3: Phosphorylation-mediated pore dysfunction is the required proximal cause of transport failure—without it, sequestration alone would yield only partial impairment because unphosphorylated Nup153 copies can compensate. Phosphorylation amplifies the defect to a threshold that causes nuclear TDP-43 depletion, creating a feed-forward loop: transport failure → reduced nuclear TDP-43 re-import → more cytoplasmic TDP-43 → more de-mixing.
5. Downstream: Sustained nuclear TDP-43 depletion and transport collapse impair RNA processing, driving progressive motor neuron dysfunction and death.

DISTINGUISHING INITIATING FROM DOWNSTREAM

The initiating mechanism is TDP-43 cytoplasmic de-mixing (supported by S3). Nup153 phosphorylation is hypothesized as the pore-level proximal cause of transport failure—not merely a downstream stress response. This is distinct from: (a) pure sequestration without phosphorylation being sufficient [S4 model], or (b) phosphorylation occurring as a passive consequence of transport failure or generalized stress without causal role.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION

Stress granules and TDP-43 aggregates physically sequester Nup153 and other transport factors, causing transport failure purely through stoichiometric depletion, with any observed nucleoporin phosphorylation representing a non-causal stress-associated bystander [consistent with S4's framework]. In this alternative, preventing Nup153 phosphorylation does not rescue transport.

EXPLICITLY SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (assumptions, not supported by supplied sources)
- That Nup153 is phosphorylated at its FG/linker domain in ALS motor neurons.
- That this phosphorylation is necessary for the transport defect (not merely correlated with stress).
- That a specific kinase is recruited or activated by TDP-43 de-mixing.
- That phosphorylation is sufficient to impair transport in the absence of TDP-43 aggregates.
- General plausibility: nucleoporin phosphorylation is known to regulate NPC assembly/disassembly during mitosis (unverified prior knowledge recalled from outside this packet).

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL TEST STRATEGY

Step 1: Establish inducible TDP-43 de-mixing in human iPSC-derived motor neurons (e.g., optogenetic TDP-43 clustering or inducible cytoplasmic TDP-43).

Step 2: Map phosphorylation sites by phosphoproteomic mass spectrometry on immunoprecipitated Nup153 ± TDP-43 aggregate induction.

Step 3 (core discriminating experiment):
- Necessity test: Knock in phospho-null Nup153 (S/T→A at mapped sites) replacing endogenous Nup153 in TDP-43 aggregate-inducing neurons. Prediction (if hypothesis is correct): nuclear import (NLS-GFP flux) and nuclear TDP-43 retention are preserved despite TDP-43 aggregate presence. If phospho-null Nup153 does not rescue transport beyond wild-type, the phosphorylation-mediator hypothesis is disfavored.
- Sufficiency test: Express phospho-mimetic Nup153 (S/T→D/E at mapped sites) in healthy neurons without TDP-43 pathology. Prediction (if hypothesis is correct): transport impairment occurs without TDP-43 aggregates. If phospho-mimetic Nup153 alone does not impair transport, phosphorylation is not sufficient and the hypothesis is incomplete.
- Sequestration control: Test whether phospho-null Nup153 rescues transport in a pure stress-granule induction model [S4 framework]. If phospho-null Nup153 rescues transport in this model too, that distinguishes phosphorylation from sequestration for both contexts; if not, sequestration alone drives stress-granule-mediated defects while phosphorylation is specific to TDP-43 de-mixing.

Step 4 (temporal): Live imaging of phospho-Nup153 signal and NLS-cargo import flux after inducing TDP-43 de-mixing. If phospho-Nup153 signal precedes measurable import decline, it supports an initiating-mediator role. If it lags import decline, it favors a bystander/consequence interpretation.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION

In TDP-43 aggregate-bearing motor neurons with confirmed transport impairment, replacing endogenous Nup153 with a phospho-null variant (properly localized to the nuclear basket at endogenous levels) fails to rescue any measured aspect of nuclear import or nuclear TDP-43 retention, while simultaneously (a) TDP-43 aggregate burden is unchanged and (b) phospho-mimetic Nup153 alone does not impair transport in healthy neurons. Together, these demonstrate that Nup153 phosphorylation is neither necessary nor sufficient for transport failure in this ALS subtype.

LIMITATIONS
- Phospho-null substitutions may alter Nup153 structure independently of removing phosphate groups.
- Other nucleoporins may be simultaneously phosphorylated; rescuing only Nup153 may yield partial effects if multiple pore components require phosphorylation for the full defect.
- This hypothesis is scoped to TDP-43 proteinopathy-associated ALS and does not address C9orf72-associated transport failure [S2], which likely involves repeat-RNA-mediated mechanisms.
- iPSC motor neuron models may not capture chronic aging-associated context of human ALS.
- The responsible kinase may act on additional substrates, limiting inhibitor-based discrimination.
