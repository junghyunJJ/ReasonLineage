# Pa082d918d9

Phosphorylation of FG-nucleoporin NUP98 initiates selective nuclear-import failure before nuclear TDP-43 loss in experimental TDP-43 proteinopathy

In neuron and cell models that express disease-linked TDP-43, phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin NUP98 is hypothesized to be an early intrinsic change in the nuclear pore complex. The phosphorylated pore weakens selective nuclear import. Loss of nuclear TDP-43 and later neuronal dysfunction follow that import failure. NUP98 phosphorylation is hypothesized to be temporally separable from recruitment of transport factors into stress granules. The claim is limited to experimental TDP-43 proteinopathy. C9orf72-linked disease and sporadic ALS are boundary conditions for comparison, and the same sequence is not assumed for every ALS subtype.

Supported premises come only from the packet. Insoluble TDP-43 aggregates associate with nuclear-pore and transport components. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in experimental systems, and related pore pathology is present in ALS tissue [S1]. Those findings support a TDP-43-pore connection. They do not identify a nucleoporin phosphorylation event or place any such event first in the sequence [S1]. Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA alters RanGAP localization and impairs nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons, and manipulations of transport or repeat-RNA-associated mechanisms can modify model phenotypes [S2]. This is a genetic-context-specific route to transport failure and does not establish a nucleoporin phosphorylation mechanism [S2]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, and accompany impaired import, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death [S3]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 accumulates in those assemblies; that modification is on TDP-43 and is not evidence of nucleoporin phosphorylation [S3]. Cellular stress can also sequester transport factors in stress granules and impair transport, and altering stress-granule assembly can modify transport defects and disease-model phenotypes [S4]. Together, [S3] and [S4] document two routes to a similar transport phenotype: granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies and granule-dependent sequestration.

Speculative bridges are explicit assumptions. No supplied source names NUP98, a kinase, or a phospho-site. Unverified prior knowledge, outside this packet, holds that FG-nucleoporins such as NUP98 contribute to the selective barrier and that CDK-family kinases phosphorylate nucleoporins when pores are remodeled in mitosis. The hypothesis assumes that a stress-responsive CDK-family activity places an analogous mark on NUP98 in post-mitotic neurons and that the mark is sufficient to reduce karyopherin-mediated import. The relevant residue is unknown and must be mapped. This bridge is not evidence.

Causal order. The initiating event is increased phosphorylation of NUP98 on assembled pores, preceding measurable loss of nuclear TDP-43. The downstream response is failed selective import, depletion of nuclear TDP-43, growth of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and neuronal dysfunction [S1, S3]. Phosphorylation of TDP-43 within those assemblies is interpreted as a later marker [S3]. Stress-granule sequestration of transport factors [S4] and C9orf72-linked RanGAP mislocalization [S2] remain alternative or context-specific routes and are not required steps in the proposed TDP-43 chain. A further alternative is that the phosphate and the import defect are parallel consequences of broader cellular stress, so the two can correlate while the phosphate is unnecessary for the transport failure.

Discriminating test. In human iPSC-derived neurons with a disease-linked TDP-43 variant, and in an inducible cytoplasmic TDP-43 system aligned with [S3], compare isogenic controls across a time course that starts before nuclear TDP-43 loss. Identify the earliest NUP98 phosphosite by mass spectrometry. At physiological expression, introduce a non-phosphorylatable NUP98 allele and a separate phosphomimetic allele at that site, without altering stress-granule scaffolds. In a crossed arm, prevent stress-granule assembly with a perturbation of the type that modifies transport when sequestration is causal [S4], leaving NUP98 intact. At matched time points, quantify NUP98 phosphorylation, nucleoporin localization, a nuclear-import reporter, nuclear versus cytoplasmic TDP-43, transport-factor occupancy of stress granules, and neuronal survival. Add a C9orf72-repeat neuron arm [S2] solely to ask whether the same NUP98 mark is required outside the TDP-43-variant context. The pore-initiation prediction is that non-phosphorylatable NUP98 preserves import and nuclear TDP-43 while stress granules still form and still contain transport factors, and that phosphomimetic NUP98 impairs import in control neurons before cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies appear. The sequestration prediction is that blocking stress granules restores import whether or not the NUP98 phosphosite is occupied. The broader-stress prediction is that NUP98 phosphorylation and import failure rise together while the non-phosphorylatable allele leaves the import defect intact.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis is false if NUP98 phosphorylation increases only after import failure and nuclear TDP-43 loss, or if non-phosphorylatable NUP98 leaves import failure, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and neuronal dysfunction unchanged while the stress-granule intervention rescues those phenotypes. Failure of phosphomimetic NUP98 to impair import in control neurons at matched expression is a second falsifier.

Limitations. iPSC-neuron timing does not establish the sequence in ALS tissue or in sporadic disease [S1]. Kinase identity and site occupancy stay assumptions until mapping and allele rescue are finished. A phosphomimetic residue copies a regulated phosphate only approximately. The design does not show how pore phosphorylation and stress-granule sequestration would combine in an intact animal, and it does not validate a treatment.
