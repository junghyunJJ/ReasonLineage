# P27dc503a6b

CDK1-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation initiates nuclear-import failure in sporadic ALS motor neurons

Hypothesis. In C9orf72-expansion-negative sporadic-ALS-derived motor neurons initially retaining nuclear TDP-43, aberrant CDK1-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation weakens pore residence, initiating import failure, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and subsequent neuronal dysfunction. Initiation denotes a proximal transport trigger, not the first cause of ALS.

Evidence and target rationale. TDP-43 pathology can disrupt pore-component localization and transport [S1]; persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can inhibit import without conventional stress granules [S3]. Stress-granule sequestration supplies another route [S4], while C9orf72 repeats supply a distinct RanGAP-associated route [S2]. None establishes NUP98 phosphorylation as an ALS initiator. Target-specific precedent—CDK1-mediated NUP98 phosphorylation during mitotic pore remodeling—is unverified prior knowledge, not packet evidence. Extrapolation to postmitotic neurons is the central target-selection weakness. The speculative bridges are neuronal CDK1 activation without mitosis, phosphorylation-dependent pore release, and depletion-driven dysfunction. Relevant CDK1 activity must therefore be demonstrated in noncycling motor neurons, excluding cycling contaminants. Unlike the supplied sequestration routes, this proposal begins with intrinsic pore modification.

Temporal and disease-specific test. Use several independent ALS and unaffected donor lines, with within-line isogenic interventions. Longitudinally follow initially import-intact neurons during unstressed maturation; use a calibrated sublethal challenge only secondarily. Predict ALS enrichment of CDK1 activity, NUP98 phosphorylation, and reduced pore residence before import failure or detectable factor sequestration, at comparable energy and stress status. Measure import kinetics, export, passive permeability, TDP-43 localization, both assembly classes, transport-factor capture, firing, and survival. Matched-culture phosphoproteomics identifies early NUP98 sites; orthogonal neuronal CDK1 activity assays, acute genetic suppression, and purified-kinase assays test dependence and direct phosphorylation. Prespecify sites before independent validation.

Phosphorylation-specific causality. Endogenous-level, post-differentiation phospho-resistant replacement, wild-type replacement, and reversion controls test necessity. However, mutation-induced structural protection or broad CDK1 suppression could mimic rescue. Therefore add reversible recruitment of a phosphatase to endogenous NUP98, with catalytic-dead and untargeted controls, confirming loss of nominated phosphates and auditing collateral pore modifications. Conversely, reversible NUP98-targeted recruitment of active CDK1 tests whether phosphorylation produces pore displacement and import failure without preceding sequestration. Reject constructs that alter baseline transport or broadly change stress signaling; artificial sufficiency alone cannot establish disease relevance. Failure to detect early, directly CDK1-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation counts against this target rather than licensing an unrestricted kinase search.

Pore-residence mediation. Add an orthogonal, inducible pore-retention rescue that anchors phosphorylated wild-type NUP98 without reducing its phosphorylation. Include inert-anchor controls and verify normal baseline pore incorporation and permeability. Rescue of early import with unchanged kinase activity, initial stress, and factor-capture burden would support residence as the mediator; phospho-resistant rescue alone would not.

Causal alternatives. Cross NUP98 interventions with conventional stress-granule suppression and, independently, an inducible assembly-deficient TDP-43 replacement at endogenous levels. Validate preserved basal nuclear localization, RNA function, and import, matched initial stress, and reduced transport-factor capture—not merely fewer puncta. Measure effects on NUP98 phosphorylation rather than assuming independence. Import rescue by NUP98 manipulation despite matched early assembly burden favors intrinsic pore alteration. Rescue by assembly suppression while NUP98 phosphorylation and displacement persist favors sequestration; suppression that prevents phosphorylation instead places assemblies upstream. Continued granule-independent monitoring remains essential because conventional granule removal cannot exclude [S3]. These engineered interventions require validation, not presumed selectivity.

Downstream mediation. A separate near-endogenous nuclear-retention TDP-43 intervention should improve firing or survival while NUP98 phosphorylation and reporter-import defects persist. Such rescue supports nuclear depletion as a functional mediator; without it, depletion remains an associated readout rather than a demonstrated causal link.

Falsification and limitations. Verified blockade of the nominated phosphorylation before onset, with preserved baseline pores, that leaves early import failure and nuclear TDP-43 depletion unchanged falsifies its proposed initiating necessity in the tested context. Phosphorylation consistently appearing after import failure also contradicts initiation. Donor heterogeneity, neuronal immaturity, imperfect manipulation specificity, and artificial tethering limit interpretation. Stress-only effects establish conditional vulnerability, not spontaneous ALS initiation; positive findings would remain restricted to the tested sporadic-ALS context, not validate a treatment.
