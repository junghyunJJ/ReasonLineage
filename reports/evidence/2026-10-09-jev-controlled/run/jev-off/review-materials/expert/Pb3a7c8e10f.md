Teriflunomide repurposing to drive uridine-rescuable differentiation of cytarabine-residual oxidative AML blasts

Hypothesis. In non-APL acute myeloid leukemia, teriflunomide will induce myeloid differentiation and reduce leukemogenic output of cytarabine-residual blasts that retain oxidative metabolism. The proposed mechanism is on-target dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) inhibition with pyrimidine depletion. The intended endpoint is differentiation, kept separate from bulk cytotoxicity and from eradication of ROS-low leukemia stem cells. Teriflunomide is treated here as the active metabolite of leflunomide. Its approval for multiple sclerosis, leflunomide’s approval for rheumatoid arthritis, and assignment of DHODH as the pharmacologic target are unverified prior knowledge and require independent confirmation.

Supported premises. In the specimens studied by Lagadinou and colleagues, functionally defined leukemia stem cells frequently had low reactive oxygen species and elevated BCL-2. BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells, which motivates a search for selective metabolic dependencies. That study leaves the therapeutic window of an arbitrary mitochondrial inhibitor unestablished across AML contexts [S1]. In the cytarabine models of Farge and colleagues, residual cells were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells. Those residual cells displayed oxidative-metabolism-associated mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation features, and altering that state changed treatment response in the settings tested [S2]. These observations keep residual disease biologically distinct from the ROS-low stem-cell population in [S1]. DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation in experimental AML systems and affected disease-related endpoints in the models investigated, linking a metabolic enzyme to a differentiation outcome rather than to nonspecific killing alone [S4]. Venetoclax plus azacitidine is an established comparator that changes leukemia-stem-cell energy metabolism, including TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation effects, in patient-associated observations and experimental follow-up [S3]. That regimen is a mechanistic control. S4 does not establish that every approved drug affecting DHODH reproduces differentiation or achieves suitable exposure and selectivity [S4].

Speculative bridges. The packet does not identify teriflunomide or leflunomide. The first bridge is pharmacological: assumed human DHODH inhibition at exposures used for the approved indication. The second bridge joins [S4] to [S2]. DHODH is mitochondrial and donates electrons to ubiquinone (unverified prior knowledge of enzyme topology). Residual blasts with intact oxidative metabolism [S2] are therefore proposed to remain competent for DHODH-dependent pyrimidine synthesis and susceptible to its blockade, including residual populations that differ from ROS-low, BCL-2-high stem cells [S1]. The third bridge is that a uridine-rescuable maturation program will lower leukemia-initiating activity in that residual context. Genotype or mutation specificity is absent from [S1]–[S4] and is not claimed.

Alternative explanations. Marker shifts could reflect stress or selective loss of immature cells rather than a maturation program. Shared use of the ubiquinone pool could make DHODH blockade phenocopy oxidative-phosphorylation failure [S1], [S3], so energy collapse could mimic differentiation. Fatty-acid-oxidation dependence [S2] could dominate residual-cell survival and leave DHODH irrelevant. Matched normal hematopoietic progenitors could be equally sensitive. Microenvironmental pyrimidine salvage could bypass the enzyme in vivo.

Discriminating preclinical test. Compare primary non-APL AML blasts, including cytarabine-residual samples where available, after separation into ROS-low and ROS-higher fractions. Arms are teriflunomide, a tool DHODH inhibitor of the class studied in [S4], venetoclax as the BCL-2 and oxidative-phosphorylation comparator [S1], [S3], and vehicle, each with and without exogenous uridine. Score separately myeloid differentiation (morphology and maturation markers such as CD11b and CD14), bulk viability and apoptosis, oxidative phosphorylation and respiratory reserve, and leukemia-initiating or long-term culture-initiating activity. Run the same assays on matched normal CD34-positive hematopoietic cells. Confirm pathway engagement by dihydroorotate accumulation with UDP and UTP depletion. Anchor concentrations to verified human exposure for the approved indication; until those levels are checked, dose remains an assumption.

Falsifying observation. In this residual oxidative-blast context the hypothesis is disconfirmed if uridine restores pyrimidine nucleotides while differentiation and loss of leukemogenic activity persist; if teriflunomide only suppresses respiration and viability in a venetoclax-like pattern [S3] without a uridine-rescuable maturation program [S4]; if pathway engagement and phenotype appear only above verified clinical exposure; or if normal hematopoietic progenitors lose clonogenicity at the same concentrations that differentiate AML blasts.

Limitations. [S1]–[S4] do not demonstrate clinical efficacy of teriflunomide. Approval, indication, protein binding, half-life, and steady-state exposure need verification outside this packet. Cytopenias and hepatotoxicity reported for approved DHODH-inhibitor use are unverified prior knowledge and mark a marrow-selectivity risk. Nucleoside salvage, lineage bias, and genetic heterogeneity may restrict activity. This design does not set a clinical dose, a combination schedule, or a claim of superiority over existing differentiation therapy or venetoclax-based treatment.
