# P64afe918b3

Nup98 phosphorylation initiates selective nuclear-import failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy motor neurons before transport-factor sequestration

This proposal advances one testable hypothesis. In spinal motor neurons with TDP-43 proteinopathy, phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 inside assembled nuclear pore complexes initiates selective nuclear-import failure. Depletion of nuclear TDP-43, growth of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and sequestration of transport factors are downstream responses. The hypothesis applies to TDP-43 proteinopathy and is tested as a separate question in C9orf72-repeat neurons. A single mechanism for all ALS subtypes is outside the claim.

Supported premises are restricted to the packet. Disease-linked TDP-43 associates with insoluble aggregates that contain nuclear-pore and transport components, disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in experimental cell systems, and occurs together with pore pathology in ALS tissue [S1]. Those findings support a connection between TDP-43 pathology and transport disruption. They leave the initiating chemistry unidentified and leave open other subtype sequences [S1]. Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA is linked to altered RanGAP localization and impaired nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons, and manipulations of transport or repeat-RNA-associated mechanisms modify model phenotypes [S2]. This is a genetic-context-specific route. It supplies no direct nucleoporin phosphorylation mechanism and no demonstration that sporadic ALS follows the same route [S2]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, and accompany impaired transport, nuclear TDP-43 loss, and cell death [S3]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 within those assemblies documents a modification of TDP-43, which is separate evidence from nucleoporin phosphorylation [S3]. The perturbations in that study do not establish every physiological ALS trajectory [S3]. Cellular stress recruits transport factors into stress granules and impairs nucleocytoplasmic transport, and altering stress-granule assembly modifies transport defects and disease-model phenotypes [S4]. Placed beside [S3], that sequestration route shows that conventional stress granules are not required for every TDP-43-associated transport defect [S4].

Speculative bridges are stated as assumptions. The modified subunit is Nup98. A proline-directed kinase that becomes active during TDP-43 pathology phosphorylates Nup98. The residues are unknown until they are mapped in the same motor neurons. Phosphorylated Nup98 binds nuclear transport receptors less effectively, and selective NLS-cargo import slows. Unverified prior knowledge from outside the packet is that phosphorylation of FG-nucleoporins can remodel pores during mitosis in non-neuronal cells. Using that analogy for post-mitotic ALS motor neurons is speculative. [S1] through [S4] do not name a kinase, a residue, or a Nup98 interaction.

Alternative explanations are tested alongside the hypothesis. A sequestration explanation holds that stress granules capture transport factors and thereby impair import [S4]. In that account, Nup98 phosphorylation is a coincident stress mark. A de-mixing explanation holds that cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies inhibit import and deplete nuclear TDP-43 without conventional stress granules [S3]. A secondary-injury explanation holds that nucleoporin mislocalization [S1] and RanGAP mislocalization [S2] follow broader cellular stress, so phosphorylation appears after transport has already failed. A genotype-restricted explanation holds that C9orf72 repeat RNA and RanGAP redistribution [S2] impair import through a route that leaves Nup98 unmodified.

The discriminating preclinical test uses order of appearance and reciprocal perturbation in human iPSC-derived spinal motor neurons. Isogenic TDP-43 proteinopathy cultures, C9orf72-repeat cultures, and matched controls are sampled from the earliest inducible window through the start of neuronal death. Measured events are Nup98 phosphorylation on isolated nuclear envelopes, the rate of NLS-reporter import, RanGAP localization, the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio of TDP-43, G3BP1-positive stress granules, and localization of importins and Ran. Candidate residues are mapped by phosphoproteomics in these neurons before genome editing. Phospho-deficient Nup98, altered only at mapped sites, tests necessity inside the disease models. Phospho-mimetic Nup98 introduced into control neurons tests sufficiency without a disease transgene. An orthogonal block of stress-granule assembly tests the sequestration explanation [S4]. Support for initiation requires three observations: phospho-Nup98 appears before NLS-import failure and before G3BP1 granules; phospho-deficient Nup98 preserves NLS import and nuclear TDP-43; and phospho-mimetic Nup98 impairs import in control neurons that still lack stress granules. Support for sequestration is restoration of import by the stress-granule block while Nup98 phosphorylation stays high [S4]. Support for the de-mixing route is import failure that continues to follow cytoplasmic TDP-43 after Nup98 phosphorylation is prevented [S3].

Falsifying observation. In the TDP-43 proteinopathy motor neurons, the hypothesis is false if NLS-import failure and nuclear TDP-43 depletion occur without an increase in phosphorylation at the mapped Nup98 sites. It is also false if a stress-granule block or a cytoplasmic-assembly intervention restores import and nuclear TDP-43 while phospho-deficient Nup98 does not. An effect present only in C9orf72-repeat neurons would limit the claim to that genotype [S2].

Limitations. Kinase identity, residue choice, and selection of Nup98 remain assumptions. Phosphomimetic alleles approximate regulated phosphorylation. iPSC motor neurons omit aging, glia, and neuromuscular circuits. The packet does not validate this mechanism or any treatment.
