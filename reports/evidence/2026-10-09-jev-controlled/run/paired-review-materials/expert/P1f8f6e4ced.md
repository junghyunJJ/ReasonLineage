# P1f8f6e4ced

Nup98 phosphorylation as a downstream amplifier of nuclear-import failure after cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly in ALS/FTD-relevant neurons

The hypothesis is that, in neurons developing ALS/FTD-type cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 on proline-directed sites is a downstream modification of the nuclear pore. That modification amplifies nuclear-import failure, loss of nuclear TDP-43, and cell death. The initiating mechanism in this context is persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing. Phosphorylation of pore-resident Nup98 is a later intrinsic pore change, separable from sequestration of mobile transport factors and from import failure that tracks broad cellular stress.

Premises supported by the packet are as follows. Insoluble TDP-43 aggregates associate with nuclear-pore and transport components. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nucleocytoplasmic transport in experimental cells, and related pore pathology is present in ALS tissue [S1]. This supports a TDP-43-to-transport inquiry in ALS/FTD. It leaves nucleoporin phosphorylation unidentified and does not set one sequence for every ALS subtype [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form without conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport proteins, and accompany import failure, nuclear TDP-43 loss, and cell death [S3]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 in those assemblies is a modification of TDP-43 and supplies no evidence of nucleoporin phosphorylation [S3]. Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA offers a separate route through RanGAP mislocalization and impaired nuclear import [S2]. Stress also recruits transport factors into stress granules and impairs transport, and changing stress-granule assembly modifies that phenotype [S4]. Read with [S3], conventional stress granules remain dispensable for some TDP-43-linked transport defects [S4].

Assumptions supply the phosphorylation step. Unverified prior knowledge is that Nup98 is a central-channel FG-nucleoporin and that mitotic proline-directed kinases, including CDK1, phosphorylate FG-nucleoporins and loosen selective gating during nuclear-envelope breakdown. The proposed bridge is that a limited form of this mark appears on NPC-incorporated Nup98 after cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies form in post-mitotic neurons. A second assumption is that those phosphates slow facilitated import enough to restrict TDP-43 re-entry and deepen nuclear depletion. The kinase is assumed to respond to the assemblies or their local stress, a path distinct from repeat RNA in [S2]. None of [S1]-[S4] names the kinase, the sites, or this order.

Alternatives can produce a similar transport phenotype. Transport factors may be sequestered in stress granules [S4] or in TDP-43 assemblies [S3] with no functionally required nucleoporin phosphate. In C9orf72-expansion carriers, RanGAP mislocalization may explain import failure [S2] while any Nup98 phosphate is incidental. Phospho-Nup98 may also be a marker of stress-kinase activity while broader proteotoxic injury is what impairs import.

The discriminating preclinical strategy uses human iPSC-derived motor neurons without a C9orf72 expansion, so the route in [S2] is not the background driver. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing is induced with temporal control. Matched cultures are sampled over time for assemblies, stress-granule markers, nuclear TDP-43, an import reporter, cell death, and pore-bound phospho-Nup98 by NPC mass spectrometry or a validated phospho-site antibody. The predicted order is that assemblies and an early import deficit precede phosphate on NPC-resident Nup98. After assemblies are established, cells receive phospho-deficient Nup98, with serine/threonine-to-alanine substitutions at the assumed proline-directed sites, or acute inhibition of the assumed kinase, while assemblies remain. Recovery of reporter import and nuclear TDP-43, with assemblies and stress granules still present, would assign the sustained defect to pore phosphorylation rather than to sequestration alone [S4]. Blocking stress-granule assembly in parallel tests the alternative in [S4]. Persistence of pore-associated phospho-Nup98 and of the late import deficit in the remaining assemblies would align the modification with the granule-independent route [S3]. Phospho-mimetic Nup98 in neurons without TDP-43 pathology tests pore autonomy: slower import over a short window with little new insoluble TDP-43 fits a downstream amplifier, whereas a rapid aggregation wave would reassign the modification as an initiator.

Falsifying observations are as follows. NPC-incorporated phospho-Nup98 stays undetectable across the interval in which import fails, nuclear TDP-43 falls, and neurons die. The phosphate is recovered only on transport factors inside granules and is absent from the pore. Biochemically confirmed removal of the Nup98 phospho-sites leaves import, nuclear TDP-43, and survival indistinguishable from the pathological baseline while assemblies persist. Appearance of phospho-Nup98 before any TDP-43 redistribution, plus launch of the full assembly and death sequence by phospho-mimetic Nup98 alone, would reclassify the mark as initiating and withdraw the downstream claim.

Limitations are built into the evidence boundary. The nucleoporin, the kinase, and the sites are unverified prior knowledge and lie outside [S1]-[S4]. Phospho-mimetic substitutions only approximate phosphate, and kinase inhibitors alter many substrates. iPSC motor neurons omit long human aging and do not represent every sporadic or familial ALS subtype. Other FG-nucleoporins may compensate, and a misassigned phospho-peptide could imitate an NPC signal. This is an unvalidated hypothesis and test plan.
