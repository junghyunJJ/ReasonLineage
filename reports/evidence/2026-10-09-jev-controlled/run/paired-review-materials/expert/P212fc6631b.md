# P212fc6631b

Hypothesis title: Phosphorylation of NUP98 initiates nuclear-import failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy models of ALS.

This proposal tests one claim: in experimental TDP-43 proteinopathy, phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin NUP98 is an initiating nuclear-pore alteration that impairs selective nuclear import and thereby promotes nuclear TDP-43 depletion and neuronal dysfunction. The initiating event is the covalent pore modification. Nuclear TDP-43 loss, cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly, and cell death are treated as downstream responses. The claim is limited to TDP-43 proteinopathy models and TDP-43-positive ALS tissue, and is not offered as a single route for every genetic or sporadic ALS subtype.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 associates with insoluble nuclear-pore and transport components, disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in cell systems, and accompanies pore pathology in ALS tissue [S1]. These findings support a causal study of pore alteration in TDP-43 proteinopathy. They leave the initiating chemistry and the sequence across ALS subtypes unspecified [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, and coincide with impaired import, loss of nuclear TDP-43, and cell death [S3]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 inside those assemblies is a modification of TDP-43 and is separate from nucleoporin phosphorylation [S3]. Expanded C9orf72 RNA is linked to RanGAP mislocalization and impaired import in that genetic context, and transport-directed manipulations can modify model phenotypes [S2]. Cellular stress can recruit transport factors into stress granules and impair transport, and changing stress-granule assembly can modify those defects [S4]. Together, [S3] and [S4] show that more than one upstream route can produce transport failure.

Speculative bridges. The choice of NUP98, the kinase, and the phospho-acceptor sites are assumptions. Unverified prior knowledge holds that mitotic CDK1 phosphorylation of FG-nucleoporins, including NUP98, loosens the selective barrier during nuclear-envelope breakdown. The bridge assumed here is that a proline-directed kinase, provisionally a CDK-family enzyme, writes an analogous mark on NUP98 in post-mitotic neurons during TDP-43 proteinopathy, and that this mark slows importin-mediated cargo entry before stress granules sequester transport factors and before insoluble TDP-43 accumulates. A further speculative bridge reads the nucleoporin mislocalization in [S1] as possible phospho-dependent release of NUP98 from the pore. No supplied source establishes that kinase, those sites, or that order.

Alternative explanations. Transport factors may be sequestered in stress granules so that import fails while the pore remains covalently intact [S4]. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing may inhibit import directly, placing any pore change downstream [S3]. TDP-43 aggregates may displace nucleoporins, so later phosphorylation would mark damaged pores [S1]. In C9orf72 models, repeat RNA and RanGAP mislocalization may explain import failure without NUP98 phosphorylation [S2]. Import failure may also follow broader energetic or proteotoxic stress.

Discriminating preclinical test. In human iPSC-derived motor neurons with inducible disease-linked TDP-43, and in a matched stress paradigm that assembles stress granules without mutant TDP-43, collect a time series of NPC-enriched NUP98 phosphorylation, a quantitative nuclear-import reporter, G3BP1-positive stress granules, and the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic TDP-43 ratio. The hypothesis predicts a rise in NUP98 phosphorylation before the import reporter declines, including when stress-granule assembly is prevented. Endogenous NUP98 is then replaced, at near-endogenous levels, by a phosphorylation-resistant variant or a phosphorylation-mimetic variant at the candidate proline-directed sites. The hypothesis predicts that the resistant variant preserves import, nuclear TDP-43, and survival when stress granules form or when cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies are already present, whereas the mimetic variant impairs import in otherwise healthy neurons. A crossed arm blocks stress-granule assembly while NUP98 phospho-status is measured independently. The same phosphorylation assay is applied to C9orf72-repeat neurons to test whether a RanGAP-linked import defect [S2] depends on NUP98 phosphorylation.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis is falsified if import failure and neuronal death keep the same timing and severity when NUP98 cannot be phosphorylated at the candidate sites, or if NUP98 phosphorylation is detected only after import has already failed and after nucleoporins are already mislocalized with insoluble TDP-43. It is also falsified if phosphorylation-mimetic NUP98 leaves import intact in healthy neurons, or if blocking stress-granule assembly restores import while NUP98 remains phosphorylated, an outcome that would favor sequestration [S4] in that model.

Limitations. Kinase identity, sites, and the selection of NUP98 lie outside [S1]–[S4]. Phosphomimetic substitutions only approximate phosphate. Phosphorylation of other nucleoporins could still matter if this NUP98-site claim fails. iPSC-neuron timing may differ from human disease chronology. The design tests a research hypothesis and does not validate a treatment.
