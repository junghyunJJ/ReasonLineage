# Pb259086673

Nup98 phosphorylation initiates selective nuclear-import failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy motor neurons before transport-factor sequestration

Nup98 is selected as a candidate FG-nucleoporin; the packet contains no nucleoporin-specific or phosphorylation evidence. In spinal motor neurons with TDP-43 proteinopathy, phosphorylation of this pore-incorporated Nup98 is hypothesized to initiate selective nuclear-import failure, with nuclear TDP-43 depletion, cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and transport-factor sequestration as downstream responses. C9orf72-repeat neurons are a separate test.

Supported premises are limited to the packet. Disease-linked TDP-43 associates with insoluble aggregates containing nuclear-pore and transport components, disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in cell systems, and accompanies pore pathology in ALS tissue; the initiating chemistry remains unidentified [S1]. Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA is linked to altered RanGAP localization and impaired nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons, and transport or repeat-RNA manipulations modify phenotypes; this genotype-specific route supplies no phosphorylation mechanism [S2]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport proteins, and accompany impaired transport, nuclear TDP-43 loss, and cell death; phosphorylated TDP-43 there is a modification of TDP-43 [S3]. Cellular stress recruits transport factors into stress granules and impairs transport, and altering granule assembly modifies phenotypes [S4].

Speculative bridges are assumptions. Unverified prior knowledge holds that Nup98 GLFG repeats bind nuclear transport receptors and that mitotic CDK1 phosphorylates Nup98 at many SP/TP sites, dispersing it and collapsing import and export together. Nup98 is therefore the candidate barrier subunit, with scaffold nucleoporins set aside and Nup62 used as the FG-nucleoporin specificity control. CDK5 is a separate proline-directed candidate assumed for post-mitotic neurons. Reports of Nup98 changes in neurodegeneration lie outside this packet and are unverified prior knowledge. Selectivity assumes limited, substoichiometric phosphorylation at FG-adjacent Nup98 sites that weakens importin-β and transportin binding while Nup98 stays pore-incorporated, so classical NLS and PY-NLS import slow while CRM1 export and passive diffusion continue. High-occupancy phosphorylation that releases Nup98 is the mitotic-like alternative. Testable novelty is the disease-context order and the necessity and sufficiency of limited pore-retained phosphorylation, by mitotic analogy.

Sequestration treats phospho-Nup98 as a coincident stress mark beside granule capture of transport factors [S4]. De-mixing lets cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies inhibit import in the absence of conventional stress granules [S3]. Secondary injury places phosphorylation after transport failure that follows nucleoporin mislocalization [S1] or RanGAP mislocalization [S2]. Genotype restriction confines the import defect to C9orf72 repeat RNA and RanGAP redistribution with unmodified Nup98 [S2]. Mitotic-like collapse removes Nup98 and blocks import and export together.

The discriminating preclinical test orders events and applies reciprocal perturbations in human iPSC-derived spinal motor neurons. Isogenic TDP-43 proteinopathy cultures, C9orf72-repeat cultures, and matched controls are sampled from the earliest inducible window through the start of neuronal death. Each sample measures Nup98 and Nup62 phosphosite occupancy on nuclear envelopes, pore incorporation of Nup98 versus a scaffold nucleoporin, importin-β and transportin binding to those pore FG-nucleoporins, classical NLS import, PY-NLS import, NES export, passive diffusion, RanGAP, importin, and Ran localization, the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic TDP-43 ratio, G3BP1 granules, and granule-independent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. The dysfunction endpoint is survival to that window plus a nuclear TDP-43-dependent RNA-processing readout. Phosphoproteomics maps sites before editing. Phospho-deficient Nup98 tests necessity; wild-type Nup98 re-expression tests site specificity; phospho-mimetic Nup98 in controls tests sufficiency and is rescued by wild-type Nup98; phospho-deficient Nup62 tests subunit specificity. Pore incorporation and site occupancy are verified on every allele. Arms are a stress-granule block [S4], a TDP-43 allele that keeps nuclear function and blocks persistent cytoplasmic assemblies [S3], and acute CDK1 versus CDK5 inhibition read out by epistasis with phospho-deficient Nup98.

Initiation requires earlier pore-retained phospho-Nup98, selectively weaker Nup98-receptor binding, intact export and diffusion, preservation of import, nuclear TDP-43, survival, and the RNA endpoint by phospho-deficient Nup98, with phospho-deficient Nup62 leaving those endpoints impaired, return of the defect with wild-type Nup98, and mimetic-driven import failure in controls that lack granules and cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. A stress-granule block that restores import while phospho-Nup98 persists supports sequestration [S4]. Import failure that still follows cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies after phosphorylation is prevented supports de-mixing [S3]. Export collapse as Nup98 leaves the pore supports mitotic-like disassembly.

Falsifying observation. In TDP-43 proteinopathy motor neurons the hypothesis is false if NLS-import failure and nuclear TDP-43 depletion occur without increased phosphorylation at the mapped Nup98 sites, if import and export fail together because Nup98 has left the pore, or if the stress-granule block or the assembly-blocking TDP-43 allele restores import, nuclear TDP-43, and the neuronal endpoint while phospho-deficient Nup98 does not. An effect confined to C9orf72-repeat neurons would restrict the claim to that genotype [S2].

Limitations. Kinase identity, sites, partial-occupancy selectivity, and the Nup98 choice remain assumptions. Phosphomimetic alleles approximate regulated phosphorylation, and CDK inhibitors can affect related CDKs, so epistasis with the phospho-deficient allele is required. iPSC motor neurons omit aging, glia, and neuromuscular circuits. The packet does not validate this mechanism or any treatment.
