# c002

Hypothesis title: CDK5 phosphorylation of Nup62 initiates nuclear-import failure in TDP-43 proteinopathy

In postmitotic neurons that develop TDP-43 cytoplasmic proteinopathy, phosphorylation of Nup62 by cyclin-dependent kinase 5 (CDK5) is proposed as the initiating nuclear-pore lesion that impairs nuclear import. Nuclear loss of TDP-43, expansion of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and neuronal death are downstream responses. The intended setting is TDP-43 proteinopathy in models of sporadic ALS and TARDBP-linked disease. Kinase identity, modified residues, and a direct CDK5–Nup62 contact are assumptions. Calling Nup62 an FG-nucleoporin, and treating CDK5 as a proline-directed kinase still available in postmitotic neurons, is unverified prior knowledge.

Supported premises. Insoluble TDP-43 associates with nuclear-pore and transport components. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in experimental cells, and related pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. That source leaves nucleoporin phosphorylation unidentified as an initiating cause and leaves the sequence open across ALS subtypes [S1]. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can form independently of conventional stress granules, mislocalize transport-associated proteins, and accompany impaired import, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death [S3]. Phosphorylated TDP-43 inside those assemblies is a modification of TDP-43; the source does not report nucleoporin phosphorylation [S3]. Cellular stress recruits transport factors into stress granules and impairs transport, and altering stress-granule assembly modifies transport defects and model phenotypes [S4]. Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA is linked to RanGAP mislocalization and impaired nuclear import, a genetic-context-specific route [S2]. Read together, [S3] and [S4] leave open more than one route to TDP-43-associated transport failure.

Speculative bridges. The nucleoporin whose localization fails in the setting described by [S1] is assumed to be Nup62, and the kinase is assumed to be CDK5. Neither assignment appears in [S1]–[S4]. CDK5 is assumed to phosphorylate a small set of FG-region serine and threonine residues on Nup62, loosening FG permeability and slowing nuclear re-import of TDP-43 and other NLS cargoes. That modification is assumed to precede stress-granule sequestration of transport factors [S4] and the transport-factor mislocalization that accompanies stress-granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies [S3]. Under the hypothesis, phosphorylated Nup62 is the initiating lesion for import failure, while cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing can still occur as a parallel or later event.

Alternatives. Transport receptors may be sequestered in stress granules, with Nup62 phosphorylation only a late correlate [S4]. Stress-granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies may mislocalize transport factors while the pore remains unmodified [S3]. In C9orf72 disease, RanGAP mislocalization may account for the import defect [S2]. Broader proteostatic stress may collapse transport after nuclear TDP-43 has already been lost.

Discriminating test. Human iPSC-derived motor neurons carry inducible cytoplasmic TDP-43 or wild-type TDP-43. Endogenous NUP62 is edited at the native locus to a phospho-null allele, with the candidate serines and threonines changed to alanine, or to a phosphomimetic allele changed to aspartate. Crossed arms delete G3BP1 to impair conventional stress-granule assembly, following evidence that changing stress-granule assembly modifies transport phenotypes [S4]. After induction, a dense time course records Nup62 phosphorylation by mass spectrometry, nuclear-rim Nup62, the Ran gradient, import of an inert NLS reporter, the nuclear-to-cytoplasmic TDP-43 ratio, stress-granule markers, and neuronal death. The initiating-phosphorylation hypothesis predicts that phospho-null Nup62 preserves NLS import and nuclear TDP-43 and reduces death even while stress granules form, and that phosphomimetic Nup62 impairs import in wild-type TDP-43 neurons before stress granules appear. A sequestration-first account [S4] predicts that G3BP1 deletion restores import on every Nup62 allele and that the phosphomimetic allele is silent when stress granules are absent. An assembly-driven account [S3] predicts import failure whenever cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies are present, including in phospho-null neurons, with Nup62 phosphorylation detected only after nuclear TDP-43 falls.

Falsifying observation. In this model the hypothesis is falsified if candidate-site phosphorylation of Nup62 is absent across the window of import failure, if that phosphorylation appears only after nuclear TDP-43 is lost, or if phospho-null Nup62 fails to preserve NLS import and nuclear TDP-43 in conditions where G3BP1 deletion or clearance of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies preserves them. Preservation of import by phospho-null Nup62 together with unchanged death would falsify the further claim that this pore lesion links import failure to neuronal death.

Limitations. A negative result retires this kinase and these sites and leaves other nucleoporins untested. Phosphomimetic residues only approximate charge and stoichiometry. iPSC motor neurons omit decades of aging. The design leaves C9orf72 repeat models untested and advances no treatment claim. Kinase inhibitors are a weak substitute for the allele edit. Finding phospho-Nup62 in ALS spinal cord would remain associative until the same temporal and epistatic tests separate cause from consequence.

## Public critiques

grok-r0
- The causal claim names CDK5 as the writer kinase, but the factorial test only edits Nup62 sites and the falsifier wrongly treats a null allele result as retiring CDK5. Add a separate CDK5 loss- or gain-of-function arm plus a kinase assay that maps CDK5-dependent sites before import fails, and split site-necessity from kinase-identity in the falsifier.
- The native-locus edit is not executable: FG-region serines and threonines are unnamed, and they are not restricted to the SP/TP motifs required if the kinase is proline-directed CDK5. Pre-register a minimal SP/TP set or an explicit phospho-mapping rule, and tie those residues to a readout that separates facilitated NLS import from passive FG-barrier leak.
- Inducible cytoplasmic TDP-43 starts the experiment at an event the hypothesis calls downstream, so timing cannot show that Nup62 phosphorylation initiates nuclear TDP-43 loss. Run the mass-spectrometry and NLS-reporter series from nuclear TDP-43 under kinase or stress activation that is not an NES-TDP-43 sink, and label the cytoplasmic-induction arm as a mediation test only.

grok-r1
- The titled cause is CDK5 writing phosphate on Nup62, but the FG-region serines and threonines stay unnamed and the only genetic perturbation edits Nup62, so the allele result cannot assign or retire CDK5. Pre-register residues from an in vitro CDK5 assay plus phospho-mass spectrometry, and add a neuron-restricted CDK5 loss or analog-sensitive CDK5 arm performed after cell-cycle exit so mitotic CDK1 use of overlapping sites remains intact.
- S1 supports nucleoporin mislocalization and co-aggregation with insoluble TDP-43, while the causal bridge treats phosphorylation as altered permeability of pore-resident Nup62. Commit the hypothesis to one pore phenotype—rim loss, increased passive leak, or selective NLS slowing with preserved rim—and pair the NLS reporter with rim imaging and a sized passive-diffusion probe.
- The S3/S4 contrast is not yet executable: G3BP1 deletion leaves G3BP2-dependent stress granules, no stressor is timed against TDP-43 induction, and clearance of cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies appears only in the falsifier. Disrupt G3BP1 and G3BP2, impose a defined stress time course, and add an assembly-clearance arm so each alternative yields a distinct observation.

grok-r2
- Candidate FG-region serines and threonines stay unnamed, so the phospho-null edit, the phosphomimetic edit, and the mass-spectrometry endpoint have no concrete molecular target. Name the residues, or one primary site plus a pre-registered selection rule, and list the phospho-peptides the time course must detect.
- The design edits Nup62 and G3BP1 while leaving CDK5 untouched, and the limitations text treats a negative allele result as retiring CDK5. Add an acute selective CDK5 perturbation with a kinase-dead control on the same import time course, and score residue necessity separately from kinase identity.
- The causal chain begins in neurons that already show cytoplasmic TDP-43 proteinopathy and never states what activates CDK5. Specify the CDK5 trigger and the TDP-43 induction method, and require phospho-Nup62 to rise while nuclear TDP-43 and inert NLS-reporter import are still intact so the proposed initiator is temporally separable from later de-mixing and death.
