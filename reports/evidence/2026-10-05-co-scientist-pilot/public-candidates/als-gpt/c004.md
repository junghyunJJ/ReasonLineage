# c004

CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation initiates nuclear-import failure in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In TARDBP-M337V human spinal motor neurons, CDK5-dependent phosphorylation of NUP98 reduces its retention at nuclear pores, initiating impaired nuclear import before detectable cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. Subsequent nuclear TDP-43 depletion and cytoplasmic accumulation amplify transport disruption and contribute to neuronal dysfunction. Selection of this allele, NUP98, and CDK5 draws on unverified prior knowledge, not evidence supplied in the packet. The proposed kinase–substrate relationship, relevant phosphorylation sites, mutation-to-kinase connection, and phosphorylation-dependent loss of pore retention are explicitly speculative bridges.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can accompany transport inhibition, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death without conventional stress granules [S3]. Stress granules can also sequester transport factors and impair transport [S4]. These observations establish plausible connections between TDP-43 pathology and transport dysfunction, but none establishes NUP98 phosphorylation as an initiating event. Phosphorylated TDP-43 in assemblies is not evidence of phosphorylated NUP98 [S3]. C9orf72-associated RanGAP abnormalities provide a distinct genetic route to impaired import [S2], arguing against assuming a universal ALS mechanism.

Proposed causal distinction. The initiating event would be phosphorylation-dependent loss of functional, pore-associated NUP98. TDP-43 redistribution and later sequestration of transport proteins would be downstream amplification. The competing explanation reverses this order: TDP-43 assemblies, stress granules, or broader cellular stress first impair transport, while NUP98 phosphorylation is a secondary injury response. Consequently, phosphorylation preceding visible aggregates is necessary evidence but insufficient by itself: undetected assemblies or shared upstream stress could produce the same temporal pattern.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Use independently derived TARDBP-M337V iPSC spinal motor-neuron cultures with isogenic corrected controls, maintaining endogenous protein expression. In a discovery cohort, quantify NUP98 phosphorylation by targeted enrichment and mass spectrometry across the earliest stages of dysfunction. Concurrently measure nuclear-envelope NUP98 occupancy, live nuclear-import kinetics, passive nuclear permeability, TDP-43 localization and assemblies, stress-granule formation, and viability. Freeze the candidate phosphosite set before testing independent cultures. The predicted sequence is increased phosphorylation, decreased pore occupancy, impaired import, then TDP-43 redistribution and functional decline.

Replace the mapped NUP98 phosphoacceptor residues at the endogenous locus with nonphosphorylatable residues. Include independently edited clones, editing controls, and restoration of the wild-type sequence. Establish equivalent baseline pore localization, import, differentiation, and NUP98 abundance before interpreting protection. Compare early responses before TDP-43 assembly burdens diverge. Blocking the proposed phosphorylation should preserve pore occupancy and import, followed by improved axonal integrity and evoked electrical activity, despite persistence of the initiating TARDBP mutation.

Test direct CDK5 phosphorylation of NUP98 biochemically and compare phosphorylated versus unphosphorylated NUP98 binding to a pore-scaffold preparation. In corrected neurons, compare brief, expression-matched active versus kinase-dead CDK5 perturbations, asking whether import impairment requires the mapped NUP98 sites. Kinase inhibition alone would not establish substrate-specific causality. A factorial comparison with stress-granule suppression would test the sequestration alternative; nongranular TDP-43 assemblies must also be monitored because eliminating conventional granules does not exclude the route described in [S3].

Falsifying observation. If the endogenous phospho-null replacement demonstrably eliminates the candidate NUP98 phosphorylation while preserving baseline pore function, yet mutant neurons develop unchanged early import failure and subsequent dysfunction, the proposed phosphorylation-dependent initiating mechanism is falsified. Phosphorylation appearing only after import failure would independently contradict the claimed temporal order. Preserved import without improved neuronal function would undermine the proposed connection to dysfunction.

Limitations. Undetected assemblies could complicate timing, and phosphosite substitutions may alter NUP98 independently of phosphorylation. Biochemical binding assays do not reproduce an intact neuronal pore, and phosphomimetic substitutions would not substitute for authentic phosphorylation. Differentiated cultures may miss aging-dependent pathology. Conclusions would therefore remain restricted to the tested TARDBP context rather than establishing an ALS-wide cause or validated treatment.
