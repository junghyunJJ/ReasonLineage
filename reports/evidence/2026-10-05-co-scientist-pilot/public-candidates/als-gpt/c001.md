# c001

NUP98 Hyperphosphorylation Initiates Nuclear-Import Failure in TARDBP-Mutant Motor Neurons

Hypothesis. In postmitotic human motor neurons carrying a pathogenic TARDBP allele, aberrant CDK1-dependent phosphorylation of NUP98 reduces its residence at nuclear pores, initiating impaired nuclear import before persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies emerge. Subsequent nuclear TDP-43 depletion promotes neuronal dysfunction and reinforces cytoplasmic assembly formation. Here, “initiating” means upstream of transport failure and persistent assemblies—not the first molecular consequence of the mutation or a universal cause of ALS.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and related pore abnormalities occur in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can recruit or mislocalize transport-associated proteins and accompany import inhibition, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death independently of conventional stress granules [S3]. Cellular stress can also sequester transport factors into stress granules and impair transport [S4]. These findings establish a relevant transport–TDP-43 connection, but none identifies NUP98 phosphorylation as its initiating event. Phosphorylated TDP-43 in assemblies is not evidence of phosphorylated nucleoporins [S3].

Speculative bridges. Selection of NUP98 draws on unverified prior knowledge that it is a phosphorylation-regulated nucleoporin. CDK1 involvement, the relevant NUP98 residues, phosphorylation-dependent loss of pore attachment, and the proposed temporal order are assumptions to test. The central mechanistic bridge is that changing pore-bound NUP98 directly compromises import rather than merely marking existing pore damage. The further bridge from import failure to sustained nuclear TDP-43 depletion and neuronal dysfunction is plausible but not demonstrated for this proposed modification by the packet.

Alternatives. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies could first sequester transport proteins, with NUP98 phosphorylation occurring afterward as a stress response [S1, S3]. Conventional stress granules could instead account for the import defect [S4]. Broader cellular deterioration could independently produce phosphorylation and transport failure. Because granule-independent assemblies are possible [S3], eliminating conventional stress granules alone would not discriminate the proposed mechanism from all sequestration routes.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Study mature motor neurons from multiple independent TARDBP-mutant backgrounds alongside their isogenic corrected controls, without an imposed acute stressor in the primary experiment. Longitudinally measure NUP98 phosphorylation in nuclear-envelope-enriched material, nuclear-rim residence, nuclear-import kinetics, nuclear TDP-43 abundance, cytoplasmic assembly burden, stress granules, and neuronal survival. Measure export, passive permeability, cellular energy status, and general stress alongside import to distinguish a selective transport change from global deterioration. Use a discovery cohort to identify early-rising NUP98 phosphosites; lock the residue set and temporal predictions before testing independent cultures.

Replace endogenous NUP98 with expression-matched wild-type or phospho-resistant versions at the mapped sites, verifying comparable baseline pore assembly and transport. The hypothesis predicts that phosphorylation resistance preserves pore residence and import, then prevents nuclear TDP-43 depletion and later neuronal dysfunction despite the unchanged TARDBP genotype. Orthogonal CDK1 suppression should reduce phosphorylation at those sites, but kinase inhibition alone would be insufficient evidence because other substrates could mediate rescue. Rescue of import before improvement in general stress would favor an intrinsic pore mechanism over secondary deterioration.

For sufficiency, attempt brief, inducible recruitment of active CDK1 to NUP98 in corrected neurons, with catalytically inactive recruitment and phospho-resistant NUP98 controls. Confirm actual site phosphorylation and monitor neighboring pore proteins to detect collateral modification. The predicted sequence is phosphorylation, reduced NUP98 pore residence, impaired import, then nuclear TDP-43 loss. Separately compare stress-granule inhibition and an independently validated cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly-blocking intervention. Preservation of early import by NUP98 phosphorylation resistance before assemblies accumulate would favor the proposed route; elimination of phosphorylation and import failure solely by blocking assemblies would favor sequestration upstream.

Falsification and limitations. Effective prevention of the mapped NUP98 phosphorylation, with normal baseline pore function and adequate measurement precision, that leaves early import failure and subsequent nuclear TDP-43 depletion unchanged would falsify its proposed necessary initiating role in this model. Site substitutions or kinase recruitment may themselves alter pore structure or neighboring substrates, so convergent interventions are essential. Culture maturity and detection limits constrain temporal conclusions. Results would apply initially to TARDBP-mutant motor neurons, not automatically to sporadic ALS or C9orf72 disease, where a distinct repeat-RNA/RanGAP-associated transport route is supported [S2].
