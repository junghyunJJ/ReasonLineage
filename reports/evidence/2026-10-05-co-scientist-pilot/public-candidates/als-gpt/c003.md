# c003

CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation initiates nuclear-import failure in TARDBP-associated ALS

Hypothesis. In mature motor neurons carrying pathogenic TARDBP variants, CDK5-dependent phosphorylation of pore-associated NUP98 is a necessary early mediator of nuclear-import failure. The proposed modification weakens NUP98 retention at nuclear pores, reducing import competence before cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies become detectable. Nuclear TDP-43 depletion then contributes to impaired neuronal firing and reduced survival. Here, “initiating” means upstream of transport failure—not upstream of the inherited genetic alteration. This proposal is restricted to TARDBP-associated ALS and does not presume a universal ALS mechanism.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and relevant pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can also accompany impaired import, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death without conventional stress granules [S3]. Neither observation establishes nucleoporin phosphorylation as the initiating lesion. Stress-granule recruitment of transport factors supplies a competing sequestration mechanism [S4]. C9orf72 repeat-associated RanGAP mislocalization and import defects establish another, genetically contextualized route to transport failure, not evidence for the proposed NUP98 mechanism [S2].

Speculative bridges. NUP98 and CDK5 are nominated experimental candidates; their proposed phosphorylation interaction is an assumption, not a finding supplied by this packet. Phosphorylation-induced NUP98 displacement, its occurrence before TDP-43 assembly, and the causal progression from import impairment to neuronal dysfunction are likewise hypotheses. Phosphorylated TDP-43 in cytoplasmic assemblies must not be treated as evidence of phosphorylated NUP98 [S3]. No particular NUP98 residue is asserted without measurement.

Preclinical strategy. Study several TARDBP-associated ALS donor-derived motor-neuron lines alongside isogenic corrected controls, using independent differentiations and blinded analyses. Avoid imposed global stress in the primary experiment. Across neuronal maturation, measure nuclear-envelope-associated NUP98 phosphorylation by targeted phosphoproteomics, NUP98 pore localization, kinetic nuclear-import reporters, independent export and passive-permeability controls, TDP-43 localization and assembly, stress-granule formation, neuronal firing, and survival. Temporal analysis should determine whether phosphorylation precedes both import failure and detectable assemblies; nuclear-to-cytoplasmic reporter ratios alone would not establish an import defect.

In an independent discovery set, identify reproducible early NUP98 phosphosites whose abundance falls after acute CDK5 suppression. Lock those sites and confirmatory endpoints before testing new cultures. A purified-kinase assay would test whether CDK5 can directly modify the same sites, while active-versus-inactive kinase rescue would strengthen attribution in neurons. Introduce endogenous phosphoresistant substitutions at the locked sites, with matched wild-type editing controls. Verify comparable NUP98 abundance, baseline pore localization, neuronal maturation, and basal transport before interpreting protection. The central prediction is that preventing these modifications preserves import, nuclear TDP-43, and neuronal function despite unchanged TARDBP genotype. CDK5 suppression should provide little additional protection once the relevant NUP98 sites are phosphoresistant.

Discriminating alternatives. Compare the NUP98 intervention factorially with separately validated suppression of conventional stress-granule formation and cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly. Include both because blocking stress granules alone cannot exclude the stress-granule-independent route [S3, S4]. The proposed intrinsic-pore mechanism predicts that NUP98 phosphorylation and import failure can persist in assembly-suppressed neurons, while phosphoresistant NUP98 remains protective. Conversely, protection by assembly suppression without dependence on NUP98 phosphorylation would favor sequestration. Parallel measurements of broad stress responses, energy status, and viability would help identify transport failure secondary to generalized cellular injury.

Falsifying observation. If verified prevention of the nominated NUP98 phosphorylation leaves early import failure and subsequent neuronal dysfunction unchanged, despite normal baseline pore function, while assembly suppression restores transport, the proposed necessary initiating role is falsified. Phosphorylation appearing only after transport failure would also contradict the specified temporal mechanism.

Limitations. Undetected small assemblies could precede measured phosphorylation. CDK5 perturbation may affect unrelated neuronal processes, and phosphoresistant substitutions may alter NUP98 independently of phosphorylation. Temporal precedence, direct modification, site-specific necessity, and neuronal rescue therefore must converge. Results would remain preclinical and context-specific; they would not establish a validated treatment or generalize automatically to sporadic or C9orf72-associated ALS.
