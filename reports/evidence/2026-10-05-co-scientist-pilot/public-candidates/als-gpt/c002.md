# c002

CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation as an initiating nuclear-import defect in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In human motor neurons carrying an ALS-associated TARDBP mutation, excessive CDK5-dependent phosphorylation of NUP98’s FG-repeat region reduces NUP98 retention at nuclear pores, initiating impaired nuclear import before nuclear TDP-43 depletion, persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies, and neuronal dysfunction. This proposes an initiating transport lesion within a defined ALS context—not the original cause of ALS or a universal mechanism across subtypes.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport, and related pore pathology occurs in ALS tissue [S1]. Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can recruit or mislocalize transport-associated proteins alongside import impairment, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death, without requiring conventional stress granules [S3]. Stress granules provide another route by sequestering transport factors [S4]. C9orf72-repeat-associated disruption of RanGAP localization and import establishes a distinct genetic-context-specific transport defect, not evidence for this proposed TARDBP–NUP98 pathway [S2]. None of these sources establishes nucleoporin phosphorylation as an initiating lesion.

Speculative bridges. Unverified prior knowledge motivates choosing NUP98 as an FG-repeat nucleoporin and CDK5 as a neuron-relevant kinase. The packet does not establish their biochemical relationship. Direct CDK5 phosphorylation of NUP98, its disease-associated increase, altered pore retention, and the resulting import defect are explicit assumptions. The proposed downstream progression from impaired import to TDP-43 redistribution and dysfunction is also unproven in this context. Phosphorylation could instead stabilize damaged pores or simply mark cellular stress.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Study independently derived TARDBP-mutant induced-pluripotent-stem-cell motor neurons with isogenic corrected controls, using matched longitudinal cultures before overt degeneration. Measure pore-associated NUP98 phosphorylation by targeted phosphoproteomics, NUP98 pore localization, nuclear-import kinetics with a standardized reporter, nuclear and cytoplasmic TDP-43, conventional stress-granule formation, transport-factor partitioning, axonal integrity, and survival. Import kinetics—not merely a steady-state nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio—should be the primary early endpoint. The predicted order is increased NUP98 phosphorylation, reduced pore residence, impaired import, then TDP-43 redistribution and dysfunction.

Map reproducibly increased phosphorylation sites within the prespecified NUP98 region, and test whether purified CDK5 can phosphorylate those same sites directly. Genetic CDK5 suppression, with rescue by active versus kinase-inactive CDK5, would test kinase dependence. Introduce endogenous nonphosphorylatable substitutions at the mapped sites alongside matched wild-type editing controls; verify comparable NUP98 expression, differentiation, and basal pore function. Protection should preserve early import despite the TARDBP mutation and without reducing upstream stress. For a sufficiency test, inducible CDK5 activation in corrected neurons should reproduce the sequence when NUP98 is phosphorylatable but not in the baseline-functional nonphosphorylatable background. This comparison is essential because CDK5 manipulation alone could affect many other substrates.

Alternatives and discrimination. Transport-factor sequestration by stress granules [S4] or stress-granule-independent TDP-43 assemblies [S3] could cause import failure first, with NUP98 phosphorylation appearing secondarily. Broader stress could independently produce both events. Measure these processes concurrently and perturb conventional stress-granule assembly in parallel. Stress-granule suppression alone cannot exclude the assembly route in S3. The phosphorylation hypothesis specifically requires early import protection by NUP98 phosphosite editing before changes in assembly burden, with comparable upstream stress and viability. Rescue occurring only after reduced sequestration would favor an alternative explanation. Temporal precedence without this site-specific intervention would be insufficient.

Falsifying observation. If baseline-functional NUP98 phosphosite editing abolishes the proposed phosphorylation event but leaves the earliest import defect unchanged under matched stress and viability, the claimed initiating mechanism is falsified—even if later neuronal survival improves.

Limitations. Relevant sites remain unknown, and substitutions may alter NUP98 independently of phosphorylation. Direct biochemical phosphorylation does not establish neuronal causality. Cultured motor-neuron maturation, kinase pleiotropy, and ALS heterogeneity constrain interpretation. Any positive result would establish a restricted preclinical mechanism, not a validated treatment or a general explanation of ALS.
