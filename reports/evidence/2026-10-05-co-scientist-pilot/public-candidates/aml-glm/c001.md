# c001

Title: Repurposing teriflunomide, a multiple-sclerosis-approved DHODH inhibitor associated with pyrimidine-depletion biology, to drive myeloid differentiation in chemotherapy-resistant and leukemia stem cell-enriched AML.

Hypothesis (single sentence): Orally administered teriflunomide — the active metabolite of leflunomide and, to my recollection, approved for relapsing multiple sclerosis (leflunomide for rheumatoid arthritis) — inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH) at clinically achievable exposures and thereby relieves the differentiation blockade in AML bulk cells and depletes functional leukemia stem cell (LSC) activity across two metabolic states: ROS-low, BCL-2-high quiescent LSC fractions [S1] and cytarabine-resistant, oxidative-metabolism-dependent residual cells [S2], with a therapeutic profile distinct from cytotoxic nucleoside analogs.

Supported premises (evidence in packet): (1) DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation and affects disease endpoints in experimental AML systems [S4] — this is the mechanistic core, established with a tool-class inhibitor, not with any marketed drug. (2) Functionally defined quiescent LSCs are frequently ROS-low, BCL-2-high, and OXPHOS- and BCL-2-dependent [S1], motivating non-cytotoxic metabolic targeting. (3) Chemotherapy-resistant residual AML cells are not necessarily LSC-enriched but require oxidative metabolism with mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation features [S2], defining a second, distinct target state. (4) A metabolic combination altered LSC energy metabolism in patients [S3], establishing that such mechanisms were detectable clinically; venetoclax-azacitidine is used here only as a comparator, and is explicitly not re-proposed.

Speculative bridges (explicitly not established): (A) An approved immunology drug can reach marrow-relevant concentrations capable of DHODH inhibition in AML blasts; teriflunomide's human pharmacokinetics (high protein binding, long half-life, enterohepatic recirculation) are recalled, unverified prior knowledge requiring verification. (B) Because DHODH transfers electrons to ubiquinone at the inner mitochondrial membrane, oxidative-state cells [S2] and quiescent ROS-low cells [S1] may differ in DHODH dependence and response; response may correlate with baseline OXPHOS signatures. This is inference, not evidence. (C) Teriflunomide may complement, rather than duplicate, BCL-2- or epigenetic-based regimens; untested here. (D) Preclinical reports of leflunomide/teriflunomide in AML plausibly exist (unverified prior knowledge; must be checked); the claim made here is the exposure-linked, context-specific bridging from [S4]'s biology to a marketed drug, not priority on the pathway itself.

Alternative explanations to control: off-target actions of teriflunomide (recalled kinase effects beyond DHODH — unverified); nonspecific antiproliferative pyrimidine starvation killing bulk cells without differentiation or LSC depletion; murine xenograft pyrimidine-metabolism artifacts; marker shifts that look like differentiation without loss of self-renewal.

Selectivity and translational limitations: normal proliferating hematopoietic progenitors also use de novo pyrimidine synthesis, so granulocyte recovery and marrow tolerance are real risks; DHODH biology also underlies systemic immunosuppression, and hepatic, teratogenic, and neuropathic liabilities assigned to this drug class are recalled and unverified. [S4] explicitly does not guarantee that an approved DHODH-targeting drug reproduces the phenotype at suitable exposures.

Discriminating preclinical test strategy: (1) Ex vivo dose-response in primary AML specimens (de novo and relapsed/refractory, n >= 20) at concentrations bracketing estimated clinically free drug levels; readouts include UMP/dTTP pool metabolomics, CD11b/CD14/CD15 flow cytometry, apoptosis, and morphology. (2) Mechanism discrimination: uridine/nucleoside add-back rescue, DHODH knockdown phenocopy, DHODH-overexpression resistance, run in parallel with the [S4]-style tool inhibitor as a positive control. (3) State discrimination: partition cells as ROS-low/LSC-enriched [S1] versus ROS-high oxidative residual-like [S2]; perform limiting-dilution NSG xenograft assays after ex vivo or PK-matched in vivo teriflunomide exposure to verify LSC depletion rather than bulk-marker change. (4) Comparator and safety arms: venetoclax and venetoclax-azacitidine conditions [S3] to test non-redundancy, plus normal cord-blood/bone marrow CD34+ progenitors in colony and differentiation assays to assess therapeutic window.

Falsifying observation: the hypothesis is disconfirmed if, at clinically achievable unbound concentrations, teriflunomide fails to deplete pyrimidine pools and does not induce CD11b/CD14 differentiation or reduce functional LSC frequency while the tool inhibitor does; or if its effects are not rescued by uridine and are not phenocopied by DHODH knockdown, indicating off-target action; or if it impairs normal CD34+ progenitors as much as AML blasts at exposures achieved in vivo.

Limitations: model-system fidelity, heterogeneous AML contexts, pharmacokinetic assumptions needing verification, and the requirement to check current approval language, safety data, and any existing AML literature on this drug before advancing. This proposal is preclinical and does not constitute a clinical regimen or a re-statement of established AML therapy.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- The pivotal premise — clinically achievable DHODH inhibition in AML blasts — is left unquantified: 'estimated clinically free drug levels' carries no numbers, no drug-exposure bracket, and no DHODH potency value, despite the candidate itself flagging that high protein binding and long half-life are recalled rather than verified; the whole hypothesis could fail on pharmacology alone. Fix: retrieve actual teriflunomide PK (Cmax, >99% protein binding, unbound fraction) and published DHODH IC50 values and state a numeric free-drug-to-IC50 margin before claiming 'clinically achievable exposures.'
- The mechanistic positive control is unnamed ('[S4]-style tool inhibitor' — brequinar is the actual compound) and the LSC partitioning lacks predefined markers or gating (only 'ROS-low' is given), weakening reproducibility of the state-discrimination and falsification tests. Fix: name brequinar, specify numeric exposure brackets, and predefine LSC identifiers (e.g., CD34/CD38/TIM3 plus ROS-low gating) and the limiting-dilution design parameters.
- Novelty is thin and rests on a hedge: extending Sykes 2016 DHODH biology to the only approved DHODH inhibitors (teriflunomide/leflunomide) is a near-obvious next step, and the candidate concedes existing AML work 'plausibly exists' without resolving it. Fix: actually perform the literature check on leflunomide/teriflunomide/brequinar in AML, and reposition the novelty claim on the genuinely new question — whether DHODH dependence tracks the ROS-low/LSC versus OXPHOS-resistant states (bridge B) — rather than on the repurposing pairing itself.

glm-r1
- The single most falsifiable premise — that teriflunomide inhibits DHODH at clinically achievable free concentrations — is never quantified: no Cmax, no free-fraction-derived unbound µM level, no DHODH IC50. Given very high protein binding and low-µM cell-level DHODH potency, sub-inhibitory unbound exposure is the likeliest failure mode, and 'clinically achievable' remains undefined until the flagged PK verification is done. Fix by anchoring the hypothesis to a numeric unbound concentration from the FDA label and enzyme/cell IC50 data before the ex vivo dose-response is designed.
- The hypothesis conflates two mechanisms without a supporting premise: S4 supports relieving a differentiation block in bulk blasts, but neither S1 nor S4 shows that quiescent, BCL-2-dependent ROS-low LSCs (which may not divide or differentiate) can be depleted via DHODH-driven differentiation, and no packet evidence links DHODH dependence to the S2 resistance state beyond bridge B's own admitted inference. Restate as a state-discriminating prediction (bulk differentiates; LSC frequency drops or does not) rather than asserting LSC depletion in both states.
- Novelty is thin at the pathway level: committed DHODH inhibitors (brequinar-class compounds; e.g., BAY 2402234) are already in AML clinical development, and the candidate itself admits existing leflunomide/teriflunomide AML reports 'plausibly exist' without checking. The residual novelty is only the approved-drug/exposure-feasibility angle; a prior-art check should be made mandatory and the novelty claim narrowed accordingly.

glm-r2
- Novelty rests on unexamined prior art: the mechanistic core is S4's established DHODH-differentiation finding with a tool inhibitor, and the candidate itself concedes (bridge D) that leflunomide/teriflunomide AML reports 'plausibly exist' without checking, nor does it address brequinar's prior clinical AML development. Fix: verify and cite or rule out existing DHODH-inhibitor AML data (including any A77 1726/teriflunomide experiments, possibly within S4's own supplementary data, and brequinar trials) before claiming repurposing originality.
- The falsification criterion and bridge A hinge on 'clinically achievable unbound concentrations,' but no numeric exposure anchor is provided and the relevant PK facts (high protein binding, long half-life) are left as recalled prior knowledge; the ex vivo dose-response 'clinically achievable' brackets are therefore unfalsifiable as written. Fix: retrieve verified population PK to estimate the steady-state unbound teriflunomide range and specify the test concentration window explicitly against it.
- Premise-bridge conflation and missing patient context: the single-sentence hypothesis asserts teriflunomide 'depletes functional leukemia stem cell activity' in both the S1 and S2 states, but S4 supports only differentiation in experimental systems — LSC depletion is inference (bridge B), not a premise; and no molecular/genotypic AML context (e.g., NPM1, FLT3, TP53 backgrounds) is specified to define who the claim covers. Fix: demote LSC depletion to a clearly labeled derivative claim in the hypothesis and stratify primary specimens by genotype and metabolic state so the claimed indication is falsifiable per subgroup.
