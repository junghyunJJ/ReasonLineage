# c004

Title: Repurposing teriflunomide (an approved multiple-sclerosis immunomodulator) as a DHODH inhibitor that induces pyrimidine-depletion-driven myeloid differentiation in acute myeloid leukemia.

Verified evidence base (packet). [S4]: DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation in experimental AML and affected disease-related endpoints, connecting metabolism to differentiation rather than nonspecific killing. [S1]: ROS-low, BCL-2-high leukemia stem-cell fractions display oxidative-phosphorylation dependency. [S2]: cytarabine-resistant residual cells rely on oxidative/fatty-acid metabolism but are not simply stem cells. [S3]: venetoclax-azacitidine, an established regimen explicitly not proposed here as any discovery, disrupts stem-cell energy metabolism and serves as a mechanistic comparator.

Unverified prior knowledge (requires confirmation): teriflunomide (Aubagio) is approved for relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis and leflunomide (Arava) for rheumatoid arthritis, converging on the active metabolite teriflunomide; their approved mechanism includes DHODH inhibition that curtails de novo pyrimidine synthesis in proliferating lymphocytes; teriflunomide is highly protein-bound with a long half-life; related DHODH inhibitors such as brequinar have been tested experimentally in leukemia. These are recollections, not packet facts.

Hypothesis: At or below clinically achievable exposures, teriflunomide inhibits DHODH in primary AML cells, depletes pyrimidine pools, and thereby induces granulocytic differentiation with loss of clonogenic/leukemia-initiating capacity, while sparing normal CD34+ progenitors at the same exposures.

Supported premises versus speculative bridges. Premise (S4): pharmacologic DHODH inhibition can overcome the AML differentiation blockade; the packet does not establish that any approved DHODH inhibitor reproduces this. Premise (S1, S2): relapse-relevant AML fractions are metabolically defined, so population coverage must be tested rather than assumed. Speculative bridge 1 (exposure): free/intracellular teriflunomide levels inhibiting AML DHODH sufficiently to change pyrimidine flux; protein binding and potency gaps between approved drugs and the research inhibitors in [S4] could sink the hypothesis. Speculative bridge 2 (biology): de novo pyrimidine dependence should track proliferation; proliferating blasts are predicted to be most sensitive, while quiescent ROS-low stem cells [S1] may partially escape, making differentiation-driven depletion of stem reservoirs proposed but unproven. Speculative bridge 3 (context): marrow uridine/salvage availability may blunt effects in vivo relative to culture.

Competing explanations to discriminate: (1) teriflunomide acts as a nonspecific antimetabolite cytotoxin; (2) DHODH-independent off-target mitochondrial or immune effects explain the phenotype; (3) in vivo benefit reflects microenvironment immunomodulation rather than tumor-cell-autonomous DHODH inhibition; (4) benefit is bulk blast reduction without stem-cell depletion, predicting regrowth.

Discriminating preclinical test strategy: (1) Ex vivo screen of at least 20 primary AML samples plus normal donor CD34+ cells over a clinically relevant exposure range; endpoints: CD11b/CD14/CD15 induction with CD34/CD117 loss (differentiation), Annexin V positivity (kill), and colony/long-term-culture-initiating-cell assays (clonogenic/LSC surrogates). (2) On-target validation: stable-isotope tracing of de novo pyrimidine flux to confirm intracellular DHODH blockade; exogenous uridine should rescue both growth arrest and differentiation if on-target; DHODH overexpression or a resistance-conferring DHODH mutant should confer protection. (3) Population resolution: sort ROS-low versus ROS-high and quiescent fractions [S1] and cytarabine-surviving residual cells [S2]; test each; run a parallel venetoclax-azacitidine comparator arm [S3] to position the metabolic and differentiation signature against an established metabolic benchmark. (4) In vivo: patient-derived xenografts with vehicle, teriflunomide, and exploratory teriflunomide plus cytarabine arms; survival, marrow differentiation, and LSC frequency by limiting dilution, with normal CD34+ colony suppression as the safety readout.

Falsifying outcomes (any one breaks the hypothesis): (a) uridine fails to rescue, indicating off-target cytotoxicity; (b) DHODH overexpression or mutation does not protect cells; (c) no differentiation and no clonogenic/LSC decline at clinically achievable free exposures while normal CD34+ colony formation is comparably suppressed, i.e., no therapeutic window; (d) no survival or LSC-frequency benefit in xenografts at tolerable exposure.

Limitations: approval status, indications, potency, pharmacokinetics, and tolerability statements above are unverified prior knowledge needing explicit checking; teriflunomide's immunosuppression would raise infection risk in cytopenic AML patients, and hepatotoxicity is a plausible concern (unverified); [S4] used potent experimental DHODH inhibitors, so bridges from a chronic oral MS drug to rapid AML differentiation therapy are speculative; genotype and quiescence heterogeneity [S1, S2] may limit breadth; this proposal is not an established AML regimen, and venetoclax-azacitidine [S3] remains the established comparator, not part of the proposed discovery.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- The pivotal exposure bridge is never quantified: 'clinically achievable exposures' is undefined, so falsifier (c) can always be deflected by claiming the tested dose was not 'clinically achievable.' Fix by anchoring even as unverified prior knowledge (e.g., teriflunomide total trough concentration, >99% albumin binding, expected free fraction) and adding a gate-zero measurement of free-drug DHODH IC50 in primary AML cells before any screen; only concentrations matching the derived free range count as testing the hypothesis.
- Novelty and competitive positioning are weak: DHODH inhibition as AML differentiation therapy is an established post-2016 drug-class direction (S4 itself), and purpose-built DHODH inhibitors plus brequinar's historical AML trials exist in prior knowledge that the answer flags but does not integrate. The case currently rests on feasibility rather than advantage. Fix by adding an explicit comparative-advantage argument (e.g., oral outpatient exposure ceiling limiting myelosuppression) and a drop-candidate criterion if teriflunomide potency is orders of magnitude worse than achievable free drug, forcing a jump to leflunomide or better chemical matter.
- The hypothesis bundles two unsupported claims as if co-equal: differentiation-driven LSC depletion (packet S4 shows differentiation and disease endpoints, not LSC depletion) and normal CD34+ sparing (stated inside the hypothesis with no support; DHODH in fact matters in proliferating normal hematopoiesis). Fix by splitting both into independently falsifiable premises with a defined therapeutic-window index (e.g., AML differentiation EC50 vs normal CD34+ cytotoxic EC50 separation threshold) and by adding genotype annotation (NPM1, FLT3, TP53, p53-pathway status) to the 20-sample screen so responder heterogeneity becomes a tested biomarker rather than an unknown.

glm-r1
- The exposure premise is never quantified: 'at or below clinically achievable exposures' is asserted without even labeled-unverified estimates of teriflunomide steady-state total/free plasma levels or its DHODH IC50, so Speculative bridge 1 — the answer's own declared point of fragility — cannot be sized in advance and the screening concentration range risks being mis-set. Fix: add approximate PK values with explicit unverified labels and compare them against reported DHODH potency values for teriflunomide and the research inhibitors used in S4.
- Speculative bridge 2 predicts that quiescent ROS-low LSC fractions may partly escape because pyrimidine dependence tracks proliferation, but this is left unreconciled with S1/S2: DHODH sits at the inner mitochondrial membrane and donates electrons to coenzyme Q, so ETC-coupled OXPHOS-dependent populations should be intrinsically sensitive, pulling the S1-based prediction in the opposite direction. Fix: state the ETC-coupling mechanism explicitly, add a respiration readout (e.g., OCR) plus dual rescue/respiration experiments to resolve which logic dominates in each sorted fraction.
- Novelty is thinly established: the differentiation-via-DHODH mechanism is the packet's own known result (S4), leaving only the approved-drug swap as the new element, and the answer never checks whether teriflunomide or leflunomide has already been proposed or tested in AML (it only flags brequinar generically). Fix: treat a prior-art check for published teriflunomide/leflunomide leukemia studies as explicit unverified work needed, or state clearly that novelty rests solely on the drug-choice-plus-exposure-gap framing.

glm-r2
- Novelty is limited: the proposal is the most direct inference seeded by S4, and DHODH inhibition as AML differentiation therapy is an established development direction (Sykes 2016 itself, plus brequinar-class agents the answer admits have been tested in leukemia); the candidate does not position itself against these known efforts to identify what is genuinely new. Fix: explicitly benchmark the contribution against prior DHODH-in-AML work and claim novelty only for the specific untested elements (approved-drug exposure gap, primary-sample falsification design, LSC-fraction resolution).
- The linchpin exposure bridge is asserted but never quantified: 'at or below clinically achievable exposures' and 'clinically relevant exposure range' appear without any numbers, yet everything hinges on free/intracellular teriflunomide versus DHODH potency, especially given the (flagged) high protein binding. Fix: verify and cite steady-state total and free plasma concentrations plus a DHODH IC50, define the test concentrations from those values, and pre-commit a free-drug Cmax/IC50 ratio threshold that would falsify the hypothesis.
- Falsifier and confounder handling is incomplete: the in vivo arm never tests bridge 3 (marrow uridine/salvage blunting the phenotype), falsifier (a) treats uridine rescue as binary when rescue in primary blasts may be partial, and the hypothesis pre-commits to 'granulocytic' differentiation before data (the marker list includes CD14, consistent with monocytic differentiation as in S4). Fix: measure uridine/salvage-pool availability in the PDX setting, define a partial-rescue criterion, and state the differentiation readout as pan-myeloid marker-based rather than lineage-committed.
