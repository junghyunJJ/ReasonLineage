My hypothesis is that teriflunomide, at concentrations consistent with its approved human dosing, inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase (DHODH), depletes de novo pyrimidine pools, and â because DHODH feeds electrons into the ubiquinone pool of the respiratory chain (standard biochemistry, not from this packet) â modestly reduces electron flux into complex III. Primary endpoint: CD11b/CD14/CD15 differentiation with reduced leukemia stem-cell frequency; secondary endpoint: elimination of cytarabine-resistant residual cells.

2) Supported premises (frozen packet evidence).
- [S4] DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation in experimental AML and affects disease-related endpoints in the studied models. This is the mechanistic core and justifies differentiation (not nonspecific killing) as the primary endpoint.
- [S1] Quiescent, ROS-low leukemia stem cells show BCL-2 elevation and dependence on oxidative phosphorylation, motivating a stem-cell metabolic vulnerability.
- [S2] Cytarabine-resistant residual AML cells are not necessarily enriched for leukemia stem cells but require oxidative metabolism with fatty-acid-oxidation features, defining the resistant-cell context in which the drug is tested.
- [S3] The established venetoclaxâazacitidine regimen's activity links to disruption of TCA/OXPHOS metabolism and provides the in-study mechanistic comparator; that combination itself is not part of the discovery claim.

3) Explicitly speculative bridges (assumptions, not packet evidence).
- Bridge A: teriflunomide achieves on-target DHODH inhibition at marrow-relevant concentrations achievable in humans. This requires new measurement of enzymatic IC50 versus verified human steady-state plasma and surrogate marrow exposure.
- Bridge B: the differentiation benefit demonstrated with potent DHODH tool compounds in [S4] generalizes to the approved, less potent drug.
- Bridge C: partial withdrawal of complex III electron supply meaningfully contributes to fate change in the ROS-low LSC fraction; magnitude is unknown.
- Bridge D (exploratory only): pairing with BCL-2 inhibition could deepen the metabolic effect; treated strictly as a side experiment with venetoclax as comparator in the same assays.

4) Alternative explanations.
Teriflunomide or leflunomide has reported effects on targets other than DHODH (e.g., tyrosine kinases, per unverified prior literature); observed differentiation could be off-target. Generalized pyrimidine starvation might kill blasts without lineage conversion. Alternatively, partial complex III inhibition rather than pyrimidine depletion could be the dominant driver.

5) Selectivity and translational limitations.
Known safety constraints of this class (teratogenicity, long half-life with slow washout, accelerated elimination by cholestyramine) are unverified prior knowledge requiring confirmation and constrain use in fertile adults. Marrow unbound exposure is unmeasurable directly; species and protein-binding differences limit extrapolation. AML molecular heterogeneity (e.g., TP53-mutant disease) and in vitro artifacts may overstate differentiation. Direct prior studies of leflunomide/teriflunomide in AML may already exist; a literature audit is required before any novelty claim, and I explicitly confirm no established AML standard-of-care regimen is being restated here.

6) Discriminating preclinical validation strategy.
Step 1: Determine DHODH enzymatic IC50 and set the target unbound concentration to verified human steady-state values. Step 2: Treat a panel of at least 15 primary AML samples across molecular subgroups versus normal CD34+ hematopoietic progenitors; quantify differentiation markers, apoptosis, and colony formation, including fate tracking of ROS-low LSC-enriched fractions. Step 3: On-target proof â uridine/pyrimidine rescue, DHODH knockdown, rescue with a teriflunomide-resistant DHODH variant, concordance with a brequinar-like tool compound, quantitation of pyrimidine pools and respiratory electron flux. Step 4: Emulate the [S2] residual state by treating blasts after cytarabine exposure, with a venetoclax comparator arm under identical conditions ([S3]). Step 5: PDX pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic in vivo testing; endpoints: blast burden, differentiation index, limiting-dilution LSC frequency, survival, and hematologic toxicity.

Falsifying observations: at concentrations matching clinically achievable exposure, any of the following would refute the drugâmechanismâcontext relationship: (a) no increase in differentiation markers and no reduction in LSC frequency; (b) effects not rescued by uridine and persisting with DHODH overexpression/replacement, indicating off-target action; (c) normal CD34+ CFU-GM toxicity at least as potent as AML toxicity, eliminating the therapeutic window; or (d) no in vivo blast/LSC reduction despite on-target marrow pharmacodynamics.

Limitations: this proposal is model-based and preclinical; exposure, selectivity, and approval details rest on unverified prior knowledge pending verification; AML heterogeneity limits generality; the bridges are assumptions until discriminating assays resolve them.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- Novelty is the weakest dimension and the answer only hedges rather than resolves it: DHODH-inhibition-driven differentiation in AML is already the mechanism of the packet's own comparator (S4) and of clinical-stage DHODH inhibitors, and prior leflunomide/teriflunomide work in AML may exist. Fix: actually perform the stated literature audit before claiming novelty, and if prior art exists, reposition the contribution explicitly as the human-exposure/selectivity advantage of an approved drug rather than a new mechanism.
- Bridge A is the load-bearing assumption but is never sanity-checked: the hypothesis collapses if teriflunomide's unbound steady-state exposure falls below its DHODH IC50, and the packet's S4 caveat explicitly warns that approved drugs may not achieve suitable exposure. Fix: add a preliminary feasibility calculation using known pharmacologic data (steady-state plasma levels, protein binding, published DHODH potency of teriflunomide/leflunomide active metabolite) before the five-step experimental plan, so the hypothesis can be triaged against exposure numbers.
- The falsification logic has a confound: uridine/pyrimidine rescue discriminates pyrimidine-pool-dependent effects, but if Bridge C (partial complex III electron-flux withdrawal) is the dominant driver, uridine-non-rescue would be misread as off-target action (falsifier b). Fix: add a discriminating arm that separates the two mechanisms, e.g., succinate-driven respiration bypassing DHODH/ubiquinone entry, or rescue with an enzyme-competent butelectron-transfer-insensitive DHODH construct, with predefined thresholds for each falsifier rather than qualitative judgments.
