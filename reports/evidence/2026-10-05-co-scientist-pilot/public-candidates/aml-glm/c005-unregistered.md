HYPOTHESIS TITLE: Bedaquiline, an approved multidrug-resistant-tuberculosis drug, collapses ATP-synthase-dependent oxidative phosphorylation to selectively deplete ROS-low leukemia stem cells and cytarabine-resistant OXPHOS-high residual AML cells.

HYPOTHESIS STATEMENT
Bedaquiline (original indication: multidrug-resistant tuberculosis — recalled, unverified prior knowledge; approval program and restrictions must be checked) will directly inhibit mammalian mitochondrial ATP synthase (complex V) at clinically achievable exposure, collapsing ATP-linked respiration in the two OXPHOS-dependent AML populations documented in the packet, reducing functional leukemia stem cell (LSC) frequency, and retaining activity independently of BCL-2 level — making it a candidate complement to, not a substitute for, venetoclax-based regimens, which are comparators here [S3], not a discovery.

SUPPORTED PREMISES (packet)
A [S1]: Quiescent, ROS-low LSCs in studied specimens showed elevated BCL-2 and OXPHOS dependence; BCL-2 inhibition suppressed OXPHOS and targeted quiescent LSCs.
B [S2]: Cytarabine-resistant residual cells were not consistently LSC-enriched yet required oxidative metabolism (mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation-linked) — a second, distinct OXPHOS dependency.
C [S3]: Venetoclax-azacitidine disrupted LSC energy metabolism (TCA/OXPHOS), providing a clinically benchmarkable comparator for OXPHOS disruption.
D [S4]: DHODH inhibition promoted myeloid differentiation, showing that metabolism-targeting can yield differentiation rather than death; S4 is a mechanistic contrast that must be empirically tested, not assumed absent.

SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (each with its discriminating number)
Bridge 1 (linchpin; recalled, unverified): bedaquiline inhibits mycobacterial F1F0 ATP synthase, and some reports recall mammalian ATP-synthase inhibition in a sub-micromolar potency range. Standard TB dosing recalls approximate total peak plasma levels of ~2-4 ug/mL with ~99.9% protein binding, giving rough free Cmax of ~3-7 nM (arithmetic on unverified inputs). The discriminating number to verify first: mammalian ATP-synthase IC50 divided by free Cmax. Engagement at clinical exposure requires IC50 at or below free Cmax; verified IC50 exceeding ~10x free Cmax renders Bridge 1 non-credible without unsafe dose escalation.
Bridge 2 (restated as a formal head-to-head prediction): direct complex-V inhibition should kill ROS-low [S1] and cytarabine-surviving residual cells [S2] regardless of BCL-2 abundance. BCL-2 dependence will be measured, not presumed: BH3 profiling quantifies antiapoptotic reliance, and venetoclax-rescue experiments define whether venetoclax actually fails in S2-type models (no packet source shows it does). The testable claim: bedaquiline retains >=EC50 activity in BCL-2-low/MCL-1-high or venetoclax-resistant models where venetoclax potency tracks BCL-2 dependence.
Bridge 3 (contested prior knowledge): normal quiescent HSCs are recalled to be glycolysis-biased, but complex V is essential in all cells and quiescent HSCs retain OXPHOS capacity, so selectivity is an empirical threshold, not a premise: a pre-specified >=5-fold differential EC50 (AML versus normal donor CD34+ cells) is the go/no-go criterion.

PRIOR-ART CHECK
OXPHOS targeting in AML is a crowded class: venetoclax is established [S3], and recalled, unverified complex-I inhibitor clinical programs reportedly met toxicity limits. The packet cannot rule out prior bedaquiline-in-cancer or AML reports; a novelty scan is part of validation. If prior AML work exists, the genuinely novel claim narrows to the BCL-2-independence/venetoclax-complementarity test, not the drug-mechanism pairing itself.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS
(1) Nonspecific mitochondrial toxicity equally affecting CD34+ cells; (2) cardiac off-target liability (recalled QT prolongation, unverified) rather than complex-V engagement; (3) a DHODH-like differentiation mechanism [S4]. Alternative 3 is now explicitly tested via a differentiation arm so it cannot masquerade as a killing-based positive.

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL TEST STRATEGY
1) Metabolic engagement: dose-response OCR/ECAR and ATP measurements in primary AML sorted ROS-low versus ROS-high and in S2-type cytarabine-surviving residual cells, anchored to a concentration grid spanning ~0.2x-2x verified free-Cmax equivalents, head-to-head with normal donor CD34+ HSCs.
2) Target validation: inhibited ATP hydrolysis/permeabilized-cell ATP measurements in purified or permeabilized systems; binding-competition assays with oligomycin at the canonical F0 site; engineered resistance via the mammalian c-ring subunit ATP5MC1 (the analog of the mycobacterial c-subunit resistance locus; locus naming recalled, verify) in editable AML cell lines; rescue by ATP synthase overexpression. Orthogonal cardiac testing (hERG, cardiomyocyte function) defines the window.
3) Mechanistic discrimination: BH3 profiling plus head-to-head venetoclax dose-response in S2-derived residual and venetoclax-resistant models [S2 vs S3]; a parallel differentiation arm mirroring [S4] — CD11b/CD14 flow cytometry, morphology, and an orthologous DHODH-inhibitor comparator — distinguishes differentiation from OXPHOS-collapse killing.
4) In vivo: cytarabine followed by bedaquiline in PDXs versus a venetoclax-azacitidine benchmark [S3] at PK-verified exposure; residual burden, LSC frequency by limiting dilution, and normal-HSC chimerism. Pre-specified go/no-go: >=5-fold selectivity index, verified IC50/free-Cmax <=1, and significant LSC depletion.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION
The drug-mechanism-context relationship is refuted if any of the following hold: verified mammalian ATP-synthase IC50 >10x free Cmax; no OCR/ATP suppression (or no killing) at <=2x free-Cmax equivalents; AML-versus-CD34+ selectivity <5-fold for bulk, ROS-low, or residual populations; BCL2-profiled killing pattern indistinguishable from venetoclax (no measured complementarity); or no residual-AML efficacy/LSC-frequency reduction at tolerated exposure in PDXs.

LIMITATIONS
All bedaquiline approval, dosing, exposure, protein-binding, ATP-synthase-potency, and cardiac claims are unverified recall requiring confirmation before any program. S1/S2 are model-derived and genotype-restricted; OXPHOS dependency may vary by subtype or prior therapy. Extensive tissue accumulation of bedaquiline (recalled, unverified) may decouple plasma Cmax from true mitochondrial exposure, complicating the selectivity arithmetic. Murine PK and protein binding may not translate. A positive result would position bedaquiline as an investigational complement to clinical comparators [S3], not an established regimen, and would require normal-comparator and cardiac safety evidence.
