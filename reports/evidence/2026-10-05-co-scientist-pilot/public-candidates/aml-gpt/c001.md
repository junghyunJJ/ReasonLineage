# c001

Teriflunomide-induced differentiation in uridine-salvage-limited AML

Hypothesis. In non-APL AML that predominantly uses de novo pyrimidine synthesis and has low baseline uridine-salvage flux, teriflunomide will inhibit DHODH and induce durable leukemic myeloid differentiation at clinically achievable unbound exposure, while relatively sparing normal hematopoietic progenitors. The intended endpoint is maturation with reduced leukemia-propagating capacity, not simply bulk-cell death. Unverified prior knowledge requiring confirmation: teriflunomide is approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis and inhibits DHODH; its reported high protein binding and prolonged persistence also require checking. The packet does not verify these drug-specific facts.

Supported premises. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation and affected disease-related endpoints in experimental AML systems [S4]. This supports investigating the pathway, but not assuming that teriflunomide reproduces those findings. Metabolic vulnerability also does not identify a single universal AML population: S1 links BCL-2 inhibition and oxidative phosphorylation to studied ROS-low leukemia stem cells, whereas cytarabine-resistant populations in S2 were not consistently enriched for such stem cells [S1, S2]. Venetoclax–azacitidine provides an established metabolic and stem-cell comparator, not a new repurposing discovery [S3].

Speculative bridges. The proposed mechanism is that teriflunomide-mediated DHODH inhibition restricts de novo pyrimidine production, and insufficient extracellular-uridine salvage prevents compensation, permitting differentiation rather than only growth arrest. The unproven bridges are that this approved drug reproduces experimental DHODH-inhibitor activity, that pretreatment salvage flux predicts response, and that effective exposure preserves normal hematopoiesis. None is established by the packet. Low salvage must be defined prospectively by isotope tracing, rather than retrospectively labeling responding specimens as dependent.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. First verify the indication, pharmacokinetics, and safety information, and establish an attainable unbound concentration range using measured protein binding. Test primary AML specimens spanning low and high baseline salvage flux, with diagnosis and post-cytarabine specimens analyzed separately. Use human-plasma-like protein and uridine conditions, including marrow-stromal coculture, to avoid creating an artificial nutrient dependency. Compare matched exposures in normal donor CD34-positive progenitors, measuring viability, colony formation, and multilineage output. Prespecify a therapeutic-window criterion and validate any flux–response association in an independent specimen set.

Measure early DHODH pathway engagement through dihydroorotate/orotate changes and pyrimidine-pool depletion, alongside oxygen consumption and apoptosis. Subsequently assess morphology, multiple maturation markers, cell-cycle exit, and persistence after drug washout. Genotype sorted mature cells to establish that maturation occurred within the leukemic clone rather than reflecting selective survival of normal cells. Measure clonogenicity separately from differentiation. For specimens capable of engraftment, transplant equal numbers of viable treated cells after washout to assess leukemia-propagating capacity; marker changes alone must not be called stem-cell eradication.

Mechanistic controls and alternatives. Include vehicle, uridine alone, an independently characterized DHODH inhibitor, inducible DHODH suppression, and venetoclax–azacitidine as the established comparator [S3]. Supraphysiological uridine rescue should restore pyrimidine pools and suppress teriflunomide-induced maturation if pyrimidine depletion is causal; this rescue is distinct from testing efficacy at physiological uridine. Orthogonal DHODH perturbations should reproduce the phenotype. Alternatives include off-target toxicity, nonspecific mitochondrial injury, transient cytostasis, and enrichment of pre-existing mature cells. Temporal measurements, clone tracking, rescue, and orthogonal perturbations distinguish these explanations.

Falsification and limitations. The central prediction is falsified if clinically attainable unbound exposure produces confirmed DHODH inhibition and pyrimidine depletion in prospectively defined low-salvage AML, yet no clone-verified durable differentiation occurs. Comparable normal-progenitor injury would separately invalidate the proposed selectivity; activity confined to unattainable exposure would invalidate its translational premise. Failure of uridine rescue despite restored pyrimidine pools would undermine the proposed causal mechanism. Marrow nutrient availability, stromal compensation, AML heterogeneity, and differences between culture and patient exposure remain substantial limitations. Short-term normal-cell assays cannot establish long-term marrow safety, and approval for another disease does not establish safety or efficacy in AML.
