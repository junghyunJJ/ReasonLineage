# c002

Teriflunomide-induced differentiation in uridine-salvage-limited, cytarabine-persistent AML

Hypothesis. In cytarabine-persistent AML cells with low uridine-salvage capacity, teriflunomide will cause DHODH-dependent pyrimidine depletion, induce durable myeloid differentiation, and reduce leukemia-propagating activity at clinically attainable unbound concentrations. Unverified prior knowledge requiring confirmation: teriflunomide is approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis and inhibits DHODH. Its approval, original indication, target pharmacology, and human exposure limits are not verified by this packet.

Supported premises. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation and affected disease-related endpoints in experimental AML systems [S4]. This supports testing differentiation as an outcome, but does not establish teriflunomide efficacy. Cytarabine-persistent cells exhibited oxidative-metabolism-associated properties without consistent enrichment for immature, quiescent leukemia stem cells [S2]. Persistence therefore provides a relevant metabolic context, not evidence of DHODH dependence. The ROS-low, BCL-2-associated stem-cell population in [S1] must remain distinct from these residual cells. Venetoclax–azacitidine provides an established metabolic comparator, not the proposed repurposing intervention [S3].

Mechanism and speculative bridges. Unverified prior knowledge is that DHODH contributes to de novo pyrimidine synthesis and extracellular uridine salvage can bypass this requirement. The proposed mechanism is DHODH inhibition followed by nucleotide depletion and release of a differentiation blockade, rather than immediate mitochondrial collapse. Three bridges remain explicitly speculative: cytarabine-persistent AML contains a salvage-limited subgroup; that subgroup retains differentiation competence; and tolerated teriflunomide exposure can sustain sufficient target inhibition. Oxidative metabolism alone will not define eligibility, because [S2] does not connect that phenotype specifically to DHODH dependence. Preferential effects on leukemia over normal progenitors are an additional requirement to test, not an assumed consequence of [S1] or [S4].

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Use paired untreated and cytarabine-persistent primary AML cultures, confirming that residual cells belong to the malignant clone. Before teriflunomide treatment, classify uridine salvage by isotope-traced incorporation into pyrimidine nucleotides under independently verified physiological uridine conditions. Include both low- and high-salvage specimens. Match measured unbound drug concentrations to independently verified human pharmacokinetics, with protein binding accounted for rather than relying on nominal culture doses.

Compare vehicle, teriflunomide, an independently validated DHODH inhibitor, and inducible partial DHODH depletion. Include venetoclax–azacitidine as a separate established comparator [S3]. Test healthy donor hematopoietic progenitors under matched proliferating and quiescent conditions, measuring colony formation and lineage output. A time course should establish whether DHODH-associated metabolite changes and pyrimidine depletion precede maturation, apoptosis, or respiratory failure. Combine morphology, differentiation-marker measurements, and single-cell fate tracking to distinguish actual maturation from selective death of immature cells. After washout, assess clonogenic persistence and viable-cell-normalized limiting-dilution transplantation; bulk differentiation alone will not establish loss of leukemia-propagating capacity.

For mechanistic rescue, provide uridine from treatment onset, using conditions demonstrably capable of restoring intracellular pyrimidine pools. Rescue is uninterpretable if salvage remains insufficient. Concordant pharmacological and genetic phenotypes, together with verified pool-restoring rescue, would distinguish the proposed mechanism from off-target toxicity.

Alternatives and falsification. Apparent differentiation could reflect cytostasis, generalized metabolic stress, or preferential killing of immature cells. The central hypothesis is falsified if confirmed DHODH engagement and pyrimidine depletion at achievable exposure fail to produce maturation and reduced post-washout leukemia-propagating activity in the predefined low-salvage subgroup. Persistence of those effects despite prevention of nucleotide depletion would contradict the proposed pyrimidine-mediated mechanism. Comparable injury to normal progenitors would reject the claimed therapeutic opportunity even if the biochemical mechanism holds.

Limitations. Marrow exposure, clinical safety, extracellular uridine availability, and the duration needed for differentiation require verification. Microenvironmental rescue and residual-cell heterogeneity may defeat an ex vivo response. Neither differentiation nor reduced transplantation frequency alone establishes patient benefit, and this proposal makes no claim of first discovery.
