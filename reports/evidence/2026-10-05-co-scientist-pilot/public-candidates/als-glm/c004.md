# c004

HYPOTHESIS TITLE: Stress-activated phosphorylation of the nucleoporin Nup98 as an initiating, pore-intrinsic defect in TDP-43-linked ALS motor-neuron transport failure

HYPOTHESIS AND CAUSAL CHAIN. In ALS motor neurons bearing cytoplasmic TDP-43 pathology, chronic proteostasis stress activates stress-responsive kinases that phosphorylate the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 at sites analogous to its mitotic regulatory phosphorylations. In dividing cells, Nup98 phosphorylation weakens nucleoporin-pore association (unverified prior knowledge, not from the packet); the hypothesis extrapolates that the same modification in postmitotic neurons partially destabilizes nuclear pores, collapsing the Ran-dependent import gradient and impairing nuclear import. The proposed initiating lesion is this pore-intrinsic phosphorylation. The downstream responses are nuclear TDP-43 depletion, cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulation and de-mixing, sequestration of transport factors, and eventually cell death. The chain is thus: TDP-43 stress -> kinase activation -> Nup98 phosphorylation -> leaky pores and import failure -> nuclear TDP-43 loss -> aggregation amplification. In the dominant alternative, the order reverses: condensates initiate the defect and phosphorylation is epiphenomenal.

SUPPORTED PREMISES (evidence). (P1) TDP-43 pathology associates with nuclear-pore and transport components, disrupts nucleoporin localization, and impairs transport in cell systems and ALS tissue [S1]. (P2) Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies form independently of stress granules and drive import inhibition, nuclear TDP-43 loss, and cell death; these assemblies contain phosphorylated TDP-43, demonstrating that kinase activity accompanies TDP-43 de-mixing [S3]. (P3) In C9orf72 repeat-expansion models and patient-derived neurons, RanGAP mislocalization and import defects occur, and transport manipulations modify phenotypes [S2]. (P4) Stress-granule assembly recruits transport factors and disrupts transport; altering granule assembly modifies disease-model phenotypes [S4], establishing a plausible assembly-based route that also serves as the key alternative.

SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (assumptions, not supported by the packet). (B1) The packet documents phosphorylated TDP-43 in assemblies [S3] but no nucleoporin phosphorylation; I assume candidate stress kinases (p38 MAPK, alternatively JNK or aberrant CDK1-like activity; kinase identity is unverified prior knowledge) act on pore substrates under TDP-43 stress. (B2) I assume Nup98 phosphorylation occurs in postmitotic neurons and that, per unverified prior knowledge of mitotic Nup98 behavior, it measurably weakens pore function. (B3) I assume phosphorylation temporally precedes transport-factor sequestration and Ran collapse rather than following them.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS. (A1) Sequestration-initiated failure: TDP-43 de-mixed assemblies [S3] and stress granules [S4] physically sequester importins, Ran machinery, or nucleoporins, with phosphorylation merely reactive. (A2) Physical pore remodeling: TDP-43 aggregates directly bind or distort pore components [S1] without phosphorylation. (A3) Genetic-context specificity: in C9orf72 ALS, repeat-RNA mechanisms drive transport defects independently [S2], so the phosphorylation mechanism may be limited to TDP-43 contexts.

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL TEST STRATEGY. Use human iPSC motor neurons harboring inducible cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing (S3-like) and, separately, C9orf72 expansion lines (S2). Measure time-resolved: (a) pore-associated phospho-Nup98 by phospho-specific antibody staining and targeted phosphoproteomics; (b) live NLS-reporter import kinetics and Ran-gradient integrity; (c) recruitment of transport factors into condensates and stress granules. Apply orthogonal perturbations: (i) Nup98 phospho-null knock-in (mitotic-site Ser/Thr to Ala) or pore-tethered phosphatase recruitment; (ii) candidate kinase inhibition; (iii) matched phosphomimetic Nup98 (Ser to Glu/Asp) expressed at physiological levels; (iv) independent blockade of condensate/granule sequestration. Predictions that discriminate: under the hypothesis, (i) and (ii) preserve import kinetics and nuclear TDP-43 even while condensates still form, and (iii) alone reduces import without condensates; under A1, only (iv) rescues transport and phospho-blocking has no effect; under A2, phospho manipulations fail while preventing aggregate-pore binding rescues. Adding the C9orf72 comparison tests convergence: if Nup98 phospho-block also rescues import there [S2 context], phosphorylation is a shared effector; if not, the mechanism is TDP-43-specific. Temporal ordering is the cause-versus-consequence criterion: phosphorylation detectable before Ran collapse and before transport-factor sequestration supports initiation; phosphorylation appearing only after collapse indicates a downstream response.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION. The hypothesis is falsified if, in the earliest measurable phase of TDP-43 de-mixing, import kinetics decline and transport factors are sequestered with no detectable pore-associated Nup98 phosphorylation at any time point, and if kinase inhibition plus Nup98 phospho-null knock-in both fail to preserve import while condensate/granule disruption fully restores it. A secondary falsifier: physiological-level phosphomimetic Nup98 that leaves import kinetics and Ran gradients unchanged.

LIMITATIONS. The packet contains no ALS nucleoporin-phosphorylation evidence; kinase identity, target sites, and pore consequences are assumptions from unverified prior knowledge. Phospho-null mutants independently alter Nup98 abundance or localization, so orthogonal perturbations and matched phosphomimetic controls are required. Motor-neuron culture timing cannot capture decades-long human disease, and postmortem tissue reflects late stages [S1 caution regarding subtype heterogeneity; S2 caution regarding genetic-context specificity]. Stress kinases have many neuronal substrates, so inhibitor phenotypes may be confounded; pore-resident genetic tools partially mitigate this. The cited PMIDs (29311743, 26308891, 30853299, 29628143) support the transport-disruption premises, not the phosphorylation mechanism itself, which remains the hypothesis under test.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- The 'initiating lesion' framing is internally strained: the stated causal chain begins with TDP-43 stress, so Nup98 phosphorylation is proximal to transport collapse but not the earliest disease event. Fix by explicitly defining 'initiating' as initiation of the pore-intrinsic transport-failure cascade and adding a test of whether kinase activation and pore phosphorylation require TDP-43 de-mixing specifically (e.g., generic proteotoxic stress vs TDP-43-specific condensates), which would substantiate the pore-intrinsic initiation claim rather than leaving it descriptive.
- The secondary falsifier (phosphomimetic Nup98 with no import effect) is not decisive because Ser-to-Glu/Asp is a poor mechanistic mimic of phosphoserine; a null result could reflect mimicry failure rather than absence of sufficiency. Replace or supplement this branch with a stronger sufficiency test, e.g., inducible recruitment of an active kinase or optogenetic kinase activation localized to the pore, so the falsification logic does not hinge on phosphomimetic chemistry.
- Detecting transient, low-stoichiometry pore-associated Nup98 phosphorylation in iPSC motor neurons is a major feasibility risk that is acknowledged only implicitly; whole-cell phosphoproteomics may miss it. Fix by adding NPC enrichment or proximal biotinylation (e.g., Nup98-fused APEX/BioID) combined with quantitative MS time courses and by specifying detection-stoichiometry thresholds, otherwise the primary temporal-ordering evidence (phosphorylation before Ran collapse) may be unobtainable as designed.

glm-r1
- Causal-chain circularity: cytoplasmic TDP-43 stress is placed upstream of kinase activation and Nup98 phosphorylation, yet cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulation is also listed as a downstream response ('aggregation amplification'), so the required initiating-versus-downstream distinction is blurred by a feed-forward loop. Fix: explicitly define Nup98 phosphorylation as the initiating lesion for the transport defect conditional on already-established TDP-43 pathology, and name the earliest measurable TDP-43 abnormality that must precede kinase activation so the temporal-ordering predictions are unambiguous.
- Mechanism conflation: the hypothesis simultaneously predicts 'leaky pores' (increased passive permeability, like mitotic pore disassembly) and 'collapsed Ran-dependent import' (reduced active import); these are distinct parameters that can move in opposite directions and are not jointly implied by partial Nup98 destabilization. Fix: state the expected direction for each transport metric (e.g., passive dextran entry vs NLS-reporter import vs Ran-gradient magnitude) and add a passive-permeability readout, so a leaky-pore outcome with preserved active import would count as a testable partial confirmation rather than an ambiguous result.
- The phospho-null knock-in at 'mitotic-site' residues assumes stress kinases (p38/JNK) phosphorylate the same Nup98 residues as mitotic CDK1-like activity; if the in-vivo stress-phosphorylation motif is elsewhere, a null mutant at those sites will not protect, producing a false negative that would be misread as refuting the hypothesis. Fix: make the targeted phosphoproteomic site-discovery step the mandatory first-line experiment and design knock-ins against the discovered sites, or designate the site-agnostic pore-tethered phosphatase recruitment as the primary loss-of-phosphorylation test with knock-ins as confirmatory.

glm-r2
- The 'initiating lesion' framing is internally inconsistent: the stated causal chain begins with TDP-43 stress and chronic proteostasis stress before kinase activation, so Nup98 phosphorylation cannot initiate the disease process, only the pore-intrinsic transport-collapse branch downstream of TDP-43 pathology. Fix by explicitly reframing phosphorylation as branch-initiating (conditional on TDP-43 stress), and add a genuinely early measure — e.g., phospho-Nup98 quantified before overt cytoplasmic de-mixing under proteotoxic stress — to test the stronger claim.
- Speculative bridge B1 is only partially honest: the packet's phosphorylated TDP-43 evidence (S3) supports generic kinase activity at most, since TDP-43 phosphorylation is classically CK1/CK2-mediated rather than p38/JNK/CDK1-like, so it should not be cited even implicitly for the proposed stress-kinase class; and the step from barrier weakening to 'collapsing the Ran-dependent import gradient' is asserted without mechanism, since the RanGTP gradient is maintained by RCC1/RanGAP localization rather than pore barrier function. Fix by treating S3 as kinase-agnostic, adding direct stress-kinase activity readouts (phospho-p38/JNK blots) and RCC1/RanGAP localization as covariates, and articulating the passive-diffusion mechanism connecting leaky pores to gradient collapse.
- The primary falsifying observation is fragile: 'no detectable pore-associated Nup98 phosphorylation at any time point' risks falsely rejecting the hypothesis because transient, low-stoichiometry phosphorylation in a small iPSC motor-neuron pool may fall below phospho-antibody or MS detection thresholds, and the secondary phosphomimetic falsifier is weak because Ser→Glu poorly mimics phospho-threonine-like mitotic sites. Fix by defining the falsifier as a quantitative stoichiometry threshold (e.g., below a specified phosphorylation level during the earliest import decline while condensate disruption rescues), using pore-enriched fractionation with peptide spike-ins, and testing both S→D and S→A mutant series.
