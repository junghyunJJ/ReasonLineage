# c002

**Hypothesis (descriptive title):** Interphase phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 is an initiating pore-intrinsic lesion that precedes cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly in TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS motor neurons.

**Causal chain (initiating vs. downstream).** In vulnerable motor neurons with TDP-43 proteinopathy (e.g., sporadic ALS, or experimental TDP-43 perturbation models), chronic stress activates a kinase — proposed here purely by assumption as a mitotic-like CDK-family activity or a stress kinase with overlapping consensus specificity — that phosphorylates Nup98 FG/GLFG repeats, the residue class that normally drives mitotic NPC disassembly (unverified prior knowledge, not from the packet). Aberrant interphase phosphorylation would weaken Nup98's cohesive interactions in the pore scaffold, degrading NPC selectivity and import capacity directly, without transport-factor sequestration. Resulting Ran-dependent import failure depletes nuclear TDP-43; cytoplasmic TDP-43 then de-mixes into phosphorylated, stress-granule-independent assemblies [S3], which secondarily recruit or mislocalize transport factors and kill cells [S3], with stress granules providing further sequestration amplification [S4]. Initiating event: kinase-driven Nup98 phosphorylation at the pore. Downstream responses: nuclear TDP-43 depletion, cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly, sequestration-based transport worsening, and late nucleoporin delocalization into aggregates [S1].

**Supported premises.** (1) NPC and transport disruption is reproducibly linked to TDP-43 pathology: pore and transport components associate with insoluble TDP-43 aggregates, and nucleoporin localization and transport are disrupted in ALS tissue [S1]. (2) Cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, accumulate phosphorylated TDP-43, and cause cell death independently of canonical stress granules [S3]. (3) Stress-granule assembly can itself recruit transport factors and impair transport, and altering granule assembly modifies disease-model phenotypes [S4]. (4) Pore dysfunction also appears in a genetic ALS subtype: C9orf72 repeat RNA mislocalizes RanGAP and impairs import in models and patient-derived neurons [S2], motivating a subtype-specific rather than universal claim. None of S1–S4 documents nucleoporin phosphorylation; every phosphorylation step here is hypothesis or assumption.

**Speculative bridges (explicit assumptions).** (a) Nup98 phosphorylation state functions as an interphase pore-integrity switch, extrapolated from mitotic biology (unverified prior knowledge). (b) A specific kinase and site set operate in post-mitotic motor neurons. (c) Phosphorylation rises temporally before any TDP-43 mislocalization. (d) Partial pore weakening suffices to mislocalize TDP-43 in intact neurons.

**Alternative explanations.** (i) Sequestration-first: transport factors are trapped in stress granules [S4] or SG-independent TDP-43 assemblies [S3], making pore defects a consequence, not a cause. (ii) Direct-interaction-first: TDP-43 aggregates physically perturb nucleoporins [S1]. (iii) C9orf72-specific mechanisms such as RanGAP mislocalization [S2], with phosphorylation downstream or absent. (iv) Phosphorylation as an epiphenomenon of generalized stress-kinase activation.

**Discriminating preclinical test strategy.** Use human iPSC-derived motor neurons with inducible TDP-43 depletion or seeded aggregation, plus a C9orf72-repeat comparator for context [S2]. (1) Temporal arm: targeted LC-MS/MS and site-specific phospho-Nup98 antibodies to map sites and time courses against live NLS-reporter flux, Ran-gradient imaging, and TDP-43 localization/phosphorylation. Prediction of this hypothesis: phospho-Nup98 rises before measurable import decline and before cytoplasmic TDP-43 phosphorylation. (2) Perturbational arm: CRISPR knock-in of non-phosphorylatable Nup98 (Ser/Thr→Ala at mapped sites) should attenuate transport failure and delay nuclear TDP-43 loss despite TDP-43 perturbation; phospho-mimetic Nup98 (Ser/Thr→Asp/Glu) in otherwise healthy neurons should suffice to impair import and secondarily drive cytoplasmic TDP-43 accumulation. (3) Specificity controls: monitor stress-granule formation so that kinase or Nup98 manipulations are not silently altering granule assembly [S4]; conversely, manipulate granule or TDP-43 assembly [S3, S4] and test whether Nup98 phosphorylation merely tracks sequestration burden — if so, the sequestration model is favored. (4) Context control: if phospho-Nup98 is unchanged in C9orf72 transport defects, the hypothesis remains restricted to TDP-43-proteinopathy ALS rather than universal disease.

**Falsifying observation.** The hypothesis is falsified if, in the TDP-43 model, phospho-Nup98 never rises before transport defects, non-phosphorylatable Nup98 knock-in does not attenuate import failure, and phospho-mimetic Nup98 fails to impair import. Such results would favor transport-factor sequestration [S3, S4] or direct aggregate–pore interaction [S1] as the primary lesion.

**Limitations.** Phosphosite mapping in scarce human neurons is technically demanding; kinase redundancy may blunt single-inhibitor tests; other FG-nucleoporins could buffer Nup98 phosphorylation; culture models may not capture adult motor-neuron stress; the packet predates 2025 and does not address newer phosphorylation findings, which this proposal does not rely on.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- The initiating agent is left disjunctive and untestable as stated ('mitotic-like CDK-family activity or a stress kinase with overlapping consensus specificity'). This hedge makes any kinase result compatible with the hypothesis and weakens falsifiability. Fix: nominate a concrete candidate appropriate to post-mitotic motor neurons (e.g., CDK5, given overlapping substrate specificity with mitotic CDKs) and add an epistatic arm — kinase inhibition/overexpression with phospho-Nup98-site readout — to the perturbational strategy.
- The falsifier is a three-null conjunction ('phospho-Nup98 never rises, knock-in does not attenuate, phosphomimetic fails to impair'), which is methodologically fragile: any single negative can be explained away after the fact, and a conjunction is easy to interpret post hoc. Additionally, Ser/Thr→Asp/Glu substitutions are weak mimics for FG-repeat cohesivity and Ser→Ala may impair normal Nup98 function rather than selectively removing the phospho switch. Fix: designate temporal precedence (phospho-Nup98 rising before import decline) as the primary falsifying observation, and make the genetic arm corroborative with added rescue controls (e.g., targeted phosphatase recruitment or single-site knock-ins).
- The causal chain presents nucleoporin delocalization into aggregates as a 'late' downstream step and treats stress as the upstream trigger, but S1 shows only an association with insoluble TDP-43 aggregates, not temporal direction — and inserting stress as the true first event blurs which lesion is 'initiating.' Fix: explicitly label the S1 aggregate association as temporally unestablished and define the initiating event relative to the stress input (e.g., phosphorylation is the initiating pore-intrinsic lesion once a stress threshold is crossed), with a stress-dose/time-course readout in the temporal arm.

glm-r1
- Premise 1 slightly overstates S1: it asserts nucleoporin localization and transport are disrupted 'in ALS tissue,' but the packet attributes those effects to experimental cell systems, with only pore pathology observed in ALS tissue. Correct the wording so each claim is matched to the model system that supports it.
- The initiating kinase is left as an unnamed 'CDK-family or stress kinase' assumption, so the temporal arm can establish ordering but never identifies the causal agent; the hypothesis remains incomplete as an initiating mechanism. Add a kinase-identification component (consensus-motif analysis of mapped phosphosites plus selective inhibitor or CRISPR kinase tests) so the perturbational arm targets the proposed lesion itself.
- Ser/Thr-to-Asp/Glu phosphomimetic substitution is a weak surrogate for phosphorylating FG-repeat residues (charge mimicry lacks phosphate bulk and dynamics), so a null in the sufficiency arm could be technical rather than biological, conflating artifact with falsification. Pair the phosphomimetic test with an independent gain-of-function route (e.g., phosphatase knockdown or recruited-kinase targeting of Nup98) and treat knock-in failure cautiously in the falsifier logic.

glm-r2
- Residue-level imprecision weakens an otherwise specific hypothesis: the answer says a kinase 'phosphorylates Nup98 FG/GLFG repeats,' but phosphorylation acts on Ser/Thr residues within or adjacent to those motifs — the FG motifs themselves are not phosphorylatable, and the claim that GLFG motifs 'normally drive mitotic NPC disassembly' conflates the FG interaction modules with the mitotic phosphosites. Fix by nominating candidate Ser/Thr phosphosites (e.g., known mitotic Cdk1-site homologs) so the LC-MS/MS mapping, phospho-antibody design, and Ser/Thr→Ala knock-in are mechanistically coherent from the start.
- The falsifying observation over-weights the phosphomimetic sufficiency arm: Ser/Thr→Asp/Glu substitutions frequently fail to reproduce true phospho-Ser/Thr, so 'phosphomimetic fails to impair import' could be a mimicry artifact and would incorrectly count toward falsification. Fix by adding an orthogonal sufficiency approach (e.g., acute recruitment or activation of a candidate kinase at the NPC, or phosphatase-tethering to the pore) and pre-specifying phosphosite occupancy thresholds from the MS data used to interpret negative perturbation results.
- A stated limitation is never converted into a test arm: the answer concedes that 'kinase redundancy may blunt single-inhibitor tests' yet builds no kinase perturbation into the discriminating strategy, leaving the initiating lesion (kinase→Nup98) untested directly. Fix by nominating a concrete kinase class from the flagged mitotic prior knowledge and adding a kinase arm — e.g., CRISPRi or chemical-genetic inhibition of CDK/Nek-family members — so temporal precedence (arm 1) can be paired with a necessity test at the upstream step, not only at Nup98 itself.
