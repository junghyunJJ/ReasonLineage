# c001

Hypothesis title: Stress-triggered interphase phosphorylation of the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 as an initiating lesion preceding nuclear-import failure and TDP-43 mislocalization in ALS motor neurons

HYPOTHESIS. In ALS motor neurons under chronic proteotoxic stress — specifically in TDP-43-proteinopathy contexts modeled on [S1] and [S3] — stress-activated kinase signaling phosphorylates the FG-nucleoporin Nup98 at regulatory serine sites during interphase. This phosphorylation weakens the pore's FG-network/carrier-docking function, causing a cell-autonomous nuclear-import defect that precedes and drives loss of nuclear TDP-43. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing [S3] and stress-granule sequestration [S4] are then treated as downstream amplifiers, not initiators. The initiating lesion is therefore an intrinsic pore modification (nucleoporin phosphorylation), explicitly contrasted with sequestration-based and stress-secondary alternatives.

MECHANISM AND SUPPORTED PREMISES.
1. Pore involvement in ALS: TDP-43 pathology is associated with pore components, disrupted nucleoporin localization, and impaired transport in experimental systems and ALS tissue [S1]. This supports the plausibility of an intrinsic pore-level lesion in TDP-43 ALS; it does not identify any phosphorylation event.
2. Transport failure is a shared ALS phenotype: C9orf72 repeat RNA disrupts RanGAP localization and nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons [S2]. This supports import failure as a central phenotype across genetic contexts and justifies a parallel C9orf72 test arm; it does not establish a phosphorylation mechanism.
3. Import failure is lethal and TDP-43-linked: persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and cause cell death independently of conventional stress granules, with phosphorylated TDP-43 accumulating in the assemblies [S3]. This supports the severity and downstream consequences my hypothesis assigns to import failure; it is not evidence of nucleoporin phosphorylation.
4. Transport defects are stress-coupled: stress granules sequester transport factors and disrupt transport [S4], motivating explicit comparison against sequestration routes.

SPECULATIVE BRIDGES (assumptions, not packet evidence).
B1: Nup98 is a phospho-regulated nucleoporin whose mitotic phosphorylation contributes to pore disassembly; this is unverified prior knowledge recalled from outside the packet, used only to nominate a candidate substrate.
B2: A stress-activated kinase (candidates such as p38-MAPK or JNK; also unverified prior knowledge) aberrantly phosphorylates Nup98 in interphase neurons. No supplied source names a kinase, site, or nucleoporin modification, and the packet explicitly flags this gap.
B3: The causal ordering phosphorylation -> import decline -> TDP-43 mislocalization is assumed. [S3] reports the reverse trigger order for cytoplasmic TDP-43, so this ordering is a speculative claim built for testing, not a derived conclusion.

ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS.
Alt 1 (sequestration): import failure is fully explained by sequestration of transport factors into TDP-43 assemblies [S3] or stress granules [S4]; Nup98 phosphorylation is absent or merely a stress bystander.
Alt 2 (genetic-context route): in C9orf72 neurons, repeat-RNA effects on RanGAP and pore function [S2] drive defects without phosphorylation being required.
Alt 3 (secondary stress): transport failure is a nonspecific consequence of broader cellular stress rather than an intrinsic pore modification.

DISCRIMINATING PRECLINICAL TEST STRATEGY.
Model: isogenic human iPSC-derived motor neurons carrying a TDP-43 disease mutation, with a parallel C9orf72 repeat-expansion line. Assays: NLS-reporter import kinetics, Ran-gradient imaging, nuclear TDP-43 quantification, aggregate/stress-granule markers, and targeted mass spectrometry of isolated pore fractions.
Step 1 (temporal evidence): profile Nup98-site phosphorylation relative to import kinetics across early stress time points; initiation requires phosphorylation to rise before import declines.
Step 2 (sufficiency): knock in phospho-mimetic Nup98 (Ser->Asp/Glu) or express it near-endogenously in healthy neurons; the hypothesis predicts import slowing and later nuclear TDP-43 loss without TDP-43 aggregates or stress granules.
Step 3 (necessity): knock in phospho-null Nup98 (Ser->Ala) in the TDP-43-mutation background, with parallel arms blocking TDP-43 de-mixing (S3-style), blocking stress-granule assembly (S4-style), and inhibiting the candidate kinase.
Discriminating outcomes: the hypothesis is supported if phospho-mimetic Nup98 alone reproduces import failure, phospho-null knock-in preserves import and nuclear TDP-43 despite cytoplasmic TDP-43 and stress granules, and kinase inhibition yields early rescue. The sequestration alternative is supported instead if import failure tracks aggregate/SG burden, phospho-mimetic Nup98 does not impair import, phospho-null fails to rescue, and sequestration blockade rescues transport while Nup98 phosphorylation remains unchanged.

FALSIFYING OBSERVATION. A single decisive falsifier: in TDP-43-mutant motor neurons, nuclear-import decline begins before any detectable increase in candidate Nup98-site phosphorylation, and phospho-null Nup98 knock-in neither delays import loss nor preserves nuclear TDP-43 while a de-mixing blockade per [S3] does rescue. Either the temporal mismatch or the necessity failure alone would falsify an initiating role for Nup98 phosphorylation.

LIMITATIONS. The kinase and site identities are assumptions; phospho-mimetic substitutions imperfectly mimic phosphorylation; multiple nucleoporins may act redundantly, requiring multi-site mutants; iPSC-derived motor neurons may not fully capture aged neuronal physiology; in vivo phosphorylation dynamics are unknown; the hypothesis is scoped to TDP-43-proteinopathy and C9orf72 contexts and makes no claim of universality across sporadic ALS; no therapeutic validation is implied.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- Executability gap: the hypothesis names 'regulatory serine sites' and candidate kinases (p38/JNK) but no residue or kinase, yet Step 2/3 depend on phospho-mimetic/null KIs at the site and Step 1 on 'targeted MS,' both of which presuppose knowing the site and kinase. Fix: insert an upfront unbiased phosphoproteomic mapping stage (e.g., PRM/EThcD on immunopurified pore fractions across early stress time points) plus a kinase-validation arm (pharmaco–genetic inhibition correlated with site-level dephosphorylation) before committing to knock-in constructs.
- Falsifier risk of false negative: the decisive falsifier counts temporal mismatch (import decline before detectable Nup98 phosphorylation) as decisive, but low-stoichiometry, transient, or hard-to-enrich site phosphorylation could evade detection and falsely 'falsify' an initiating role. Fix: pre-specify detection bounds (site stoichiometry thresholds, enrichment sensitivity, phosphatase-sensitivity controls) before accepting the temporal mismatch as falsification.
- Traceability and arm underspecification: the final ranking attaches only PMIDs 29311743, 26308891, 30853299, omitting S4 (29628143) even though Alt 1 and the stress-granule-blockade arm are built on S4; additionally, the parallel C9orf72 arm is included but no predicted C9orf72-specific discriminating observation is stated (Alt 2 would predict Nup98 phosphorylation unchanged while import falls). Fix: list all four source PMIDs in the ranking and state the C9orf72 outcome that would count for or against pore-phosphorylation involvement.

glm-r1
- The phosphorylation event is underspecified: the hypothesis names no residue(s) on Nup98 and no committed kinase ('regulatory serine sites' and p38/JNK are placeholders), yet Steps 2-3 build single-site S->D/E and S->A knock-ins and targeted MS around unspecified sites. Fix: pre-map candidate residues from existing Nup98 mitotic-phosphorylation/phosphoproteomics data, register a shortlist of sites (plus multi-site mutants, which the limitations only mention in passing), and bind the MS assay to those exact peptides so a negative result cannot be dismissed as monitoring the wrong site.
- The kinase-inhibition arm does not uniquely test the hypothesis: p38/JNK and other stress kinases also phosphorylate TDP-43 itself (S3 notes phosphorylated TDP-43 in assemblies), so 'early rescue' after kinase inhibition is equally consistent with blocking phospho-TDP-43 de-mixing rather than Nup98 phosphorylation. Fix: make causal attribution substrate-level — e.g., a pore-local kinase-site-mutant Nup98 allele versus phospho-null, or a kinase condition under which Nup98 phosphorylation but not TDP-43 phosphorylation is preserved.
- The Step 3 outcome bundle is internally inconsistent with the stated causal chain: if cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing is downstream of import failure, phospho-null Nup98 that rescues import should also largely prevent de-mixing, yet the prediction asserts preserved import and nuclear TDP-43 'despite cytoplasmic TDP-43 and stress granules'. Fix: state in advance which downstream phenotypes phospho-null is expected to rescue versus leave intact, and specify which observed pattern counts as hypothesis-support versus sequestration-support.

glm-r2
- The phosphorylation target is formally vague: 'regulatory serine sites' on Nup98 names no residue or kinase, yet Steps 2-3 (Ser->Asp/Ala knock-ins) and the targeted MS readout cannot be constructed or costed without a nominated site. Fix: pre-specify candidate residues mapped from documented mitotic Nup98 phospho-sites filtered by stress-kinase consensus motifs, with an unbiased phospho-proteomics arm to catch missed sites.
- The falsifying observation is vulnerable to a measurement artifact: 'import decline begins before ANY detectable increase in phosphorylation' would count as falsification even if early phosphorylation exists below the assay's detection limit, producing a false rejection of the initiating role. Fix: pre-register a quantitative detection threshold and require concordant negative results from two orthogonal methods (site-specific phospho-antibody plus MS site-occupancy quantitation in sorted motor neurons) before accepting the temporal mismatch.
- The causal bridge from import slowing to nuclear TDP-43 loss is asserted rather than mechanized: a slowed (not blocked) import may leave the TDP-43 shuttle equilibrium intact, and the packet's own S3 shows cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing as a driver, so the hypothesized ordering (phosphorylation -> import -> TDP-43 loss) is only measured at two points. Fix: insert a direct TDP-43 shuttling readout (e.g., heterokaryon or FLIP-based assay) into the time-course so all three links in the proposed chain are independently quantified.
