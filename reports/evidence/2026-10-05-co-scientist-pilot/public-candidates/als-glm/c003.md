# c003

Title: Premature stress-kinase phosphorylation of Nup98 initiates nucleocytoplasmic transport failure upstream of TDP-43 mislocalization in ALS motor neurons.

Hypothesis and causal chain. In TDP-43-positive ALS motor neurons, including but not limited to C9orf72 expansion carriers, an aberrantly activated stress-associated kinase phosphorylates the nucleoporin Nup98 at sites normally used only during mitotic pore disassembly. This premature phosphorylation weakens Nup98 association with the pore scaffold, causing partial nuclear-pore destabilization and early import failure. Collapsed Ran-dependent import then prevents nuclear retention of TDP-43, driving nuclear TDP-43 depletion, cytoplasmic TDP-43 de-mixing, and ultimately cell death. Under this hypothesis, nucleoporin phosphorylation is the initiating event; TDP-43 pathology and aggregate-associated sequestration are downstream responses.

Supported premises. (1) TDP-43 pathology is physically linked to nuclear-pore and transport components, with pore abnormalities observed in ALS tissue [S1]. (2) C9orf72 repeat expansion disrupts nuclear import and RanGAP localization, and transport-related perturbations modify model phenotypes [S2], confirming that transport failure is a plausible disease-relevant node. (3) Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies impair import, deplete nuclear TDP-43, and cause cell death independently of conventional stress granules [S3], establishing that transport failure and TDP-43 mislocalization are mechanistically coupled. (4) Stress granules can sequester transport factors and impair transport [S4], defining the main alternative class of mechanism. None of S1-S4 establishes a nucleoporin phosphorylation event as an initiating cause; that step is purely hypothesized.

Speculative bridges (assumptions, not evidence). The identity of the kinase is an assumption; from recalled, unverified prior knowledge, mitotic kinases such as Cdk-family kinases phosphorylate Nup98 during pore disassembly, and Cdk5 or MAPK-family stress kinases could aberrantly reactivate such programs in post-mitotic neurons. The specific Nup98 phosphorylation sites are unknown and must be mapped. The direction of causality (phosphorylation preceding TDP-43 mislocalization) is the hypothesis itself, not established fact. Whether the mechanism operates in sporadic ALS, C9orf72 ALS, or both is open; the hypothesis is deliberately restricted to TDP-43-positive contexts and makes no universal claim.

Alternative explanations. (a) Sequestration-first: stress granules sequester Ran and import factors, impairing transport without any pore modification [S4]. (b) TDP-43-first: cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies physically pull pore or transport components out of position, with phosphorylation appearing only secondarily [S1, S3]. (c) Generic collapse: broad energetic or proteostatic stress secondarily degrades transport with no specific pore phosphorylation.

Discriminating preclinical strategy. Use iPSC-derived motor neurons from C9orf72 ALS patients and non-C9 sporadic ALS patients with TDP-43 pathology, plus isogenic CRISPR-corrected controls, and an acute stress model. First, temporal mapping: time-resolved phosphoproteomics of enriched pore fractions and phospho-specific antibodies against mapped Nup98 sites, tracked in parallel with live imaging of NLS-GFP import kinetics, RanGAP localization, and endogenous TDP-43 localization. The hypothesis predicts Nup98 phosphorylation rises before measurable import decline and before nuclear TDP-43 loss. Second, perturbational necessity: CRISPR knock-in of non-phosphorylatable Nup98 (S-to-A) at candidate sites should rescue import defects and reduce TDP-43 mislocalization despite C9orf72 repeats or stress. Third, perturbational sufficiency: phospho-mimetic knock-in (S-to-D/E) in control neurons should alone induce import failure and nuclear TDP-43 depletion without stress or pre-existing TDP-43 aggregation. Fourth, staged kinase inhibition: blocking the suspected kinase early should rescue transport and prevent TDP-43 mislocalization, whereas late inhibition after aggregate formation should not. Fifth, discrimination from the sequestration alternative [S4]: repeat the assays in neurons where stress-granule assembly is blocked (for example via G3BP1/2 loss; this perturbation rationale is unverified prior knowledge); the hypothesis predicts persistent import impairment if Nup98 phosphorylation occurs, whereas pure sequestration predicts rescue. Sixth, discrimination from the TDP-43-first alternative [S1, S3]: prevent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly or express nuclear-restricted TDP-43; the hypothesis predicts early Nup98 phosphorylation and import loss even when cytoplasmic aggregation is blocked.

Falsifying observation. The hypothesis is falsified if import failure and nuclear TDP-43 loss precede detectable Nup98 phosphorylation in time-resolved assays; if non-phosphorylatable Nup98 knock-in fails to rescue transport while stress-granule disruption rescues it; or if phospho-mimetic Nup98 produces no transport defect in otherwise healthy motor neurons. Any of these outcomes would demote phosphorylation to a downstream marker at best.

Limitations. Phosphosite detection in scarce motor-neuron material may lack depth; iPSC motor neurons may not reproduce decades-long aging dynamics; the kinase could differ across ALS subtypes; model rescue demonstrates necessity and sufficiency in cells, not that phosphorylation initiates disease in patients.

## Public critiques

glm-r0
- The staging-kinase-inhibition arm (experiment four) is not actually executable as written because no kinase is named and no inhibitors are identified, even though the whole causal chain depends on it. Fix by committing to a specific druggable candidate (e.g., a named Cdk-family or MAPK member with available inhibitors and prior evidence of Nup/Nup98 targeting) or by replacing that arm with a kinase-independent direct comparison of the site knock-ins.
- The central bridging premise — that Nup98 carries mitotic-disassembly phosphosites and can be re-phosphorylated by stress kinases in post-mitotic neurons — rests entirely on self-declared unverified recall, with no citation, even though published phosphosite/mapping literature exists. Because this is the load-bearing initiating step, anchor it to specific external evidence or explicitly gate the hypothesis on a preliminary phosphoproteomic mapping result before the perturbational experiments.
- The final ranking's PMID list (29311743, 26308891, 30853299) omits S4 (29628143) although S4 defines the primary sequestration alternative that the discriminating strategy (G3BP1/2 loss) is built against; additionally, the TDP-43-first discrimination uses a confounded manipulation (nuclear-restricted TDP-43 itself alters RNA metabolism) — list all sources used and specify a cleaner way to block cytoplasmic TDP-43 assembly, such as temporally controlled aggregation-defective TDP-43 variants.

glm-r1
- The kinase is left as an unspecified class (Cdk5 vs. MAPK), yet the staged-inhibition arm requires a named target: 'blocking the suspected kinase' is not executable as written. Fix by adding a committed step in which mapped phosphosite motifs (e.g., S/TP for proline-directed kinases) select one kinase-inhibitor pair before the inhibition experiment.
- The necessity experiment presumes clean S-to-A knock-in of Nup98, but the hypothesized sites are mitotic-disassembly sites that function in dividing cells; editing them in iPSCs before differentiation could impair mitotic pore disassembly, cell fitness, and cloning, confounding rescue interpretation. Use inducible or post-differentiation editing in already post-mitotic motor neurons, with cell-fitness and NPC-composition controls.
- Alternative (c) (generic energetic/proteostatic collapse) is named but never experimentally excluded: none of the six arms distinguishes a specific Nup98 phosphorylation event from broad stress-associated phosphorylation. Add global-stress readouts (general translation/stress markers, a panel of unrelated phosphosites) to demonstrate Nup98 phosphorylation is specific rather than a bystander of cellular collapse.

glm-r2
- The staged kinase-inhibition experiment (step 4) is not yet executable: the kinase is left as an unnamed family (Cdk5 vs MAPK 'suspected kinase'), so necessity-by-inhibition cannot be designed. Fix: commit to one candidate kinase chosen from the phosphosite map (motif + phosphoproteomics), with paired genetic (kinase knockdown/kinase-dead) and pharmacologic tools and off-target controls, and state that step 4 is conditional on that identification.
- The causal bridge from 'partial Nup98 destabilization' to 'early import failure' is asserted without a mechanism-level readout; the supplied evidence (S2-S4) supports sequestration-based transport defects, so a scaffold-weakening phenotype could be confounded with it. Fix: add a discriminating readout such as pore-composition imaging (other Nups' retention), cargo-selective import assays, or a head-to-head Nup98-phosphorylation vs Ran/import-factor-sequestration comparison to show they produce distinguishable transport signatures.
- The phosphomimetic S-to-D sufficiency test is a weak falsifier: S-to-D frequently fails to mimic phosphate-dependent structural effects, so a negative result would not cleanly falsify the hypothesis. Fix: add an orthogonal sufficiency test, e.g., engineered targeting of the candidate kinase to the nuclear pore or inducible phosphatase interference, so sufficiency does not rest on a single poor mimic.
