Hypothesis title: Leflunomide relieves differentiation blockade in AML through on-target DHODH inhibition at rheumatoid-arthritis-relevant exposure.

The proposal is that leflunomide, whose original indication is rheumatoid arthritis and whose active metabolite is teriflunomide (approval status, indication, and human exposure ranges are unverified prior knowledge), inhibits dihydroorotate dehydrogenase in differentiation-blocked acute myeloid leukemia blasts and induces myeloid differentiation. The context is primary human AML with morphologic or immunophenotypic differentiation arrest. The endpoint is myeloid maturation. Bulk cytotoxicity, leukemia-stem-cell depletion, and normal progenitor toxicity are scored separately so differentiation is distinguished from stem-cell eradication and from nonspecific killing.

Supported premises. Experimental DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation in AML models and affects disease-related endpoints in the systems investigated, connecting this metabolic step to relief of differentiation blockade as an outcome distinct from nonspecific cell killing [S4]. That evidence does not establish that every approved drug affecting the pathway reproduces the result or achieves suitable exposure and selectivity [S4]. Functionally defined leukemia stem cells in the specimens studied frequently had low reactive-oxygen-species levels and elevated BCL-2, and BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells [S1]. This motivates study of specific dependencies and does not establish a therapeutic window for an arbitrary mitochondrial inhibitor [S1]. Venetoclax with azacitidine is an established comparator connected to leukemia-stem-cell TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation changes, and those findings cannot be transferred directly to another drug; that combination is not the candidate [S3]. Residual cells after cytarabine were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells; they showed oxidative-metabolism and fatty-acid-oxidation-associated features, and altering that state changed treatment response in the studied settings [S2]. Those model findings do not establish clinical efficacy of a new compound [S2].

Speculative bridges. Moving the differentiation phenotype from experimental DHODH inhibition [S4] to leflunomide is an assumption. This packet contains no AML measurement of teriflunomide uptake, DHODH occupancy, or maturation. The hypothesis assumes that unbound concentrations reached during rheumatoid arthritis treatment, still requiring verification, engage DHODH inside primary blasts, and that myeloid maturation, rather than nucleotide-starvation cytotoxicity alone, explains any fall in leukemic burden. A selectivity window versus normal hematopoietic progenitors is also assumed.

Alternative explanations. Marker gains could reflect selective loss of immature blasts and persistence of cells that were already mature. Possible non-DHODH activities of leflunomide are unverified prior knowledge and could yield a phenotype that uridine does not reverse. Blasts that import extracellular pyrimidines could bypass the enzyme. Oxidative-phosphorylation collapse would align the result with BCL-2-linked stem-cell metabolism [S1] and the venetoclax-azacitidine comparator [S3], a different mechanism from DHODH-dependent differentiation [S4]. Loss of cytarabine-residual cells could instead track the fatty-acid-oxidation-associated state in those models [S2].

Discriminating preclinical test. In primary AML samples with differentiation arrest, paired with normal CD34-positive hematopoietic cells, measure dihydroorotate accumulation, pyrimidine-nucleotide depletion, morphology, and myeloid surface-marker acquisition across concentrations anchored to independently verified human free teriflunomide levels. Uridine supplementation is the on-target rescue; reversal supports DHODH dependence [S4]. A parallel venetoclax exposure is only a mechanistic comparator, with oxidative-phosphorylation rate recorded so DHODH-linked differentiation is separated from BCL-2-linked suppression of oxidative phosphorylation [S1][S3]. In a cytarabine-residual assay motivated by the oxidative residual state [S2], classify remaining cells by maturation versus death, keeping that readout distinct from the ROS-low stem-cell population [S1]. Normal myeloid colony formation is the selectivity comparator. A xenograft, if used, would match exposure and prespecify human-cell maturation together with normal hematopoietic toxicity.

Falsifying observation. The claim is disconfirmed if verified clinically relevant free teriflunomide exposure produces neither uridine-reversible DHODH-pathway engagement nor increased myeloid differentiation in primary differentiation-blocked AML. It is disconfirmed if that exposure suppresses normal myeloid colony formation to a similar degree. It is disconfirmed if maturation markers rise only with venetoclax-like oxidative-phosphorylation collapse [S1][S3] and persist after uridine rescue, placing the effect outside on-target DHODH inhibition [S4].

Limitations. The packet supports a pathway-level differentiation effect of DHODH inhibition in the models tested [S4], not clinical activity, subtype coverage, or sufficient leflunomide exposure. Approval, the rheumatoid arthritis indication, protein binding, and unbound plasma concentrations remain unverified prior knowledge. Tissue nucleosides may lessen pyrimidine depletion seen in culture. Differentiation can increase circulating mature myeloid cells and complicate burden readouts. Stem-cell oxidative metabolism [S1], chemotherapy-residual oxidative metabolism [S2], and venetoclax-azacitidine observations [S3] bound interpretation and are not efficacy evidence for leflunomide.
