# Unregistered public response

Not registered as a candidate because output validation or the writing batch failed.

Hypothesis title: Leflunomide, through DHODH inhibition by its active metabolite, relieves differentiation blockade in non-APL AML at rheumatoid-arthritis-relevant exposure.

Hypothesis and biological context. Leflunomide, whose original approved use is rheumatoid arthritis, will drive myeloid differentiation and reduce leukemic propagation in non-acute-promyelocytic-leukemia AML blasts that retain a DHODH-sensitive maturation arrest. The prespecified endpoint is differentiation with loss of clonogenic or engrafting capacity. Bulk cytotoxicity, killing of ROS-low leukemia stem cells, and the venetoclax–azacitidine metabolic effect are separate readouts, not the claimed activity. Approval status, indication, conversion of leflunomide to teriflunomide, human DHODH potency, and free-drug exposure are unverified prior knowledge and require confirmation outside this packet.

Supported premises. Experimental DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation in AML systems and affects disease-related endpoints in the models studied, linking pyrimidine metabolism to relief of differentiation blockade rather than to nonspecific killing alone [S4]. That evidence does not show that every approved agent affecting the pathway reproduces the result or achieves selective exposure [S4]. In a different biological frame, functionally defined leukemia stem cells in the specimens examined often had low reactive oxygen species and elevated BCL-2, and BCL-2 inhibition reduced oxidative phosphorylation and affected quiescent leukemia stem cells [S1]. Residual cells after cytarabine were not consistently enriched for immature quiescent leukemia stem cells; they showed mitochondrial and fatty-acid-oxidation-related oxidative features, and changing that state altered treatment response in those settings [S2]. Venetoclax plus azacitidine has been tied to leukemia-stem-cell TCA-cycle and oxidative-phosphorylation changes in patient-associated observations and experiments and is an established comparator only [S3].

Speculative bridges. The proposal bridges from [S4] to leflunomide by assuming that teriflunomide concentrations achieved in rheumatologic dosing inhibit AML-cell DHODH enough to deplete pyrimidines and phenocopy the experimental inhibitors. It also assumes a responsive subset of non-APL blasts that can still execute a myeloid maturation program, and a window in which those blasts differentiate before normal hematopoietic progenitors are depleted. A further assumption is that this differentiation effect is mechanistically separable from the BCL-2–associated oxidative state of quiescent leukemia stem cells [S1] and from the oxidative residual state after cytarabine [S2]. Those populations are covariates and safety contexts, not the primary target claimed here.

Alternative explanations. Off-target actions of leflunomide or teriflunomide, reported in unverified prior knowledge beyond DHODH, could produce the same surface phenotype. Selective death of immature blasts could enrich residual mature cells and imitate differentiation. Pyrimidine stress might secondarily suppress oxidative phosphorylation and resemble the energy-metabolism pattern described for venetoclax–azacitidine [S3] or the oxidative dependence of cytarabine-residual cells [S2], so a stem-cell metabolic kill [S1] could be mistaken for DHODH-guided maturation. Equivalent injury to normal myeloid progenitors would remove a therapeutic window even if the leukemic marker changes are real.

Discriminating preclinical test. In primary non-APL AML samples, matched normal CD34-positive cells, and an AML model in which experimental DHODH inhibition induces differentiation [S4], compare leflunomide and teriflunomide with a selective DHODH-inhibitor control under uridine rescue. Anchor concentrations to independently verified rheumatologic free-drug levels. Score three prespecified endpoints: myeloid differentiation by morphology, maturation markers, and a differentiation transcriptional program; leukemic progenitor or engraftment function after drug washout; and viability plus differentiation of normal progenitors. Confirm enzyme engagement by DHODH activity or a pyrimidine-depletion signature. Stratify by ROS-low, BCL-2-high fractions [S1] and by cytarabine-residual oxidative cells [S2]. Run venetoclax–azacitidine only as a comparator for oxidative-phosphorylation suppression [S3].

Falsifying observation. The drug–mechanism–context claim fails if, at verified clinically relevant exposure, AML blasts show uridine-rescuable DHODH inhibition yet no increase in myeloid differentiation and no loss of leukemic propagation relative to vehicle, or if normal progenitors are depleted to the same or a greater degree. It also fails if differentiation is absent after rescue by uridine and is absent with a selective DHODH inhibitor, which would assign the phenotype to an off-target activity. A genotype-restricted miss narrows the indicated context; it leaves the mechanism intact in models already responsive to experimental DHODH inhibition [S4].

Limitations. The packet supports a pathway-level differentiation effect of DHODH inhibition in defined experimental systems [S4]. It supplies no leflunomide exposure, selectivity, or AML efficacy data. Results on ROS-low stem cells [S1], cytarabine-residual oxidative cells [S2], and venetoclax–azacitidine [S3] are not efficacy evidence for this drug. In vitro levels may diverge from patient free drug. Differentiation can carry clinical risks outside this packet, and AML genetic heterogeneity can limit any positive preclinical subset.
