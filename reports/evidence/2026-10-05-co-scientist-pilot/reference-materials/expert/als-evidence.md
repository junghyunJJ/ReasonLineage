# ALS evidence packet — draft v1

This curated input is not a comprehensive review. Every method receives the same UTF-8 file. Summaries are original paraphrases. Bibliographic metadata and abstracts were checked through the public NCBI ID converter and PubMed EFetch on 2026-10-05. Selected studies predate 2025; this does not remove knowledge already in a model's training. Co-Scientist outputs are not included.

## Research question

Propose one specific, testable hypothesis connecting phosphorylation of a nuclear-pore-complex nucleoporin to ALS-related neuronal dysfunction. Explain the causal chain, distinguish an initiating mechanism from a downstream response, and propose an experimental strategy that discriminates the hypothesis from an alternative. Identify supported premises, speculative bridges, and a falsifying observation. This adapts Co-Scientist Supplementary Note 10; nucleoporin phosphorylation is not supplied as an established ALS cause.

## S1 — TDP-43 and nuclear-pore dysfunction

Chou et al. (2018), *TDP-43 pathology disrupts nuclear pore complexes and nucleocytoplasmic transport in ALS/FTD*. Nature Neuroscience. DOI: `10.1038/s41593-017-0047-3`; PMID: `29311743`; PMCID: `PMC5800968`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29311743/).

Nuclear-pore and transport components were associated with insoluble TDP-43 aggregates. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupted nucleoporin localization and nuclear transport in experimental cell systems; relevant pore pathology was observed in ALS tissue. This supports examining the connection between TDP-43 pathology and transport disruption. It does not identify a nucleoporin phosphorylation event as the initiating cause or establish the same sequence in every ALS subtype.

## S2 — C9orf72-associated transport defects

Zhang et al. (2015), *The C9orf72 repeat expansion disrupts nucleocytoplasmic transport*. Nature. DOI: `10.1038/nature14973`; PMID: `26308891`; PMCID: `PMC4800742`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26308891/).

Expanded C9orf72 repeat RNA was linked to altered RanGAP localization and impaired nuclear import in models and patient-derived neurons. Manipulations of transport or repeat-RNA-associated mechanisms modified model phenotypes. This supplies a genetic-context-specific route to transport dysfunction. It does not show that sporadic ALS arises by the same route or directly establish a nucleoporin phosphorylation mechanism.

## S3 — A stress-granule-independent route

Gasset-Rosa et al. (2019), *Cytoplasmic TDP-43 De-mixing Independent of Stress Granules Drives Inhibition of Nuclear Import, Loss of Nuclear TDP-43, and Cell Death*. Neuron. DOI: `10.1016/j.neuron.2019.02.038`; PMID: `30853299`; PMCID: `PMC6548321`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30853299/).

Persistent cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies could form independently of conventional stress granules. They recruited or mislocalized transport-associated proteins and were associated with impaired transport, nuclear TDP-43 depletion, and cell death. Phosphorylated TDP-43 accumulated in these assemblies. That is not itself evidence of nucleoporin phosphorylation. The experimental perturbations do not establish that every physiological ALS trajectory begins in this way.

## S4 — A stress-granule-associated route

Zhang et al. (2018), *Stress Granule Assembly Disrupts Nucleocytoplasmic Transport*. Cell. DOI: `10.1016/j.cell.2018.03.025`; PMID: `29628143`; PMCID: `PMC6083872`. [Primary record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29628143/).

Cellular stress recruited transport factors into stress granules and impaired nucleocytoplasmic transport. Altering stress-granule assembly modified transport defects and disease-model phenotypes. This provides a sequestration-based alternative to an intrinsic pore-modification hypothesis. Read alongside S3, it does not justify assuming that conventional stress granules are required for every TDP-43-associated transport defect.

## Evidence boundaries

- These selected passages do not establish that nucleoporin phosphorylation initiates ALS. Label a proposed kinase, modification site, or interaction as an assumption unless a supplied source supports it.
- Specify the ALS context; do not assume a universal mechanism across genetic and sporadic disease.
- Distinguish pore alteration, transport-factor sequestration, and failure secondary to broader stress.
- Explain temporal and perturbational evidence that would distinguish cause from consequence.
- Propose a research hypothesis rather than claiming a mechanism or treatment has been validated.
