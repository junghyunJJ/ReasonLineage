# P255c3d4421

CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation as an initiating nuclear-import defect in TARDBP-mutant motor neurons

Hypothesis. In heterozygous TARDBP-M337V human motor neurons, increased endogenous CDK5 activity phosphorylates NUP98’s FG-repeat region, shortening pore residence and initiating impaired nuclear import before nuclear TDP-43 depletion, persistent cytoplasmic assemblies, and neuronal dysfunction. This proposes a proximal initiating transport lesion, not the origin of ALS or a universal mechanism. The allele’s ALS association is unverified prior knowledge; this causal chain is speculative.

Supported premises. Disease-linked TDP-43 disrupts nucleoporin localization and transport, with related pore pathology in ALS tissue [S1]. Cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies can recruit transport-associated proteins alongside import impairment, nuclear depletion, and death without conventional stress granules [S3]. Conventional stress granules provide another sequestration route [S4]. C9orf72-associated RanGAP/import defects establish a separate genetic-context-specific mechanism [S2], not evidence for this phosphorylation pathway. None establishes nucleoporin phosphorylation as an initiating lesion.

Speculative substrate rationale. Unverified prior knowledge motivates CDK5 as a neuron-relevant, proline-directed kinase and NUP98 as an FG nucleoporin. The FG-region choice assumes phosphorylation alters interactions controlling pore retention. No packet evidence favors this pairing over another kinase or anchoring-domain modification. Map early mutant-enriched FG-region phosphosites and test motif compatibility. Measure endogenous CDK5 activity, abundance, and regulatory-complex association before import divergence. Compare competing neuronal kinases against the same sites; purified CDK5 must phosphorylate them directly. Missing sites or absent CDK5 dependence would reject this pairing, not justify substituting another mechanism.

Temporal and pore-specific tests. Use multiple independently derived mutant/corrected isogenic iPSC motor-neuron pairs and sham-edited controls. Require a documented normal-import baseline after motor-neuron specification; pre-degeneration sampling alone cannot establish initiation. Track phosphosite occupancy, pore-resolved photoconversion kinetics of endogenously tagged NUP98, import kinetics, TDP-43 distribution, assemblies, transport-factor partitioning, stress, axonal integrity, and survival. Reconstitute import in semi-permeabilized neurons with identical soluble transport machinery and energy, verifying phosphorylation retention, comparable Ran gradients, and envelope integrity. Include passive-permeability and export controls. Persistent impairment supports a pore-retained defect; disappearance favors soluble-factor depletion rather than proving intrinsic pore failure.

Substrate-specific causality and mediation. Test CDK5 dependence by genetic suppression with active versus kinase-inactive rescue. Introduce endogenous nonphosphorylatable substitutions at mapped sites, requiring normal expression, differentiation, and basal transport against wild-type editing controls. In corrected neurons, CDK5 activation matched to mutant activity should reproduce the residence/import sequence with phosphorylatable NUP98 but not the functional nonphosphorylatable background. Kinase manipulation or phosphosite rescue alone cannot establish residence mediation. Independently restore NUP98 residence using an inducible pore-targeted tether calibrated to normal dwell times, with anchor-only and wild-type-tether controls. Import rescue despite persistent phosphorylation would support residence as mediator. Site-edit rescue without residence restoration would instead undermine that causal link. Evaluate early protection before assembly burden changes, with matched upstream stress and viability.

Alternative-pathway discrimination. Monitoring stress-granule-independent assemblies is insufficient. Alongside conventional stress-granule suppression [S4], develop and prevalidate a cytoplasm-restricted, assembly-disrupting TDP-43 intrabody as a candidate intervention against S3-type sequestration. Require reduced assemblies and assembly-associated transport-factor partitioning, unchanged total TDP-43, and normal basal nuclear TDP-43 and import in corrected controls. Reject the reagent if it directly alters CDK5 or NUP98; include an inactive binder and verify comparable upstream stress. Apply interventions before divergence and after assemblies appear. The hypothesis predicts that suppressing sequestration will not prevent the first phosphorylation/residence/import defect, whereas NUP98 editing will prevent import failure before changing assemblies. Assembly suppression preventing early phosphorylation and import failure would favor sequestration-first causation. Phosphorylation appearing only after import failure would favor a downstream response. Broader stress independently causing both remains an alternative; timing alone cannot exclude it.

Falsifying observation. If baseline-functional phosphosite edits abolish the mapped CDK5-dependent phosphorylation event but leave the earliest import defect unchanged under matched stress and viability, the claimed initiating mechanism is falsified—even if later survival improves.

Limitations. A sufficiently selective assembly intervention may not be achievable; without validated specificity, that comparison is inconclusive. Editing, tagging, permeabilization, and tethering can independently perturb pores. Biochemical phosphorylation does not establish neuronal causality. Temporal resolution, kinase pleiotropy, cultured-neuron immaturity, and allele specificity limit inference. Positive findings would establish a restricted preclinical mechanism, not a validated treatment.
