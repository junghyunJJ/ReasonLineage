Teriflunomide-induced differentiation of pyrimidine-salvage-poor residual AML after cytarabine

Hypothesis. At human-attainable unbound exposure, teriflunomide will inhibit DHODH, induce durable myeloid differentiation, and reduce leukemia-propagating activity in cytarabine-residual, oxidative AML unable to compensate through pyrimidine salvage, while preserving normal progenitor function. The speculative bridges are inadequate adaptive salvage, maturation rather than arrest or death, and a selective exposure window.

Supported premises. Cytarabine-resistant AML cells displayed oxidative-metabolism-associated properties without consistent enrichment for immature, quiescent leukemia stem cells [S2]. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation in experimental AML [S4]. Neither finding establishes DHODH dependence in oxidative residual disease. ROS-low, BCL-2-associated leukemia stem cells constitute a separately assessed population [S1]. Venetoclax–azacitidine is an established metabolic comparator, not this repurposing proposal [S3]. Bulk killing, differentiation, and loss of leukemia-propagating activity remain distinct endpoints.

Drug-specific uncertainties. Unverified prior knowledge identifies teriflunomide as approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis, a DHODH inhibitor, and substantially protein-bound; it also links DHODH to de novo pyrimidine synthesis and uridine to salvage. These recollections require verification. Before translational testing, confirm approval and original indication, biochemical and cellular potency, protein binding, human pharmacokinetics, and documented hematopoietic toxicity. Measure free drug in the experimental medium rather than extrapolating from total plasma concentrations.

Quantitative exposure gate. Establish numerical human unbound Cmax and AUC ceilings from verified, tolerable labeled dosing before efficacy experiments. Translational concentration–time profiles must have unbound Cmax and AUC ratios no greater than 1 relative to those ceilings, with an experimental entry criterion of at least 50% DHODH inhibition. These are protocol gates, not validated efficacy thresholds. The packet cannot supply absolute exposure values; failure to engage DHODH within these bounds stops the translational claim. Marrow exposure requires separate measurement.

Incremental contribution. DHODH-driven differentiation is already supported [S4]; neither that mechanism nor first teriflunomide use in AML is claimed as novel. Prior teriflunomide/leflunomide AML studies require checking because they are absent from the packet. The proposed increment is adaptive-salvage stratification in post-cytarabine disease, with comparison against a validated selective DHODH inhibitor.

Prospective population definition. Use paired pretreatment/post-cytarabine specimens and independently derived residual-disease models. Low baseline salvage flux is insufficient: in separate classification aliquots, measure isotope-resolved de novo and salvage flux before and 24–48 hours after reference DHODH blockade under measured physiological uridine and protein conditions. Provisionally classify salvage-poor samples as maintaining UTP at no more than 50% of vehicle at both time points despite adaptation, and competent samples as recovering to at least 80%; analyze intermediate values continuously. These prespecified conventions require robustness testing. Define oxidative-high/low groups using reference-cohort, viable-cell-normalized oxygen-consumption tertiles. Factorially compare both oxidative groups and both salvage classes to determine whether oxidative status adds predictive value.

Discriminating experiments. Compare vehicle, teriflunomide, a biochemically validated selective DHODH inhibitor, and inducible partial DHODH suppression at matched target engagement. Measure intracellular exposure, pyrimidine pools, flux, and target inhibition before substantial cell loss. Test validated gain/loss perturbations of uridine uptake or phosphorylation: increasing functional salvage should attenuate differentiation and propagation loss, whereas reducing salvage should sensitize competent cells. Confirm flux changes rather than relying on expression. Uridine rescue is interpretable only when uptake is demonstrated and does not establish unique target specificity. An independently validated, catalytically functional teriflunomide-insensitive DHODH rescue would strengthen attribution. Include venetoclax–azacitidine [S3], untreated AML, and normal donor progenitors.

Functional outcomes and alternatives. Track initially immature, genotype-confirmed leukemic cells with lineage labels, morphology, and maturation markers to distinguish induced differentiation from survival of pre-existing mature cells. Measure apoptosis separately. After verified drug clearance, assess clonogenic recovery and transplantation-based leukemia initiation. Exposure-matched xenografts should measure marrow pharmacology; normal-progenitor colony formation and engraftment test selectivity. Off-target toxicity, reversible arrest, chemotherapy-associated oxidative stress, and culture-induced uridine scarcity remain alternative explanations addressed by orthogonal perturbations, longitudinal tracking, and physiological conditions.

Falsification and limitations. Confirmed DHODH inhibition and pyrimidine depletion within exposure bounds, without differentiation or reduced propagation in preclassified salvage-poor residual AML, would falsify the central mechanism–context prediction. Failure of verified salvage manipulation to alter response would reject salvage as the causal stratifier. Comparable normal-progenitor impairment would reject selectivity; activity only above human exposure bounds would reject translation. Heterogeneity, unstable salvage adaptation, transplantation biases, uncertain marrow exposure, and overlapping prior work limit interpretation. Positive preclinical findings would not establish clinical efficacy.
