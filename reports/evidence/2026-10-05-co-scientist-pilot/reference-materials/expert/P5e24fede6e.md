# P5e24fede6e

Title: Repurposing teriflunomide, an approved relapsing–remitting multiple sclerosis drug, as a clinically achievable DHODH inhibitor for cytarabine-resistant oxidative-metabolic AML residual cells.

1) Hypothesis and context.
The hypothesis is deliberately narrow. Teriflunomide (approved for relapsing–remitting multiple sclerosis; identification as leflunomide's active metabolite is unverified prior knowledge requiring regulatory-labelling confirmation) can, at clinically achievable unbound concentrations, inhibit DHODH and force differentiation (S4 mechanism) specifically in the cytarabine-resistant, oxidative-metabolic residual-cell state (S2). The novelty is not DHODH-based differentiation per se — that mechanism is established by S4 and by clinical-stage DHODH inhibitors (unverified prior knowledge: brequinar-class tool compounds are substantially more potent than teriflunomide) — but whether an approved drug's human pharmacokinetics reach on-target marrow exposure, and whether the combined insult of pyrimidine depletion plus partial respiratory electron-flux reduction tips the S2 residual state toward differentiation rather than survival.

2) Known landscape (labeled unverified prior knowledge, not packet evidence).
Reported teriflunomide DHODH IC50: sub-micromolar to low micromolar. Steady-state plasma at approved 14 mg/day: high total levels with >99% protein binding; whether unbound exposure reaches IC50 is the central open question. Prior leflunomide-in-AML reports may exist (possibly in PTEN-mutant contexts); the claim is repositioned from mechanism discovery to an exposure-feasibility test in a specific residual-cell context, which is the genuinely incremental contribution.

3) Supported premises (frozen packet evidence).
[S4] DHODH inhibition promotes myeloid differentiation in experimental AML — mechanistic core. [S2] Cytarabine-resistant residual cells are not necessarily LSC-enriched but require oxidative metabolism with FAO features — defining the contextual niche. [S1] ROS-low, BCL-2-high quiescent LSCs depend on OXPHOS — motivating a stem-cell metabolic vulnerability. [S3] Venetoclax–azacitidine disrupts TCA/OXPHOS — the in-study mechanistic comparator.

4) Speculative bridges (assumptions, not packet evidence).
Bridge A: On-target exposure is achievable — the go/no-go gate: verified unbound steady-state ≥ published DHODH IC50; if below by >5-fold, deprioritize before experimental commitment. Bridge B: Differentiation effects generalize from potent tool compounds to the less potent approved drug. Bridge C (exploratory): Partial complex III electron-flux reduction contributes to fate change; pyrimidine depletion alone may suffice. Bridge D (exploratory): ROS-low, BCL-2-high LSCs undergo differentiation or loss of self-renewal — S4 shows bulk differentiation only; this LSC extension is speculative. Bridge E (exploratory): BCL-2 inhibition deepens the metabolic insult; side experiment with venetoclax as comparator.

5) Alternative explanations.
Off-target kinase effects (reported for this class, unverified prior knowledge); nonspecific pyrimidine-starvation killing without lineage conversion; dominant electron-flux effect rather than pyrimidine mechanism; salvage-pathway compensation blunting de novo depletion.

6) Discriminating preclinical strategy.
Step 0: Feasibility triage — verify DHODH IC50, steady-state plasma, protein binding from regulatory/pharmacokinetic sources; compute estimated unbound marrow exposure; apply go/no-go gate. Step 1: Confirm enzymatic IC50. Step 2: 15+ primary AML samples vs normal CD34+ progenitors; differentiation, apoptosis, CFU, LSC limiting-dilution; additionally profile salvage-pathway capacity (uridine phosphorylase activity, thymidine pools) to pre-stratify samples vulnerable to de novo pathway block versus those expected to be resistant. Step 3: On-target and mechanism-discriminative proof: uridine/pyrimidine rescue; DHODH knockdown; teriflunomide-resistant DHODH replacement; mechanistic arms separating pyrimidine from electron-flux contributions — succinate-driven respiration (bypasses DHODH/ubiquinone entry point), paired respirometry under matched pyrimidine rescue, catalytic-competent but CoQ-binding-defective DHODH construct. Predefined thresholds: uridine fully rescues all endpoints → electron-flux contribution refuted; succinate bypass fails to rescue → electron-flux contribution supported; concurrent dihydroorotate accumulation with partial uridine rescue → on-target but unresolved branching. Step 4: Cytarabine-resistant residual-state emulation (S2); venetoclax comparator arm (S3). Step 5: PDX PK/PD in vivo.

Falsifying observations (predefined thresholds):
(a) No differentiation-marker increase and no LSC-frequency reduction at achievable concentrations.
(b) Effects not rescued by uridine AND not phenocopied by DHODH knockdown → off-target rather than on-target.
(c) Normal CD34+ CFU-GM toxicity ≥ AML toxicity, eliminating the therapeutic window.
(d) No in vivo blast/LSC reduction despite confirmed on-target marrow pharmacodynamics.
(e) Step 0 gate failure: estimated unbound exposure < DHODH IC50 by >5-fold, deprioritizing the hypothesis.

Limitations.
Preclinical and model-based; exposure, approval, and potency details rest on unverified prior knowledge pending verification; AML heterogeneity limits generality; salvage-pathway compensation may blunt differentiation; the claim is an incremental exposure-feasibility test in a specific residual-cell context, not a new-mechanism discovery; direct prior teriflunomide/leflunomide-in-AML reports may predate or obviate this proposal.
