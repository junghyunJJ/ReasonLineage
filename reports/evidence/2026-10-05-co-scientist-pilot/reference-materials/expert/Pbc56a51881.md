# Pbc56a51881

Teriflunomide-induced differentiation in uridine-salvage-limited residual AML

Hypothesis and evidence. In oxidatively active, cytarabine-persistent AML with limited uridine salvage, teriflunomide will induce DHODH-dependent pyrimidine restriction and leukemic differentiation, reduce leukemia-propagating capacity, and spare normal progenitors. Cytarabine residual cells displayed oxidative-metabolism-associated properties without consistent stem-cell enrichment [S2]; DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation in experimental AML [S4]. Neither establishes teriflunomide sensitivity or salvage dependence. ROS-low, BCL-2-associated stem cells represent a distinct context [S1]. Venetoclax–azacitidine is an established comparator, not this repurposing intervention [S3]. De novo pyrimidine dependence, causal salvage limitation, differentiation instead of toxicity, and normal-cell selectivity are speculative bridges.

Drug and novelty gaps. Unverified prior knowledge: teriflunomide is approved for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis and inhibits DHODH; uridine salvage can bypass DHODH-dependent de novo pyrimidine synthesis. Verify these claims, potency, selectivity, protein binding, and human unbound exposure against regulatory records and primary pharmacology studies before assessing feasibility. The packet cannot supply that verification; translational feasibility remains unresolved. Prior teriflunomide/leflunomide AML literature also needs checking, so novelty is unestablished. The discriminating prediction is that manipulating salvage reversibly changes differentiation at comparable DHODH engagement—not that DHODH-induced differentiation is new.

Operational context. Before response testing, use isotope tracing to define a salvage-reserve index: maximal uridine-derived pyrimidine-production flux across a measured marrow-relevant uridine range divided by basal total pyrimidine-production flux. Prespecify low reserve as ≤0.25 and high reserve as ≥0.75. These are design cutoffs, not validated biological boundaries; analyze reserve continuously and retain intermediate samples. Compare paired pretreatment/post-cytarabine specimens after cytarabine washout, independent residual-disease models, and healthy-donor progenitors. Include stromal coculture, measured extracellular uridine, and respiratory phenotyping without inferring stemness.

Exposure and mechanism. Set a concentration–time ceiling from verified human unbound pharmacokinetics and measured culture binding; supra-ceiling effects cannot establish feasibility. Compare vehicle, an orthogonal DHODH inhibitor, a mitochondrial-stress control, and venetoclax–azacitidine [S3]. Measure DHODH-pathway metabolites, pyrimidine pools, and flux before apoptosis. Require concordance with DHODH suppression, uridine restoration of pools with reversal of differentiation, and a biochemically validated inhibitor-resistant DHODH rescue in tractable models. Morphology and myeloid markers must identify viable differentiated cells carrying the leukemia genotype, excluding normal-cell enrichment or selective blast loss.

Causal context test. Uridine rescue alone cannot establish baseline salvage as a response modifier. Identify the limiting salvage step experimentally; increase its activity in low-reserve AML and decrease it in high-reserve AML, confirming changed flux and comparable target engagement. Use factorial vehicle/drug arms: increased reserve must attenuate drug-induced differentiation, and reduced reserve must intensify it. Prespecify an inverse drug-by-continuous-reserve interaction using donor-blocked analyses. Test residual status for additional predictive value after accounting for reserve and respiration; absent incremental value rejects residual status as an enrichment biomarker, not necessarily salvage dependence.

Endpoints. Prespecified success requires at least a 15-percentage-point increase in viable genotype-confirmed differentiated cells, increased absolute mature leukemic-cell numbers, and at least a 50% reduction in leukemia-initiating frequency after washout using equal viable-cell inputs. Measure clonogenicity separately from bulk death. Normal progenitor colony output and long-term engraftment must retain at least 80% of controls at matched exposure. These are experimental decision thresholds, not clinical surrogates. Use donor-level replication and a power calculation for the interaction; confirm activity in residual-disease xenografts only with measured, exposure-compatible marrow concentrations.

Alternatives, falsification, and limitations. Off-target injury, nonspecific mitochondrial stress, and selection of pre-existing mature clones could mimic benefit. Track initially immature leukemia-genotyped cells over time and apply target-bypass controls. Adequately powered failure to meet differentiation and leukemia-initiation criteria despite confirmed DHODH inhibition and pyrimidine depletion in prespecified low-reserve residual AML would reject the drug–context prediction. Validated salvage augmentation that restores pyrimidines but leaves differentiation unchanged would reject the causal modifier; benefit surviving target-resistant rescue would reject the target mechanism. Normal-function retention below 80% rejects the proposed therapeutic window at that exposure. Stromal salvage, inadequate free marrow exposure, specimen heterogeneity, culture-induced metabolic changes, and transplantation artifacts limit extrapolation. Differentiation may leave propagating clones intact. A positive result supports further development, not clinical AML efficacy.
