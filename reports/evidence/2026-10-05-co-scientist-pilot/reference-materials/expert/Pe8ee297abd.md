CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation initiates nuclear-import failure in TARDBP-associated ALS

Hypothesis. In mature motor neurons carrying pathogenic TARDBP variants, impaired calcium clearance activates a calcium–calpain–p35/p25–CDK5 pathway. CDK5-dependent NUP98 phosphorylation reduces its pore residence, causing import failure before detectable cytoplasmic TDP-43 assemblies. Nuclear TDP-43 depletion then contributes to firing deficits and death. This initiating chain is proposed, not established. “Initiating” means upstream of transport failure, not the inherited variant; the scope is TARDBP-associated ALS.

Evidence and candidate-selection gap. Disease-linked TDP-43 can disrupt nucleoporin localization and transport [S1]. Cytoplasmic assemblies can impair import without conventional stress granules [S3], whereas stress-granule sequestration supplies an alternative [S4]. C9orf72-associated RanGAP abnormalities provide another genetic-context-specific route [S2]. None establishes nucleoporin phosphorylation; phosphorylated TDP-43 is not evidence of phosphorylated NUP98 [S3]. Unverified prior knowledge motivates candidate selection: TDP-43 participates in RNA regulation, calcium-dependent calpain cleavage of p35 to p25 can deregulate CDK5, and mitotic phosphorylation can promote NUP98 release from pores. The speculative bridges are altered RNA regulation impairing calcium handling, direct CDK5 modification of NUP98, and transfer of phosphorylation-sensitive pore release to postmitotic neurons. These precedents motivate selection but do not establish this pairing or literature-wide novelty.

Longitudinal strategy and decision rules. Use multiple donor-derived TARDBP motor-neuron lines, isogenic corrections, independent differentiations, and blinded analyses without imposed global stress. Measure calcium-clearance kinetics, calpain activity, p25:p35, CDK5 activity, NUP98 phosphosite occupancy, endogenous NUP98 pore residence by single-particle tracking, kinetic import, export, passive permeability, TDP-43 localization/assemblies, stress granules, firing, energy status, and survival. Discovery cultures identify reproducible early phosphosites; lock sites and endpoints before independent confirmation. Preregister ≥20% import-rate impairment and ≥50% closure of mutant–corrected import and firing deficits as operational impairment/rescue thresholds. Require upstream calcium/p25/CDK5 changes before phosphorylation, phosphorylation before residence loss, and both pore changes ≥24 hours before import failure and detectable assemblies.

Modification causality. In mature neurons, compare calpain inhibition and cleavage-resistant p35 replacement, with matched-expression p25 bypass, to test the proposed upstream trigger. Acute CDK5 suppression with active-versus-inactive kinase rescue tests kinase dependence; purified CDK5 must modify the same locked sites. Endogenous phosphoresistant NUP98 editing and inducible NUP98-targeted phosphatase recruitment provide orthogonal modification tests. Include wild-type edits and inactive-phosphatase controls, and verify unchanged neighboring nucleoporin phosphorylation, NUP98 abundance, maturation, baseline pore architecture, and basal transport. Phosphoresistance should preserve import and neuronal function while occluding additional CDK5-dependent protection.

Pore and transport mediation. Site-dependent rescue alone cannot establish displacement as the mediator. Inducibly tether NUP98 to a localization-validated NUP93 pore anchor, with anchor-only and tether-defective controls. Require restored residence while nominated phosphosites remain phosphorylated and baseline pore function remains normal. Rescue under these conditions would support displacement-mediated failure rather than other NUP98 functions. Joint rescue still does not establish transport mediation: after tether rescue, titrated inducible importin-β depletion should reproduce the original import deficit and eliminate neuronal protection without changing NUP98 residence. Interpret this only before generalized injury develops.

Alternative discrimination. Factorially compare NUP98 interventions with candidate assembly suppressors. Nominated from unverified prior knowledge, use G3BP1/2 CRISPRi versus ISRIB for conventional granules, and HSPB8 induction versus an engineered assembly-selective TDP-43 intrabody for nongranular assemblies. Their selectivity is not assumed. Interpret only independently validated conditions reducing targeted assemblies by ≥50%, preserving TDP-43 abundance and splicing function, and not independently changing CDK5 activity, basal import, calcium, energy status, or stress in pre-defect controls. Include non-targeting and inactive-binder controls; failed validation makes an arm inconclusive. The pore mechanism predicts early phosphorylation, residence loss, and import failure persist despite validated assembly suppression. Restoration of import despite persistent phosphorylation and residence loss instead favors sequestration. Blocking conventional granules alone cannot exclude the route in [S3].

Falsification and limitations. Verified prevention of the locked phosphorylation events that leaves early import failure and neuronal dysfunction unchanged falsifies their necessary initiating role, especially if assembly suppression restores transport. Phosphorylation appearing only after import failure contradicts the temporal claim. Successful retention with preserved phosphorylation but no import rescue rejects displacement as the mediator. Undetected small assemblies, unresolved RNA-to-calcium linkage, artificial tethering, phosphatase spillover, editing artifacts, and kinase pleiotropy remain limitations. Transport re-blocking accompanied by broad injury cannot resolve mediation. Findings would remain preclinical and TARDBP-context-specific, not a validated treatment.
