# Pba2557a9b5

Low uridine salvage sensitizes cytarabine-residual AML to teriflunomide-induced differentiation

Hypothesis. In cytarabine-residual AML with an oxidative phenotype and persistent differentiation blockade, insufficient uridine salvage relative to pyrimidine demand permits human-compatible unbound teriflunomide exposure to inhibit DHODH, deplete pyrimidines, and induce durable maturation of initially immature malignant cells. This reduces leukemia-propagating capacity while relatively sparing normal progenitors. Salvage adequacy—not oxidative metabolism alone—is the proposed sensitivity determinant.

Supported premises. DHODH inhibition promoted differentiation in experimental AML [S4]. Cytarabine-resistant cells showed oxidative-metabolism-associated features without consistent enrichment for immature, quiescent stem cells [S2]. Consequently, residual AML and the ROS-low, BCL-2-associated stem-cell population in [S1] require separate analyses. Venetoclax–azacitidine supplies an established metabolic comparator, not a new repurposing discovery [S3]. These findings establish neither teriflunomide efficacy nor salvage-based selectivity.

Drug and novelty boundaries. Unverified prior knowledge requiring confirmation: teriflunomide’s original approved indication is relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis; it inhibits DHODH; and uridine transport/uridine-cytidine kinase (UCK)-mediated salvage can bypass de novo pyrimidine synthesis. Human-compatible target inhibition, differentiation rather than arrest/death, and normal-cell compensation remain speculative bridges. Prior teriflunomide/leflunomide–AML and salvage-resistance literature requires checking before asserting novelty. The candidate distinguishing prediction is reciprocal sensitivity shifts with demand-adjusted salvage adequacy at equal DHODH engagement, not another DHODH–AML association.

Exposure and physiological constraints. Exposure feasibility is currently unquantified. Before efficacy experiments, establish a numerical licensed-dose human unbound concentration–time envelope, including steady-state peak, trough, AUC, and duration, from independently verified pharmacokinetics and binding data. Measure extracellular uridine in dilution-controlled human marrow specimens and matched plasma; predefine the observed marrow range, including its upper end. Reproduce that range and exposure envelope in protein-containing stromal coculture, measuring free drug directly. Do not increase exposure beyond the envelope to obtain efficacy. Confirm free plasma/marrow exposure in xenografts; supraclinical efficacy cannot establish feasibility.

Operational biomarker and causality. Baseline labeled-uridine incorporation alone is insufficient. Define R=C/D: C is isotope-measured absolute salvage-derived pyrimidine flux during brief reference-DHODH blockade before growth or viability changes; D is pretreatment pyrimidine demand from macromolecular incorporation plus irreversible loss, in matching per-cell-time units. Prespecify R<1 as insufficient and validate this provisional cutoff in independent samples, also analyzing R continuously. Study paired pretreatment/residual samples spanning R and respiration. Inducibly increase or decrease experimentally verified limiting uridine transport/UCK activity after residual selection. Require reciprocal sensitivity shifts at matched free drug and DHODH engagement, accounting for demand changes and drug-free controls. Repeat flux/demand measurements during treatment; expression differences alone do not demonstrate causality.

Discriminating endpoints. Include vehicle, a characterized DHODH inhibitor, DHODH suppression, and venetoclax–azacitidine, alongside normal donor CD34-positive progenitors. Establish pathway-metabolite and nucleotide changes before apoptosis or maturation. Test uridine rescue with isotope-confirmed pool restoration and unchanged drug exposure/target engagement; supraphysiological rescue is mechanistic, not evidence of marrow protection. Index-sort initially immature cells and track individual lineages longitudinally using imaging and heritable barcodes, confirming leukemia genotype in mature descendants. This excludes apparent differentiation caused solely by survival of pre-existing mature leukemic cells. Assess persistent maturation after washout through blinded morphology and myeloid immunophenotyping, separately from apoptosis, viable output, and colonies. Randomized xenografts should assess limiting-dilution secondary transplantation after verified washout using equal viable malignant-cell inputs at every dose, reporting total killing separately. Test normal colony formation and hematopoietic engraftment in parallel.

Alternatives, falsification, and limitations. Off-target toxicity, cytostasis, mature-cell selection, or microenvironmental uridine rescue could explain apparent benefit. Confirmed DHODH engagement and sustained pyrimidine depletion at permitted exposure in low-R residual AML, but neither lineage-proven differentiation nor reduced propagating frequency, would falsify the central prediction. Persistence after verified pyrimidine restoration would contradict the proposed mechanism; failure of bidirectional salvage perturbation to shift sensitivity would reject salvage adequacy as the determinant. Failure to engage DHODH within the human envelope rejects translational feasibility. Changing demand, marrow pharmacokinetics, disease heterogeneity, and normal-progenitor toxicity limit extrapolation. The packet supports a pathway rationale, not this drug’s therapeutic window, clinical benefit, or novelty.
